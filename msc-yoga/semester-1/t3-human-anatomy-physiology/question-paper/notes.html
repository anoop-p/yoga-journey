Know this · what the marks reward
  • 2 marks: one clear fact plus one supporting word or example. About 2 lines. No introduction.
  • 5 marks: 5 distinct points (1 mark each), or a neat labelled diagram with 6–8 labels plus a line of explanation. About 10–12 lines.
  • 10 marks: short introduction, 6–8 headed points, a yoga or practical link, one-line conclusion. About 1.5 pages.
  • Section C always asks for yoga relevance. Learn one reliable structure and reuse it: what happens in the body → what the practice does → why it helps → caution.
Memory aid · choosing questions

Pick Section A questions you can answer in one line (usually 11 of 13 are easy: Q1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13). Skip the diagram question unless you are sure of the labels. In B take the diagrams and “process” questions (Q16, Q18, Q19) because their points are fixed. In C take Q20, Q21 and Q22 only if you have a yoga example for each; two are enough.

Five short answers and one 5-mark answer. Revise from the Unit 1 pages.

Q1 · 2 marks

What is a cell?

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living things.
  • It is the smallest unit that can carry out the processes of life (taking in food, making energy, growing, reproducing).
Marking points: “basic unit” + “structure and function”. class
Q2 · 2 marks

Name any one organelle found inside a cell.

Mitochondria (any one of: nucleus, ribosome, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosome).
  • Organelles are small specialised structures in the cytoplasm, each with its own job.
Tip: choose the one you can describe in a line; the examiner may ask for nothing more but a function line earns safety marks. general
Q5 · 2 marks

Name any two types of tissues.

Epithelial tissue and muscle tissue. (The four basic types: epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous.)
Tip: name two and add “the four types are…” only if you are sure of all four. class
Q7 · 2 marks

What is the role of mitochondria in a cell?

Mitochondria make energy for the cell by aerobic respiration, in the form of ATP. They are called the “powerhouse of the cell”.
  • Cells with a high energy need (muscle, liver) contain many mitochondria.
Marking points: ATP/energy + powerhouse. class
Q10 · 2 marks

Write any two functions of epithelial cells.

  1. Protection: covers the body surface and lines cavities, shielding underlying tissue from injury, germs and drying (skin).
  2. Absorption and secretion: lining of the intestine absorbs nutrients; glands secrete mucus, enzymes and hormones.
Others you may use: filtration (kidney), sensation, excretion. class general
Q14 · 5 marks

What is balance in the body? Explain.

Reading taken. The Unit 1 syllabus ends with “tissue, organs, systems, homeostasis”, so “balance in the body” is answered as homeostasis. If your teacher meant postural balance, the last point covers it. added: confirm with teacher

  1. Meaning: homeostasis is the body's ability to keep its internal environment steady (temperature, water, pH, blood sugar, oxygen) although the outside world keeps changing.
  2. How it works: a receptor senses a change, a control centre (brain) compares it with the normal value, and an effector (muscle or gland) corrects it. This is a feedback loop, mostly negative feedback.
  3. Example: body temperature about 37 °C. Too hot: sweating and skin blood vessels widen. Too cold: shivering and vessels narrow.
  4. Who maintains it: the nervous and endocrine systems coordinate; kidneys, lungs, skin and liver carry out the corrections.
  5. Importance and yoga link: cells work only in a narrow range, so failure of balance causes disease. Regular yoga practice, with relaxation and slow breathing, may help the body return to a calm, balanced state. added
Marking points: definition (1), feedback loop (1), example (1), systems (1), importance (1).

Which is the longest bone in the human body?

The femur (thigh bone).

Q4 · 2 marks

How many main types of muscles are there?

Three: skeletal (voluntary, striated), smooth (involuntary, non-striated) and cardiac (involuntary, striated, only in the heart). class
Q8 · 2 marks

Draw a simple diagram of a muscle cell.

  • A skeletal muscle cell (fibre) is long, cylindrical and multinucleate, packed with myofibrils whose repeating sarcomeres give the striped (striated) look. Mitochondria supply the ATP. class
Marking points: neat shape (½), any three labels (1½). Draw it with a pencil and label with ruled lines.
Q11 · 2 marks

What are bio-mechanical movements?

Biomechanical movements are the movements of the body studied by applying the methods of mechanics (forces, levers, planes and axes) to the structure and function of the human body.
  • Examples: flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation, circumduction. They occur in planes (sagittal, frontal, transverse) and about axes.
Source: kinesiology/biomechanics handout (SAI): kinesiology is the study of human movement; mechanics applied to the body is biomechanics. class
Q13 · 2 marks

Name the shortest bone in the human body.

The stapes (stirrup), a tiny bone in the middle ear.
Tip: the other two ear bones are the malleus and incus. general
Q15 · 5 marks

What causes muscle fatigue?

Muscle fatigue is the fall in a muscle's ability to keep up force after prolonged or repeated activity. class Its causes:

  1. Energy (ATP) shortage: stores of ATP, creatine phosphate and glycogen run low in long or intense effort.
  2. Lactic acid and hydrogen ion build-up: during hard exercise with too little oxygen, the muscle makes lactate, the acidity rises and slows the contractile process. (Lactate is not the only cause.) general
  3. Oxygen shortage / poor blood supply: the muscle cannot get oxygen and remove wastes fast enough.
  4. Electrolyte and fluid imbalance: loss of sodium, potassium or magnesium through sweating weakens muscle and nerve function; magnesium deficiency can cause weakness and spasm. class
  5. Duration, intensity, fibre type, training and recovery also decide how quickly fatigue sets in. A well-trained muscle fatigues later. class
Yoga link (one line): holding a pose for long makes the muscles shake; rest in Shavasana and slow breathing aid recovery. added
Q17 · 5 marks

Name two types of joints in the body.

Joints are classified by the tissue at the articulation into three types. Two of them:

  1. Synovial joint (freely movable, diarthrosis). The “true joint”: bone ends covered with hyaline cartilage, a joint capsule enclosing a cavity with synovial fluid, held by ligaments. Wide range of movement. Examples: ball and socket (shoulder, hip), hinge (elbow, knee), pivot (atlas-axis), saddle (thumb base), plane (between small bones), condyloid (wrist).
  2. Fibrous joint (immovable, synarthrosis). Bones joined by tough fibrous tissue; little or no movement. Examples: skull sutures, tibiofibular joint (syndesmosis), tooth in its socket (gomphosis).
A third type: cartilaginous joint (slightly movable): bones joined by cartilage, for example the joints between vertebrae and the pubic symphysis.
Marking points: two names (2), features (2), examples (1). class
Q22 · 10 marks

Explain how asanas and pranayama are good for muscles and bones.

Introduction. Muscles move and protect the bones, and bones give muscles a lever to pull. Yoga loads both gently and repeatedly, and pranayama supports them with oxygen and relaxation.

AMuscles

  1. Strength and endurance: holding poses such as Tadasana, Trikonasana, Virabhadrasana and Bhujangasana keeps muscles working against body weight.
  2. Flexibility: stretching in Paschimottanasana (hamstrings) and Ardha Matsyendrasana (spinal muscles) lengthens muscles within safe limits (extensibility and elasticity). class
  3. Posture: postural muscles of back and abdomen are trained, which can reduce slouching and back strain.
  4. Relaxation and less fatigue: Shavasana and slow rhythmic breathing allow tense muscles to let go and recover.

BBones and joints

  1. Bone strength: weight-bearing poses (Tadasana, Virabhadrasana, Vrikshasana) put gentle load on the bones, and bone responds to load by staying strong. Evidence in yoga for bone density is modest; say “may help”. general
  2. Joint mobility: bending and rotating through the full range keeps synovial fluid circulating and cartilage nourished.
  3. Spine health: forward, backward and twisting poses move all the vertebral joints and may keep intervertebral discs supple.

CRole of pranayama

  • Slow deep breathing (Anuloma-viloma, Bhastrika with caution) improves oxygen supply to working muscles and helps remove carbon dioxide. added
  • It calms the nervous system, reducing muscle tension and helping muscles relax. added
  • Deep abdominal and chest breathing exercises the diaphragm and intercostal muscles.

Conclusion. Practised regularly, with proper alignment and within one's limit, asanas and pranayama may help keep muscles strong and flexible, bones and joints healthy, and the body relaxed. Avoid forcing a pose in pain or with a joint or bone problem.

Q18 · 5 marks

What is the digestive process?

Digestion is the breakdown of food into small soluble molecules the blood can carry to the cells. It has five steps.

  1. Ingestion: food enters the mouth; teeth chew it and saliva moistens it (mechanical digestion).
  2. Digestion: saliva starts starch breakdown; the stomach churns food with hydrochloric acid and pepsin to protein-digesting juice; in the small intestine pancreatic juice, bile and intestinal juice finish carbohydrates, proteins and fats.
  3. Absorption: mainly in the small intestine, where villi pass nutrients into blood and lymph; the large intestine absorbs water.
  4. Assimilation: the cells use the nutrients for energy, growth and repair.
  5. Elimination: undigested residue leaves as faeces through the rectum and anus.
Route: mouth → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum → anus. Helpers: salivary glands, liver, gallbladder, pancreas.
Marking points: five steps, 1 mark each. A simple flow line earns a neat look. class
Q21 · 10 marks

How do asanas help in digestion?

Introduction. Digestion depends on muscular movement of the gut (peristalsis), the flow of blood and juices, and a calm nervous system. Certain asanas may support all three.

AHow asanas may help

  1. Massage of abdominal organs: forward bends, twists and backbends alternately compress and release the abdomen, which may stimulate the stomach, intestines, liver and pancreas.
  2. Better blood flow to the digestive organs, bringing oxygen and nutrients.
  3. Stronger abdominal muscles that support the gut and help the bowel move its contents.
  4. Relief of gas and constipation: twists and knee-to-chest poses help move trapped gas along the colon.
  5. Stress reduction: relaxation after practice shifts the body toward “rest and digest” (parasympathetic action), which favours gut activity.

BAsana examples

AsanaDigestive benefit (may help)
PavanamuktasanaExpels gas; eases bloating and constipation
Ardha MatsyendrasanaTwists and massages the abdomen; stimulates liver and pancreas
PaschimottanasanaCompresses abdomen; may help constipation
Bhujangasana, DhanurasanaStretch the front of the abdomen; stimulate digestive organs
VajrasanaCan be done after meals; supports calm digestion
Uttanapadasana, NaukasanaStrengthen abdominal muscles

CCautions

  • Practise on an empty stomach (after 3–4 hours); avoid strong forward bends right after food.
  • Avoid strong abdominal poses in ulcers, hernia, pregnancy, menstruation (as advised) and after abdominal surgery. added

Conclusion. Asanas can support digestion by moving, massaging and relaxing the gut, but they work best with regular meals, enough water and fibre.

Q16 · 5 marks

Draw and label the parts of the respiratory system.

  1. Nasal cavity: warms, filters and moistens air.
  2. Pharynx (throat): common passage for air and food. Larynx (voice box): holds the vocal cords.
  3. Trachea (windpipe): C-shaped cartilage rings keep it open; divides into two bronchi.

Q19 · 5 marks

How do we breathe?

Breathing is the movement of air in and out of the lungs. It is done by the diaphragm and the intercostal muscles, using pressure changes (air flows from higher to lower pressure).

  1. Inspiration (breathing in): the diaphragm contracts and flattens; the external intercostals lift the ribs up and out. The chest volume rises, lung pressure falls below the outside pressure, and air rushes in.
  2. Expiration (breathing out): the diaphragm and intercostals relax; the diaphragm rises, the ribs come down, the volume falls, pressure rises and air flows out. Quiet expiration is passive; forced expiration uses the abdominal and internal intercostal muscles.
  3. Path of air: nose → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.
  4. Gas exchange: in the alveoli oxygen passes into the blood and carbon dioxide passes out.
  5. Control: the respiratory centre in the brainstem sets the rate (about 12–16 breaths per minute at rest).
Marking points: inspiration (2), expiration (2), muscles/pressure/path (1). class R&W
Q20 · 10 marks

How does breathing affect the body in yoga practice?

Introduction. Breath is the one automatic function we can also control. In yoga, breath is joined to movement and used as a tool to steady the body and mind.

AEffects on the body

  1. More oxygen, better exchange: slow deep breathing uses more of the lung, including the bases, so more oxygen reaches the blood and carbon dioxide is cleared.
  2. Stronger respiratory muscles: the diaphragm and intercostals are exercised; vital capacity and chest expansion may improve.
  3. Calm nervous system: slow, long exhalation may increase parasympathetic activity, lowering heart rate and stress.
  4. Relaxed muscles: breathing out in a stretch helps the muscle release and move deeper into the pose.
  5. Focus and steadiness: following the breath holds attention and keeps movement smooth and rhythmic.
  6. Circulation and digestion: pressure changes in chest and abdomen may help venous return and massage the abdominal organs.

BIn practice

  • Asanas: breathe in while lengthening or opening the chest (backbends), breathe out while folding or twisting.
  • Pranayama: Anuloma-viloma (alternate nostril), Ujjayi, Bhramari, Kapalabhati and Bhastrika (the last two vigorous and with caution), each with puraka (inhale), kumbhaka (retention) and rechaka (exhale).

Cautions and conclusion. Breath retention and fast breathing should be learned under a teacher and avoided in high blood pressure, heart disease, or lung problems without advice. Practised properly, regulated breathing may support the lungs, calm the mind and improve the body's response to practice.

Q11 · 10 marks

Explain the structure and functions of the respiratory system, and how pranayama benefits it.

Introduction. The respiratory system brings oxygen into the body and removes carbon dioxide. Cells need oxygen to release energy from food.

AStructure (the air route)

  1. Nose and nasal cavity: warm, filter and moisten the air.
  2. Pharynx: shared passage for air and food.
  3. Larynx: voice box; the epiglottis covers it during swallowing.
  4. Trachea: windpipe held open by C-shaped cartilage rings.
  5. Bronchi and bronchioles: branching tubes into each lung.
  6. Alveoli: millions of thin-walled air sacs surrounded by capillaries, the site of gas exchange.
  7. Lungs and pleura: right lung three lobes, left two; each lung covered by pleura with pleural fluid between the layers.
  8. Diaphragm and intercostal muscles: the muscles of breathing.

BFunctions

  • Breathing (ventilation): moves air in and out.
  • Gas exchange: oxygen into blood, carbon dioxide out.
  • Protection: hairs, mucus and cilia trap dust and germs.
  • Voice production (larynx) and smell (nose).
  • Helps regulate blood pH by removing carbon dioxide. R&W

CHow pranayama benefits the system

  • Trains the diaphragm and intercostal muscles; improves chest expansion and lung capacity.
  • Slow deep breaths ventilate the lower lungs, improving oxygen uptake and clearing stale air.
  • Cleaning practices such as Kapalabhati may clear the nasal passages; alternate nostril breathing may calm breathing rate.
  • Builds awareness and control of breathing and reduces stress that tightens breathing.

Conclusion. A healthy respiratory system gives every cell oxygen; regular, correct pranayama may keep it efficient. Learn the vigorous practices from a teacher.

Marking points: intro (1), structure (4), functions (2), pranayama (2), conclusion (1). Pranayama benefits: write “may”. added
