Q1 · 2 marksOverview

Define digestion.

Tap to reveal answer
Digestion is the breakdown of large, insoluble food molecules into small, soluble ones that can be absorbed into the blood.

It is both mechanical (chewing, churning) and chemical (enzymes). class

Q2 · 2 marksOverview

Name the five activities of the digestive system.

Tap to reveal answer
Ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation and elimination.

Absorption puts nutrients into the blood; assimilation is the cells using them. class

Q3 · 2 marksSalivary glands

Name the three pairs of salivary glands.

Tap to reveal answer
Parotid, submandibular and sublingual glands.

Parotid: in front of the ear. Submandibular: below the jaw. Sublingual: under the tongue. class

Q4 · 2 marksPhysiology

Which enzyme is present in saliva and what does it do?

Tap to reveal answer
Salivary amylase starts the digestion of starch, breaking it into smaller sugars (maltose).

Note: your class notes say “breaks carbs to starch”; that is reversed. Write starch → sugars. general

Q5 · 2 marksStomach

Name the four parts of the stomach.

Tap to reveal answer
Cardia, fundus, body and pyloric region.

Cardia is at the oesophageal opening; the pyloric region leads to the duodenum through the pyloric sphincter. class

Q6 · 2 marksStomach

What is chyme?

Tap to reveal answer
Chyme is the semi-fluid, acidic paste formed when the swallowed food (bolus) is churned and mixed with gastric juice in the stomach.

It passes in small amounts into the duodenum. class

Q7 · 2 marksGastric juice

What is the role of hydrochloric acid in the stomach?

Tap to reveal answer
HCl activates pepsinogen into pepsin, gives the acid medium pepsin needs, and kills most microbes in food.

It is made by the parietal cells of the gastric glands. class

Q8 · 2 marksGastric juice

What is intrinsic factor? Why is it important?

Tap to reveal answer
Intrinsic factor is a protein made by the parietal cells that binds vitamin B12 so it can be absorbed in the ileum.

Without it, B12 deficiency (pernicious anaemia) can follow. class R&W

Q9 · 2 marksSmall intestine

Name the three parts of the small intestine.

Tap to reveal answer
Duodenum, jejunum and ileum.

The duodenum (about 25 cm) is where chyme, pancreatic juice and bile enter. class

Q10 · 2 marksSmall intestine

What are villi? State their function.

Tap to reveal answer
Villi are tiny finger-like projections of the small intestine lining. They greatly increase the surface area for absorption.

Each villus has a capillary network and a lacteal (lymph vessel) for carrying away absorbed nutrients and fat. class

Q11 · 2 marksLiver

Write any two functions of the liver.

Tap to reveal answer
  1. Makes bile, which emulsifies fats.
  2. Processes absorbed nutrients and detoxifies drugs and alcohol (also stores glycogen).

Q17 · 5 marksSalivary glands

Name the salivary glands and write the functions of saliva.

Tap to reveal answer

Glands: three pairs: parotid, submandibular and sublingual (plus many small glands in the mouth lining). Release is controlled by the autonomic nervous system and by reflex: food in the mouth (unconditioned) or sight, smell or thought of food (conditioned).

Functions of saliva:

  1. Moistens food and forms the bolus for swallowing.
  2. Salivary amylase starts starch digestion.
  3. Dissolves food so it can be tasted.
  4. Cleans the mouth and teeth; helps defend against germs.
  5. Lubricates the mouth and helps speech.

Note: the class line “salivary glands help to control the ANS” is reversed: the ANS controls the glands. pH about 6–7. class R&W

Q18 · 5 marksGastric juice

Describe the composition and functions of gastric juice.

Tap to reveal answer

Gastric juice is secreted by the gastric glands of the stomach. Its parts and jobs:

  1. Hydrochloric acid (parietal cells): activates pepsinogen, makes the stomach acidic and kills microbes.
  2. Pepsinogen (chief cells): inactive; becomes pepsin, which starts protein digestion.
  3. Mucus (mucous cells): forms a protective barrier on the stomach wall (with bicarbonate).
  4. Intrinsic factor (parietal cells): helps absorb vitamin B12.
  5. Water and salts make the juice fluid. The hormone gastrin (from G cells, carried in the blood) stimulates its release.

Note: write “strongly acidic, about pH 1–3”. Your class notes say 0–3 and the PDF says 1–1.5; Ross & Wilson says pepsin works at 1.5–3.5. Gastrin is a hormone, not part of the juice itself. class R&W

Q19 · 5 marksSmall intestine

Describe the structure of the small intestine and explain how it is adapted for absorption.

Tap to reveal answer

Structure: a long coiled tube (the longest part of the canal) of three parts: duodenum (about 25 cm, receives bile and pancreatic juice), jejunum and ileum, joined to the large intestine.

Adaptations for absorption:

  1. Great length and folds of the lining.
  2. Villi: finger-like projections that multiply the surface.
  3. Microvilli (brush border) on each lining cell: a further increase in surface area.
  4. Capillary network in each villus: carries glucose and amino acids to the blood.
  5. Lacteal in each villus: carries absorbed fat (as chylomicrons) in the lymph.

Marks: structure (2), adaptations (3). Draw a labelled villus if time allows. class

Q20 · 5 marksLiver and bile

Write the functions of the liver and the role of bile.

Tap to reveal answer

Functions of the liver:

  1. Makes bile.
  2. Processes absorbed nutrients (stores glycogen, changes sugars and amino acids).
  3. Detoxifies drugs, alcohol and wastes.
  4. Makes plasma proteins and stores some vitamins and iron.

Role of bile: bile is stored and concentrated in the gallbladder and reaches the duodenum through the common bile duct. It has bile salts and pigments but no enzymes. Bile salts emulsify fat into tiny droplets so lipase can act, and help absorb fats and fat-soluble vitamins.

Path: liver → hepatic ducts → gallbladder → common bile duct → duodenum. The functional unit of the liver is the hepatic lobule. class R&W

Q21 · 5 marksPancreas

Explain the role of the pancreas in digestion.

Tap to reveal answer
  1. The pancreas lies behind the stomach and pours pancreatic juice into the duodenum through the pancreatic duct (joined with the bile duct).
  2. The juice is alkaline (about pH 8); with bile it neutralises the acid chyme.
  3. Pancreatic amylase digests starch.
  4. Trypsin, chymotrypsin and carboxypeptidase digest proteins to peptides. Trypsinogen is activated by enterokinase from the intestinal wall.
  5. Lipase digests fats; nucleases digest nucleic acids.

Add: the pancreas is also an endocrine gland (insulin and glucagon). class general

Q22 · 5 marksAbsorption

Explain how the end products of digestion are absorbed.

Tap to reveal answer
  1. Final products: carbohydrates → glucose; proteins → amino acids; fats → fatty acids and monoglycerides.
  2. Where: mostly in the small intestine, through the villi. The stomach absorbs only a little (water, alcohol).
  3. Glucose and amino acids: carried by sodium-dependent transport into the lining cells, then into the capillaries and blood.
  4. Fats: form small micelles with bile salts, enter the cells, are repackaged as chylomicrons and enter the lacteal (lymph).
  5. Water and salts: absorbed along the small intestine; the large intestine absorbs more water.

class

Q23 · 5 marksLarge intestine

Describe the parts and functions of the large intestine. How is defaecation brought about?

Tap to reveal answer

Parts: caecum (with the appendix), colon (ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid), rectum and anus.

Functions:

  1. Absorbs water and electrolytes.
  2. Forms and stores faeces.
  3. Houses gut bacteria (microbiota).

It has no villi and no digestive enzymes.

Defaecation: faeces entering the rectum stretch its wall; a reflex contracts the rectum and relaxes the internal anal sphincter, and the voluntary external sphincter lets faeces pass.

class R&W (the external sphincter detail is general)

Q24 · 10 marksGross anatomy + process

Describe the structure of the digestive system and explain the process of digestion.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. The digestive system turns food into small molecules the blood can carry. It has the alimentary canal (a tube of about 9 m from mouth to anus) and accessory organs.

AStructure

  1. Mouth: teeth (32 permanent, 20 milk), tongue and salivary glands.
  2. Pharynx and oesophagus (about 25 cm): carry food to the stomach by peristalsis; the epiglottis protects the airway.
  3. Stomach: cardia, fundus, body, pylorus; gastric glands secrete juice.
  4. Small intestine: duodenum, jejunum, ileum, with villi.
  5. Large intestine: caecum, colon, rectum and anus.
  6. Accessory organs: salivary glands, liver, gallbladder, pancreas.
  7. Wall: mucosa, submucosa, muscularis and serosa.

BProcess

  1. Ingestion: chewing and saliva begin digestion.
  2. Digestion: stomach (acid, pepsin) then small intestine (pancreatic juice, bile, intestinal enzymes).
  3. Absorption: in the small intestine through villi; water in the large intestine.
  4. Assimilation: cells use the nutrients.
  5. Elimination: faeces leave through the anus.

Yoga link and conclusion. Practices that relax the nervous system and gently move the abdomen may support digestion (see Q27). A balanced diet and regular meals are equally important.

Marks: intro (1), structure (4), process (4), conclusion (1). class

Q25 · 10 marksPhysiology

Explain digestion in the stomach and the small intestine. Describe gastric juice and how acid is produced.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Chemical digestion starts in the mouth (starch) and is mainly carried out in the stomach (protein) and small intestine (all food groups).

AIn the stomach

  1. Food becomes chyme by churning with gastric juice.
  2. Gastric juice has HCl, pepsinogen, mucus and intrinsic factor; gastrin (a hormone from G cells) stimulates secretion.
  3. Acid production: parietal cells pump hydrogen ions into the stomach lumen (a proton pump that uses ATP) and chloride follows, forming HCl. Inside the cell, carbon dioxide and water form carbonic acid (enzyme: carbonic anhydrase), which supplies the hydrogen ions. R&W
  4. HCl activates pepsinogen to pepsin, which breaks proteins into peptides at low pH.
  5. The stomach does not digest itself because of the mucus-bicarbonate barrier, inactive pepsinogen and rapid cell renewal.

BIn the small intestine

  1. Chyme enters the duodenum in small amounts; pancreatic juice (alkaline) and bile neutralise the acid.
  2. Carbohydrates: pancreatic amylase, then maltase, lactase and sucrase (lactase splits lactose into glucose and galactose).
  3. Proteins: trypsin, chymotrypsin and carboxypeptidase, then peptidases to amino acids.
  4. Fats: bile salts emulsify; lipase splits them to fatty acids and monoglycerides.
  5. The end products are absorbed through the villi.

Conclusion. The stomach prepares and starts protein digestion; the small intestine completes digestion and absorbs.

Marks: intro (1), stomach (4), intestine (4), conclusion (1). Quote the pH as “about 1–3”. class R&W

Q26 · 10 marksGlands

Describe the structure and role of the salivary glands, liver and pancreas in digestion.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. These accessory organs make the juices that digest food; food never passes through them.

ASalivary glands

  • Three pairs: parotid, submandibular, sublingual; saliva pH about 6–7.
  • Saliva moistens food, forms the bolus, and salivary amylase starts starch digestion. Release is reflex-controlled (conditioned and unconditioned).

BLiver and gallbladder

  • The largest gland, made of hepatic lobules; the gallbladder stores and concentrates bile.
  • Bile (salts and pigments, no enzymes) emulsifies fat and aids absorption of fat-soluble vitamins; the liver also processes nutrients and detoxifies.
  • Bile path: liver → hepatic ducts → gallbladder → common bile duct → duodenum.

CPancreas

  • Lies behind the stomach; pancreatic juice enters the duodenum by the pancreatic duct.
  • Alkaline juice (about pH 8) with amylase, trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase, lipase and nucleases; trypsinogen is activated by enterokinase.
  • Also an endocrine gland (insulin, glucagon).

Conclusion. Together the three provide the enzymes and bile that digest carbohydrate, protein and fat.

Marks: intro (1), saliva (2), liver (3), pancreas (3), conclusion (1). class R&W

Q27 · 10 marksYoga relevance

Explain how yoga practices (asanas and pranayama) help digestion and digestive disorders.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Digestion depends on gut movement, blood flow and a calm nervous system. Yoga may support all three. (Write “may help”: traditional rationale is not the same as proven physiology.)

AHow yoga may help

  1. Rest and digest: relaxation and slow breathing favour the parasympathetic nervous system. class
  2. Abdominal massage: twists and forward bends compress and release the abdomen and may stimulate the gut.
  3. Stronger abdominal muscles support the gut and bowel movement.
