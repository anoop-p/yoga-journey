U1 Q1 · 2 marksDefinition

What is the meaning of the word yoga? Give its three roots.

Tap to reveal answer
Yoga comes from yuj.

Yuj samyoge (to unite), yuj samadhau (to concentrate), yuj samyamane (to control).

The Katha Upanishad (2.3.10-11).

It defines yoga as steady holding of the senses.

Tap to reveal answer

Samhita (mantras), Brahmana (application), Aranyaka (practice, upasana), Upanishad (knowledge). The first three are karma kanda; the Upanishads are jnana kanda.

U1 Q14 · 5 marksCharvaka

Explain the Charvaka philosophy.

Tap to reveal answer

Materialist. Only perception is valid; four elements only; consciousness arises from them; no soul, karma or rebirth; pleasure is the aim. Critics: rejecting inference refutes itself.

U1 Q17 · 10 marksEpics

Explain yoga in the Ramayana (Aranya Kanda).

Tap to reveal answer

Seven kandas; Aranya Kanda has 75 sargas set in the forest. Episodes: Viradha (liberation), Sharabhanga (willed departure, utkranti), sages, Agastya (tapas, mitahara, wisdom arms action), Jatayu. The Rama Gita (Adhyatma Ramayana) teaches Atman and Maya, three gunas, karma, jnana, bhakti.

U2 Q9 · 5 marksBhakti

Explain the types of bhakta.

Tap to reveal answer

By the Gita 7.16: arta (distressed), jijnasu (seeker), artharthi (seeker of wealth), jnani (wise). By guna: sattvika, rajasika, tamasika. Gauni (secondary) and mukhya (primary, para) bhakti, in Narada.

class

U2 Q10 · 5 marksBhakti

Explain bhakti according to the Narada Bhakti Sutra.

Tap to reveal answer

84 sutras. Bhakti is supreme love for the divine and is nectar. It is greater than karma and jnana because of its result. Gauni and mukhya bhakti. Eleven forms of attachment: guna, rupa, puja, smarana, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, kanta, atma-nivedana, tanmaya, viraha.

class

U2 Q11 · 5 marksBhakti

Tap to reveal answer

Shravana, kirtana, smarana, padasevana, archana, vandana, dasya, sakhya, atma-nivedana. Example: Prahlada. Bhavas: shanta, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, madhurya.

class

U2 Q12 · 5 marksKarma

Explain sakama and nishkama karma.

Tap to reveal answer

Sakama: action for a specific result (money, status). Nishkama: action without attachment to the fruit. Gita 2.47: right to action, not to fruit.

class

U2 Q13 · 5 marksMantra

Explain Mantra yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Man (mind) + tra (protection). Mantra, japa, concentration, purification, one-pointed mind, meditation. Om is pranava (YS 1.27, 1.28). Types: bija, saguna, nirguna.

class

U2 Q14 · 5 marksLaya

Explain Laya yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Laya is dissolution of the mind into its source. Methods: nada, mantra, breath awareness, concentration, meditation, kundalini practices. Nada: external, inner, subtle.

class

U2 Q15 · 5 marksKundalini

Explain the structure of Kundalini yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Nadis (ida, pingala, sushumna), seven chakras, three granthis, static shakti (kundalini) and dynamic shakti (prana). Practice turns static into dynamic and draws it to sahasrara.

class

U2 Q17 · 10 marksKarma yoga

Explain Karma yoga in detail.

Tap to reveal answer

Intro: duty without attachment to the fruit. Background: Gita, Krishna to Arjuna, ch 3. Verses 2.47, 2.48, 2.50, 3.9. Structure: karma, svadharma, nishkama karma, ishvara-pranidhana, samatva, chitta-shuddhi, jnana, moksha. Types: sanchita, prarabdha, agami. Epistemology: pratyaksha, anumana, shabda. Metaphysics: atma, prakriti, ishvara, karma, dharma, moksha.

class

U2 Q18 · 10 marksJnana yoga

Explain Jnana yoga in detail.

Tap to reveal answer

Intro: knowledge of the self; who am I. Background: Upanishads and Vedanta. Sadhana chatushtaya (viveka, vairagya, shat-sampatti, mumukshutva). Method: shravana, manana, nididhyasana; neti neti. Epistemology: pratyaksha, anumana, shabda. Metaphysics: atman, Brahman, avidya, moksha. Mahavakyas.

class

U2 Q19 · 10 marksBhakti yoga

Explain Bhakti yoga in detail.

Tap to reveal answer

Intro: love and surrender. Texts: Gita 12, Bhagavata, Narada, Shandilya. Structure: shraddha, bhakti, smarana, seva, sharanagati, moksha. Navavidha bhakti, saguna and nirguna, five bhavas. Epistemology: shraddha, shabda, anubhava. Metaphysics: jiva, Ishvara, jagat, maya, moksha. Advaita, Vishishtadvaita, Dvaita views.

U3 Q8 · 5 marksPatanjali

Explain the contribution of Patanjali to yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Compiled and systematised yoga; not its founder. Yoga Sutra: definition 1.2, eight limbs, kriya yoga, kleshas, nine obstacles, kaivalya. Removed mysticism; mind-body approach.

class

U3 Q9 · 5 marksGorakshanath

Explain the contribution of Gorakshanath to Hatha yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Nath guru (11th-12th c.). Taught asana, pranayama, mudra, bandha, kundalini, chakras, nadis. Siddha body and kaya siddhi. Texts: Goraksha Samhita, Shataka, Siddha Siddhanta Paddhati. Guru-centred, experience over theory.

class

U3 Q11 · 5 marksAurobindo

Tap to reveal answer

Psychic (looking within to the soul), spiritual (light and peace), supramental (realising the supermind).

class

U3 Q12 · 10 marksAurobindo

Explain Sri Aurobindo's integral yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Born 1872, samadhi 1950. Synthesis of jnana, bhakti and karma; no emphasis on hatha. Life is the platform. Involution and evolution through ten planes: inconscient matter, subconscient, vital physical, mind, higher mind, illumined, intuitive, overmind, supermind, sat-chit-ananda. Triple transformation. Books: Savitri, The Life Divine, Synthesis of Yoga, The Human Cycle. Ashram 1926, Auroville 1968.

class

U3 Q13 · 10 marksRamana

Explain the yogic teachings of Ramana Maharshi.

Tap to reveal answer

Born 1879, samadhi 1950. Self-enquiry removes dehatma buddhi. Reality is loss of ego. Heart is the Supreme. Internal guru pulls, external guru pushes. Surrender. Moksha is already our nature. Books: Who Am I, Be As You Are.
