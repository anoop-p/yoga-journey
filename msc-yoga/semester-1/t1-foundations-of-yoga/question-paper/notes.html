01 · Section A

Section A: Two-mark questions (answer any 10)

Questions 1 to 13. A good answer is one or two lines.

Q1 · 2 marksVedas

What is the meaning of Veda?

Tap to reveal answer
Veda comes from the root vid, to know. It means knowledge, especially sacred knowledge.

The four Vedas are Rig, Yajur, Sama and Atharva. They are the oldest scriptures of India and are called shruti (what was heard).

general

Q2 · 2 marksVedanga

Write the names of the Vedangas.

Tap to reveal answer
The six Vedangas are Shiksha, Kalpa, Vyakarana, Nirukta, Chhandas and Jyotisha.

Shiksha is phonetics, Kalpa ritual, Vyakarana grammar, Nirukta word origin, Chhandas metre and Jyotisha astronomy. They are limbs that help in understanding the Vedas.

general

Q3 · 2 marksUpanishad

What is the meaning of Upanishad?

Tap to reveal answer
Upa (near) + ni (down) + shad (to sit): sitting down near the teacher to receive the highest knowledge.

It also carries the sense of destroying ignorance. The Upanishads end the Vedas, so they are called Vedanta.

general

Q4 · 2 marksJainism

What is Anekantavada?

Tap to reveal answer
The Jain teaching that reality has many sides, so no single view is the whole truth.

It goes with syadvada (every statement is true from one standpoint) and nayavada (partial viewpoints). The usual image is blind men describing an elephant.

general

Q5 · 2 marksHatha yoga

What is the meaning of Ha and Tha?

Tap to reveal answer
Ha is the sun (pingala nadi, pranic energy) and Tha is the moon (ida nadi, mental energy). Hatha yoga is their balance.

Another reading takes hatha as force or effort. class

general

Q6 · 2 marksYoga Sutra

What is yoga according to the Yoga Sutra?

Tap to reveal answer
Yogash chitta-vritti-nirodhah (1.2): yoga is the stilling of the movements of the mind.

When the mind is still, the seer rests in his own nature (1.3).

general

Q7 · 2 marksModern yoga

Who developed Transcendental Meditation?

Tap to reveal answer
Maharishi Mahesh Yogi.

He taught it from the late 1950s. It is a mantra-based technique practised for about 20 minutes twice a day.

general

Q8 · 2 marksModern yoga

Who is the father of modern yoga?

Tap to reveal answer
T. Krishnamacharya (1888–1989) is usually called the father of modern yoga.

His students include B.K.S. Iyengar, K. Pattabhi Jois, T.K.V. Desikachar and Indra Devi. added Some books give this title to Swami Vivekananda, who first brought yoga to the West (1893); write what your teacher says.

general

Q9 · 2 marksModern texts

Write the author of the text “Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha”.

Tap to reveal answer
Swami Satyananda Saraswati of the Bihar School of Yoga.

He wrote it in 1969. It is also the reference book given in your syllabus for the practical papers.

general

Q10 · 2 marksUpanishads

What do you understand by Upanishad? Write the names of the major Upanishads.

Tap to reveal answer
Upanishad means sitting near a teacher to receive the highest knowledge. There are ten principal (mukhya) Upanishads.

Isha, Kena, Katha, Prashna, Mundaka, Mandukya, Taittiriya, Aitareya, Chandogya and Brihadaranyaka. Shvetashvatara is often added as the eleventh.

general

Q11 · 2 marksBhagavad Gita

Write two definitions of yoga according to the Bhagavad Gita.

Tap to reveal answer
(1) Samatvam yoga uchyate (2.48): evenness of mind is yoga. (2) Yogah karmasu kaushalam (2.50): yoga is skill in action.

A third (6.23): yoga is separation from union with pain.

general

Q12 · 2 marksVedanta

Write the names of Shat-sampatti.

Tap to reveal answer
Shama, dama, uparati, titiksha, shraddha and samadhana.

Calmness of mind, control of the senses, withdrawal from distraction, endurance, faith and one-pointedness. They are the third of the four means (sadhana chatushtaya) in Vedanta.

general

Q13 · 2 marksYoga Sutra

Write the types of karma according to the Patanjali Yoga Darshan.

Tap to reveal answer
Four (YS 4.7): shukla (white), krishna (black), shukla-krishna (mixed) and ashukla-akrishna (neither white nor black).

The last belongs to the yogi who acts without desire for results; the first three belong to ordinary people. Karma ripens as birth, lifespan and experience (2.13).

general

Section B: Five-mark questions (answer any 4)

Questions 14 to 19. About 150 words each, or a short table.

Q14 · 5 marksBuddhism

Explain the Ashtangika Marga (eightfold path) given in Buddhism.

Tap to reveal answer

The Buddha gave the eightfold path as the fourth noble truth: the way to end suffering. It is a middle path between indulgence and harsh self-denial.

GroupStepMeaning
Wisdom (prajna)1. Right viewUnderstanding the four noble truths
2. Right intentionFree of craving, ill-will and cruelty
Ethics (sila)3. Right speechTruthful, kind, no gossip
4. Right actionNo killing, stealing or misconduct
5. Right livelihoodEarning without harming
Concentration (samadhi)6. Right effortAvoiding bad states, building good ones
7. Right mindfulnessAwareness of body, feelings, mind and mental states
8. Right concentrationThe four jhanas (deep meditation)

Right effort, mindfulness and concentration are the part that matches yoga practice.

Marks: intro (1), eight steps (3), grouping (1). general

Q15 · 5 marksSamkhya and Yoga

Explain the relationship between Samkhya and Yoga Darshan.

Tap to reveal answer

They are sister systems. Samkhya gives the theory; Yoga gives the practice. Both say there are two ultimate realities, Purusha (pure consciousness) and Prakriti (nature with three gunas), and that suffering ends when Purusha is seen as separate from Prakriti.

PointSamkhyaYoga
EmphasisKnowledge (jnana)Practice (ashtanga)
Principles25 tattvasThe same 25, plus Ishvara
GodClassical Samkhya does not need IshvaraIshvara is a special Purusha, untouched by kleshas
MindBuddhi, ahamkara and manasChitta as one inner instrument
LiberationViveka-khyati (discriminative knowledge)Reached by yoga, ending in kaivalya

Patanjali builds the Yoga Sutra on Samkhya ideas: the seer and the seen (2.17), the three gunas, and kaivalya (4.34).

Marks: shared base (2), table (3). general

Q16 · 5 marksJainism

Explain Tri-Ratna (given in Jainism).

Tap to reveal answer

The three jewels are the path to liberation (moksha), as given in the Tattvartha Sutra.

JewelMeaning
Samyak darshana (right faith)Faith in the true teachers, scriptures and the seven tattvas
Samyak jnana (right knowledge)Knowing the soul and non-soul as they are
Samyak charitra (right conduct)Living by the five great vows: ahimsa, satya, asteya, brahmacharya and aparigraha

The three work together. Faith without knowledge is blind; knowledge without conduct does not free the soul.

Marks: three jewels, 1 each; conclusion (2). general

Q17 · 5 marksBhakti

Explain the types of bhakta.

Tap to reveal answer

The Gita (7.16) names four kinds of people who worship God:

TypeMeaning
ArtaThe distressed, who pray in trouble
JijnasuThe seeker of knowledge
ArtharthiThe seeker of wealth and gain
JnaniThe wise one, who loves God for God’s sake

All four are noble, but the Jnani is the best (7.17) because his devotion is steady and has no demand. added Your teacher may also teach the Bhagavata gradation (kanishtha, madhyama, uttama bhakta): ask which she wants.

Marks: four types (4), best (1). general

Q18 · 5 marksBhakti

Explain Bhakti according to the Narada Bhakti Sutra.

Tap to reveal answer

Meaning. Bhakti is supreme love for God (sutra 2); it is nectar itself (3). One who attains it is satisfied and wants nothing else (4).

Greatness. It is greater than karma, jnana and yoga, because it is itself the fruit (25).

Eleven forms of attachment (sutra 82): to God’s glory, form, worship, remembrance, service (dasya), friendship (sakhya), parental love (vatsalya), the love of lover and beloved (kanta), self-surrender, complete absorption (tanmayata) and the pain of separation (viraha).

Means. Giving up attachment to objects, constant worship, hearing and singing God’s glory, and the grace of a great soul.

Marks: meaning (1), greatness (1), eleven forms (2), means (1). general

Q19 · 5 marksNadanusandhana

Explain the stages of Nadanusandhana.

Tap to reveal answer

Nadanusandhana is listening to the inner sound until the mind rests in it (HYP chapter 4). There are four stages:

StageWhat happens
Arambha (beginning)The Brahma granthi is pierced; bliss; tinkling inner sounds
Ghata (vessel)Prana enters sushumna; the Vishnu granthi is pierced; sound of a kettledrum
Parichaya (increase)Sound of a drum; the great void; pain, old age and disease overcome
Nishpatti (consummation)The Rudra granthi is pierced; sound of flute and vina; ends in raja yoga (samadhi)

Method: close the ears with the hands, listen within and fix the mind there (4.82). Within fifteen days restlessness goes (4.83).

Marks: four stages, 1 each; method (1). class

Section C: Ten-mark questions (answer any 2)

Questions 20 to 22. About 400 words each: introduction, body in headings, conclusion.

Q20 · 10 marksBuddhism

Explain the eight noble paths of Buddhism.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. The Buddha (Siddhartha Gautama, 6th century BCE) taught that life has suffering and that suffering can end. His first sermon, at Sarnath, gave the four noble truths and the eightfold path.

AThe four noble truths

  1. Dukkha: life contains suffering (birth, old age, illness, death, loss).
  2. Samudaya: suffering has a cause, which is craving.
  3. Nirodha: suffering can end, which is nirvana.
  4. Magga: there is a path to that end: the eightfold path.

BThe eightfold path (Ashtangika Marga)

StepMeaningYoga link
Right view (samma ditthi)Seeing life through the four truthsViveka, knowledge
Right intentionRenunciation, goodwill, harmlessnessAhimsa
Right speechTruth, no slander, no harsh wordsSatya
Right actionNo killing, stealing or sexual misconductYama
Right livelihoodA job that harms no oneAhimsa in work
Right effortPreventing and removing bad states, building good onesAbhyasa
Right mindfulnessAwareness of body, feeling, mind and mind objects (satipatthana)Dharana, dhyana
Right concentrationFour jhanas, from joy to equanimitySamadhi

The path is grouped into wisdom (view, intention), ethics (speech, action, livelihood) and concentration (effort, mindfulness, concentration). It is a middle way between indulgence and self-torture.

Conclusion. The eight steps are practised together, not one after another. The goal is nirvana, the end of craving.

Marks: intro (1), four truths (3), eight steps (5), conclusion (1). general

Q21 · 10 marksRajayoga

Explain Rajayoga in detail.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Raja yoga (royal yoga) is the path of mastering the mind, given by Patanjali in the Yoga Sutra (about 195 sutras in four padas). Hatha yoga is said to be a ladder to it. Its goal is kaivalya, the seer resting in his own nature.

AThe mind and its movements

Yoga is chitta-vritti-nirodha (1.2). The five vrittis are pramana, viparyaya, vikalpa, nidra and smriti (1.6). The obstacles are the five kleshas: avidya, asmita, raga, dvesha and abhinivesha (2.3).

BThe eight limbs (ashtanga, 2.29)

LimbMeaning
YamaAhimsa, satya, asteya, brahmacharya, aparigraha
NiyamaShaucha, santosha, tapas, svadhyaya, Ishvara pranidhana
AsanaSteady, comfortable posture (2.46)
PranayamaRegulating breath (2.49)
PratyaharaWithdrawing the senses
DharanaHolding the mind on one place
DhyanaUnbroken flow of attention
SamadhiAbsorption in the object

The first five are outer (bahiranga) and the last three are inner (antaranga). Together dharana, dhyana and samadhi are called samyama (3.4).

COther key ideas

  • Kriya yoga (2.1): tapas, svadhyaya and Ishvara pranidhana, for beginners.
  • Abhyasa and vairagya (1.12): practice and detachment.
  • Samadhi: samprajnata (with seed) and asamprajnata (seedless), ending in kaivalya.

Conclusion. Raja yoga gives a full path from conduct (yama, niyama) through body and breath to meditation and liberation.

Marks: intro (1), vrittis and kleshas (2), eight limbs (5), other ideas (1), conclusion (1). general

Q22 · 10 marksKarma yoga

Explain Karma Yoga in detail.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Karma yoga is the yoga of selfless action, taught mainly in the Bhagavad Gita (chapters 3 and 5). Arjuna refused to fight; Krishna said no one can stay without action (3.5), so the question is how to act without being bound.

AThe core teaching

  • Right to action only (2.47): you have a right to the work, not to its fruits.
  • Nishkama karma: act without desire for results.
  • Equanimity (2.48): be even in success and failure; that evenness is yoga.
  • Skill in action (2.50): yoga is doing the work well.
  • Yajna (3.9): action done as an offering is free; other action binds.

BPractice

  1. Do your own duty (svadharma) fully (3.35).
  2. Give up the sense of “I am the doer” (3.27).
  3. Offer the action and its result to the Lord (Ishvararpana, 3.30).
  4. Work for the welfare of all (loka-sangraha, 3.20).

CBenefits

It cleans the mind, reduces ego and stress, and prepares for meditation and knowledge. Swami Vivekananda made karma yoga a core of his teaching, with service as worship.

Conclusion. Karma yoga turns daily work into yoga without leaving the world.

Marks: intro (1), core teaching (4), practice (3), conclusion (2). general
