In one paragraph

The Vedas are the oldest scriptures: four collections, each with four layers, from hymn to philosophy. The Upanishads, the last layer, speak about Brahman, the self and the world, and are where the word “yoga” first appears. The four purusharthas give the aims of human life, ending in moksha. class general

02 · Vedas and Upanishads

The Scripture Base of Yoga

Four Vedas, four parts, and the Upanishads.

2AThe four Vedas

VedaContent (class summary)
RigvedaChanting and mantras; 21 branches
YajurvedaYajnas and rituals; Shukla and Krishna
SamavedaSongs
AtharvavedaDaily life, healing, spells
Know this
  • Each Veda has four parts: Samhita (mantras), Brahmana (how to use the mantras), Aranyaka (practices and upasana, studied in the forest), Upanishad (knowledge). class
  • Samhita + Brahmana + Aranyaka form the karma kanda; the Upanishads are the jnana kanda. Together: the Veda. class
  • Vedic yoga: mantra yoga in yajnas; complex mudras in worship; upanayana at about age 8 included Gayatri mantra, surya namaskara and nadishodhana pranayama (to enter the seven realms: bhur, bhuvah, svah, mahah, janah, tapah, satyam). History notes
  • The Vedas are Shruti (“that which is heard”), and are traditionally regarded as authorless (apaurusheya). general

2BUpanishads

Know this
  • Upa (near) + ni + shad (sit): sitting near the teacher, near the truth. 108 Upanishads, 10 principal (mukhya). They speak of jiva, jagat and Brahman (Ishvara, jiva, jagat). class History notes
  • The ten: Isha, Kena, Katha, Prashna, Mundaka, Mandukya, Taittiriya, Aitareya, Chandogya, Brihadaranyaka. class general
  • Katha Upanishad (II.3.10-11) is the first to use the word yoga: steady control of the senses. History notes
  • 20 Yoga Upanishads deal with hatha, mantra, laya and raja yoga, nadis, chakras, mudras, bandhas, pranayama, kundalini, dhyana and samadhi. Examples include Yoga Kundalini, Yogatattva, Yogashikha, Yogachudamani, Tejobindu, Shvetashvatara. History notes
  • Upanishads are known as Vedanta, the end of the Vedas; see the Vedanta page for the Mahavakyas and the prasthanatraya. History notes
Related texts
  • Smriti (Manu, Yajnavalkya) tells how to live in society; the Puranas (18 and 18 upa-puranas) and the Itihasas (Ramayana, Mahabharata) carry teaching as story. class

03 · Purushartha chatushtaya

The Four Aims of Life

Aims, ashramas and how they map onto the Veda.

3APurushartha chatushtaya

The four aims of human life: dharma (right living), artha (wealth), kama (desire, enjoyment) and moksha (liberation). class

AshramaAge (class note)Where the Veda speaks of it
Brahmacharya (student)up to 24Samhita
Grihastha (householder)24-48Brahmana
Vanaprastha (retired)48-72Aranyaka
Sannyasafrom 72Upanishads
Know this
  • The first three aims are pursued within the world; moksha is the final aim, as in Vedanta. general
  • Charvaka accepts only artha and kama (see the Buddha, Jaina and Charvaka page). BJC notes
  • Vedanta names moksha the ultimate purushartha. Vedanta notes

10 marksVedas

Explain the Vedas, Upanishads and Purushartha chatushtaya as the scripture base of yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Vedas. Rig, Yajur, Sama, Atharva; each with Samhita, Brahmana, Aranyaka, Upanishad; karma kanda vs jnana kanda. Vedic yoga: mantra yoga in yajnas, mudras, upanayana with Gayatri, surya namaskara and nadishodhana.

Upanishads. Vedanta; 108, ten principal; Ishvara, jiva, jagat; Katha first uses “yoga”; 20 Yoga Upanishads on hatha, mantra, laya, raja yoga, nadis, chakras, kundalini.

Purusharthas. Dharma, artha, kama, moksha, linked to the four ashramas and the parts of the Veda; moksha is the aim that yoga serves.

Conclusion. Yoga draws its vocabulary and aim from these texts.

What is the importance of the Upanishads for yoga?

Tap to reveal answer

They are Vedanta; they relate Ishvara, jiva and jagat; the Katha Upanishad first uses the word yoga; twenty Yoga Upanishads describe hatha, mantra, laya and raja yoga, nadis, chakras, kundalini, dhyana and samadhi.

5 marksVedas
