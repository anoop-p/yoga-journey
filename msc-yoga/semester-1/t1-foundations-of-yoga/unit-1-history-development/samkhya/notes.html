In one paragraph

Samkhya starts from a plain fact: every living being wants happiness and meets suffering. It asks what is really going on, and answers that there are two ultimate realities, conscious Purusha and unconscious Prakriti. Suffering comes from not seeing the difference. Liberation is the clear knowledge that Purusha is separate from Prakriti. Samkhya notes class notebook added

03 · Core ideas: epistemology

How We Know, According to Samkhya

The three pramanas and why right knowledge is the heart of the system.

Epistemology (pramana-shastra) studies how knowledge is gained and what makes it valid. The analysis uses three words: the knower pramata, the object prameya, and the valid means pramana. Example: a student (pramata) sees a flower (prameya) by direct observation (pramana). Samkhya notes

3AThree pramanas in Samkhya

PramanaMeaningExample
PratyakshaDirect perception through the sense organsSeeing a tree; hearing a bell
AnumanaInference from a known relationship to an unknown factSmoke on a hill, so fire
ShabdaReliable verbal testimony, including authoritative teachingLearning a teaching from a reliable teacher

Source: the Samkhya Karika as set out in your typed notes. Samkhya notes

Know this
  • Perception is the primary proof. Other proofs such as inference depend on it. Perception is the ascertainment of each object by its sense. class notebook
  • Inference works from the middle term (linga, e.g. smoke, the less extensive, vyapya) to the major term (lingi, e.g. fire, the more extensive, vyapaka). It needs prior knowledge of both terms. class notebook
  • Inference matters in Samkhya because Prakriti and Purusha are not ordinarily perceived as physical objects. Samkhya notes
  • Shabda: your notebook says it is self-authoritative because the Vedas have no human author and so are free of defect; the knowledge of Sruti, itihasa and purana is also valid because they have the Vedas as source. Your typed notes put it more carefully: it is not acceptance of every statement, and its authority depends on the tradition’s idea of reliable testimony. class notebook Samkhya notes
  • Intuitive knowledge of yogis who have transcended ordinary things exists, but is not treated here because it does not help ordinary understanding. class notebook

3BWhy epistemology matters in Samkhya

Understand it

We say “I am anxious”, “I am confused”, “I am unhappy”, treating every passing state as me. Samkhya separates the witnessing consciousness from the changing mental states. This does not make emotions unreal; it says they belong to Prakriti while Purusha is consciousness itself. Valid knowledge removes this confusion, which is the root of bondage. Samkhya notes

04 · Core ideas: metaphysics

Two Realities, 25 Tattvas and the Theory of Cause

Purusha and Prakriti, how the world evolves from nature, and why consciousness must be separate.

Metaphysics asks what is real. Samkhya answers: two things, Purusha and Prakriti, with the whole manifest world, including mind, a product of Prakriti. This is not the mind-body dualism of some Western thought: Samkhya separates pure consciousness from the entire changing domain of nature, mind and intellect included. Samkhya notes

4HSatkaryavada: the theory of causation

The effect exists in its cause before the cause acts. Production is manifestation of what was already there, not creation from nothing. Because it is the real transformation of the cause, this is also parinamavada. Samkhya notes class notebook

4JSamyoga: why Purusha and Prakriti meet

Understand it

Because of their closeness, insentient Prakriti appears intelligent and neutral Purusha appears to act. That false impression is the cause of bondage. Prakriti evolves for the display of nature to Purusha and for its release. Analogy in your typed notes: a dancer (Prakriti) performs while a spectator (Purusha) witnesses. 7A10 marks

10 marksSamkhya

Explain the Samkhya philosophy in detail.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Samkhya is one of the six darshanas, founded by Kapila. It begins from the three kinds of suffering (adhyatmika, adhibhautika, adhidaivika) and holds that suffering ends with discriminative knowledge. Realistic and dualistic: two realities, Purusha and Prakriti.

Epistemology. Three pramanas: pratyaksha (primary), anumana (needed to know Prakriti and Purusha, who are not perceived) and shabda. Valid knowledge removes the confusion of Purusha with the activities of Prakriti, which is the root of bondage.

Metaphysics. Purusha: pure, many, witness, non-agent. Prakriti: unconscious, one, made of three gunas (sattva, rajas, tamas). Evolution gives 25 tattvas: Prakriti; mahat; ahamkara; manas; five jnanendriyas; five karmendriyas; five tanmatras; five mahabhutas; Purusha. Causation is satkaryavada (parinamavada). Purusha is proved by five arguments and its plurality by three. They meet in samyoga, like a dancer and a spectator.

Ethics. The motive is to end suffering. Vedic ritual means are impure, perishable and unequal; the superior way is renunciation and discriminative knowledge.

Liberation. Purusha realises it is distinct from Prakriti; Prakriti ceases to act for it; at the end of the body it attains final freedom, kaivalya.

Conclusion. Samkhya gives Indian philosophy its map of consciousness and matter; Yoga adds the practice; Vedanta takes the two further into unity.

5 marksSamkhya and Yoga in the paper

Explain the relationship between Samkhya and Yoga Darshan.

Tap to reveal answer

They are sister systems: Samkhya gives the theory, Yoga the practice. Both accept two realities (Purusha and Prakriti), the three gunas, many Purushas, satkaryavada, three pramanas, and that suffering ends when Purusha is known to be separate from Prakriti.

They differ in that Yoga adds Ishvara (a special Purusha untouched by klesha and karma), treats buddhi, ahamkara and manas together as chitta, and gives a practical path, ashtanga yoga, leading to viveka-khyati and samadhi. Samkhya relies on discriminative knowledge. Both reach kaivalya.

5 marksTattvas

Explain the 25 tattvas of Samkhya.

Tap to reveal answer

Tattva is a fundamental principle of reality. Classical Samkhya has 25: Purusha and Prakriti, and 23 evolving from Prakriti: mahat (buddhi), ahamkara, manas, five jnanendriyas (ear, skin, eye, tongue, nose), five karmendriyas (speech, hands, feet, excretion, generation), five tanmatras (sound, touch, form, taste, smell) and five mahabhutas (akasha, vayu, tejas, apas, prithvi). Prakriti is the evolvent; mahat, ahamkara and the tanmatras are both evolvent and evolute; the sixteen are only evolutes; Purusha is neither.

5 marksGunas

Explain the three gunas.

Tap to reveal answer

Prakriti consists of sattva (clarity, lightness, illumination; pleasure), rajas (activity, energy, restlessness; pain) and tamas (inertia, obscuration; delusion). They are always together; only predominance changes. They dominate, support, produce and cooperate with each other, like wick and oil. Liberation is not merely more sattva but discrimination from all gunas.

5 marksCausation

Explain satkaryavada.

Tap to reveal answer

The effect exists in its cause before the cause acts. Five reasons: the non-existent cannot be produced; effects need a fitting material cause; not everything arises from everything; a cause produces only what it can; the effect has the essence of the cause. Examples: oil in sesame, curd from milk, a mango tree giving only mangoes. Samkhya applies this to Prakriti: the universe manifests by its transformation, hence parinamavada.

5 marksPurusha

Prove the existence and plurality of Purusha.

Tap to reveal answer

Existence: aggregates are for another; the spirit lacks the three gunas; a controller is needed (chariot and charioteer); objects of enjoyment need an experiencer; activity aims at release. Plurality: birth, death and organs are allotted individually; activities are not simultaneous; the gunas differ among beings. Purusha is a pure witness: solitary, neutral, spectator, non-agent.

5 marksSuffering

Explain the three kinds of suffering (duhkha-traya).

Tap to reveal answer

Adhyatmika: within the person: bodily (disorder of wind, bile, phlegm) and mental (lust, anger, greed, fear, envy, grief). Adhibhautika: from other beings: men, beasts, birds, reptiles, plants. Adhidaivika: from supernatural or unseen forces. Ordinary remedies are uncertain and impermanent, so Samkhya seeks the certain and final remedy, discriminative knowledge.

5 marksPramanas

Explain the pramanas in Samkhya.

Tap to reveal answer

Pratyaksha (perception, the primary proof), anumana (inference through linga and lingi, e.g. smoke and fire; essential for knowing unperceived Prakriti and Purusha) and shabda (reliable testimony, with the Vedas as source). Yogic intuition exists but is not treated. Yoga accepts the same three; Nyaya four; Advaita six.
