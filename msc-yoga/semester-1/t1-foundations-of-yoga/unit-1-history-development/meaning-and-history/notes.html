In one paragraph

“Yoga” has several meanings (union, absorption, control, equanimity, skill, separation from pain), and each belongs to a different tradition. Its history runs from shamanic and Indus Valley traces through the Vedas, Upanishads, Jain and Buddhist practice, Patanjali, the Hatha texts and the modern teachers. 02 · Meaning and definitions

The Word Yoga and Its Definitions

Three roots, definitions by text, and definitions by modern teachers.

2AMeaning of the word

The word yoga comes from the Sanskrit root yuj. Yujyate anena iti yoga: that by which one is joined. Three roots are given: Meaning notes

RootMeaningDefinition that goes with it
Yuj samyogeTo uniteYoga Yajnavalkya: union of jivatman and paramatman
Yuj samadhauTo contemplate; absorptionVyasa on Yoga Sutra 1.1: “Yoga is samadhi”
Yuj samyamaneTo controlKatha Upanishad: steady control of the senses and mind

SourceDefinitionMeaning

Critical analysis (your notes)

All these definitions are philosophical: they speak of the Atman and the mind and ignore the body. Hatha Yoga, in the post-classical texts, adds the body: cleansing the body, controlling its movements and controlling prana in order to control the mind. So yoga is not only physical, only mental or only spiritual: it is a unified system of all three (body, mind, spirit). Meaning notes

04 · Origin and lineages

Who Taught Yoga First

Traditional accounts of the origin of yoga and the four guru-shishya lines.

4AWho created yoga? Traditional views

Know this
  • Knowledge of yoga passed orally from guru to shishya under the gurukula system, which is believed to predate the Vedic age. History notes
  • Guru (Advayataraka Upanishad): gu = darkness, ru = its dispeller; the guru removes ignorance. History notes Meaning notes
  • Hiranyagarbha is called the creator of yoga in the Yajnavalkya Smriti and the Mahabhagavatam. The Rig Veda calls him the self-manifested (svayambhu) creator of the universe; some texts equate him with Brahma. History notes
  • Ahirbudhnya Samhita (ch. 12, Vaishnava): Hiranyagarbha composed two yoga works: Nirodha Yoga and Karma Yoga. History notes
  • The Pashupati seal of the Indus Valley (Shiva seated in meditation) is the earliest depiction of yoga excavated, so Shiva is called Adi Guru. History notes

4BGuru-shishya lineages (parampara)

LineChain
VaishnavaHiranyagarbha → Krishna → Vasishtha → Patanjali (said to be an incarnation of Adishesha)
Shaiva (Nath)Shiva (Adinath) → Matsyendranath → Gorakshanath → 33 Nath yogis → Svatmarama (founder of Hatha Yoga as given in your notes)
Mundaka Upanishad (1.1.2)Brahma → Atharva → Angiras → Bharadvaja → Satyavaha (Brahma-vidya, Raja Yoga)
Bhagavad Gita (4.1)Krishna → Vivasvan (Sun) → Manu → Ikshvaku → rajarshis

History notes Hatha texts also stress the guru: HYP 4.9 (without the teacher’s compassion, sahaja is hard to attain) and Shiva Samhita 3.11 (only knowledge from the guru’s lips is powerful and useful).

05 · Five periods

The History of Yoga in Five Periods

From shamanism and the Indus Valley to the modern teachers.

5AThe five periods

PeriodApproximate date (your notes)Main evidence
1. Pre-VedicShamanism ~25,000 BCE; Indus Valley ~3300 BCEShamanic postures; Pashupati seal and seated figures
2. Pre-Classical (Vedic)Vedas ~1750 BCE to about 500 BCEVedas, Upanishads, Jainism, Buddhism, Tantra
3. Classical~500 BCEYoga Sutra of Patanjali
4. Post-ClassicalFrom 10 CENath, Hatha and Tantric texts
5. ModernFrom about 1800 CETeachers from Vivekananda onwards

5BPre-Vedic

Know this
  • Shamanism (Siberia, ~25,000 BCE): “shaman” is linked to Pali samana and Sanskrit shramana, “seeker”. Shamans used postures like vajrasana, sukhasana, padmasana, utkatasana with meditation for trance journeys. History notes
  • Indus Valley (~3300 BCE): seals and figures from Harappa and Mohenjodaro resemble asanas; the Pashupati seal is the earliest depiction. History notes

5CPre-Classical or Vedic period

StrandYoga content (your notes)
Vedas (~1750 BCE)Mantra yoga in yajnas; complex mudras in worship; upanayana at about age 8 included Gayatri mantra, surya namaskara and nadishodhana pranayama
UpanishadsCalled Vedanta; Katha (II.3.10-11) first uses the word yoga; they relate Ishvara, jiva and jagat; 20 Yoga Upanishads deal with hatha, mantra, laya, raja yoga, nadis, chakras, mudras, bandhas, pranayama, kundalini, dhyana and samadhi
JainismYoga as the combination of mind, body and speech; yama panchakam, ahimsa; kayotsarga; Mahavira is said to have attained enlightenment in mulabandhasana
Buddhism (563 BCE)Middle path, against self-torment; eightfold path parallels ashtanga yoga; vipassana
Tantra (~5 BCE)Shiva as father of Tantra yoga; tanoti (expand) + trayati (liberate); base of hatha, mantra and kundalini yoga; union of Shiva and Shakti
Pre-ColumbianSaid to show hatha yoga evidence (Colombia), as proof of universality

5DClassical period: the Yoga Sutra

Know this
  • Written by Maharshi Patanjali, who codified yoga scattered across the Vedas, Upanishads, Buddhism and Jainism. He is not the father of yoga. History notes Meaning notes
  • Credited with works on Sanskrit grammar and Ayurveda; hence the Patanjala mantra chanted before yoga practice. Meaning notes
  • The Sutra is also called Yoga Darshana, Ashtanga Yoga and Raja Yoga (a name given by Swami Vivekananda). History notes
  • It made yoga a practical discipline by stressing the eight limbs, removing mysticism, and describes the nature of the mind and ways to control its restlessness. Meaning notes

History notes Tirumandiram (Thirumoolar): described as the first major hatha yoga text; nine tantras; describes tantric kundalini yoga; seven key asanas (padma, svastika, bhadra, gomukha, simha, vira, sukhasana); three pranayama stages (puraka, kumbhaka, rechaka) in the ratio 1:4:2 (12:64:32 in the scan).

5FWhy hatha yoga arose

Know this
  • The Yoga Sutra gives asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana and dhyana little more than definitions; it is mainly mental discipline (a mind-body practice). History notes
  • Yogis realised that physical fitness is needed for pranayama, meditation and daily activity, and that strict yama and niyama discouraged many people. History notes
  • The Nath yogis (Matsyendranath, Gorakshanath) developed hatha yoga, a body-mind practice, to prepare for raja yoga. HYP 1.2: kevalam raja-yogaya hatha-vidya upadishyate. History notes
  • Meaning of hatha: ha = sun, pingala, prana; tha = moon, ida, apana. Balancing the two, or merging prana and apana at the navel (Siddhasiddhanta Paddhati). History notes

5GYoga elements in other religions, and the modern period

Know this
  • Christianity (1st c.): “yoke” as the same idea as yoga; prithvi mudra on icons; rosary meditation. Islam (6th c.): prayer postures resemble vajrasana and shashankasana; 99-bead misbaha. Sufism (13th c.): vajrasana for meditation. History notes
  • Modern period (from about 1800): Vivekananda, Kuvalayananda, Aurobindo, Sivananda, Lahiri, Yogananda (Kriya Yoga), Mahesh Yogi, Vishnudevananda, Satyananda (Bihar School), Ramana, Dayananda, Dhirendra Brahmachari, T. Krishnamacharya (Ashtanga Yoga), Swami Rama, Shriram Sharma Acharya. History notes
  • Modern focus: fitness and therapy with scientific backing; new trends (power yoga, hot yoga) are moving away from traditional hatha yoga. History notes

5HConclusions in your notes and International Day of Yoga

Remember
  • Yoga is known mainly through Patanjali’s Yoga Sutra, but Patanjali was not the founder. History notes
  • Yoga was practised before Patanjali by Jains, Shaivas, Buddhists and other ascetics; the Indus people probably knew parts of it. History notes
  • The exact origin is unknown: yoga is “as old as civilisation”, universal and not tied to one religion. History notes
  • Ashtanga yoga (mind-body) removed mysticism; hatha yoga (body-mind) prepares for it and is the most popular branch. History notes
  • International Day of Yoga: proposed by India’s PM at the UN General Assembly on 27 September 2014; the UN declared 21 June; first celebrated 21 June 2015. History notes

Source note. Several dates and claims here (shamanism 25,000 BCE, pre-Columbian hatha yoga, Mahavira in mulabandhasana) are statements in your teacher’s notes; many are traditional or disputed in modern scholarship.
