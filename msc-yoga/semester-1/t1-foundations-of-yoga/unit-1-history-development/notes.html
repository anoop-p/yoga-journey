01 · Unit

Yoga: meaning, definitions, aims and history

From the root yuj to the modern period.

1Meaning of the word yoga

Yoga comes from the root yuj. Three roots give three meanings: yuj samyoge (to unite), yuj samadhau (to concentrate, absorb) and yuj samyamane (to control). class

RootMeaningSource of the idea
Yuj samyogeUnion of jivatma with paramatmaYoga Yajnavalkya 1.44 (valid for the advaita view)
Yuj samadhauSamadhi, total absorptionVyasa on Yoga Sutra 1.1
Yuj samyamaneControl of mind and sensesKatha Upanishad 2.3.10-11, the first Vedic text to use the word yoga

2Definitions

SourceDefinition
Yoga Sutra 1.2Yoga is stilling the movements of the mind (chitta vritti nirodhah)
Gita 2.48Evenness of mind in success and failure (samatvam yoga uchyate)
Gita 2.50Skill in action (yogah karmasu kaushalam)
Gita 6.23Separation from union with pain
Swami SivanandaHarmony between individual and universe, thought and act
Sri AurobindoAll life is yoga
KuvalayanandaA message for body, mind and spirit

Yoga is not only physical, not only mental and not only spiritual. It is a joined system of all three. class

3Aims and objectives

  • Health of body, and yoga as therapy for lifestyle and stress disorders
  • Better immunity, moral and ethical values, yogic diet
  • Personality development: physical, mental, emotional, social, spiritual
  • Harmony of thought, word and deed
  • Highest aim: self-realisation, knowing that atman and Brahman are one

4Misconceptions

Yoga is not a Hindu-only practice, not magic or siddhis, not only for monks, not only asana, not only treatment. It can be practised by all, whatever caste, creed, age or sex. class

5History: five periods

  1. Pre-Vedic
  2. Pre-classical (Vedic)
  3. Classical
  4. Post-classical
  5. Modern
PeriodWhat happened
Pre-VedicShamanism (Siberia, about 25,000 BCE) used vajrasana, sukhasana, padmasana. Indus Valley (about 3300 BCE): the Pashupati seal, Shiva seated in meditation, earliest picture of yoga. So Shiva is called Adi Guru.
Pre-classicalVedas (about 1750 BCE): mantra, mudra, upanayana with Gayatri, surya namaskar and nadishodhana. Upanishads: Katha first uses "yoga"; 20 yoga Upanishads. Jainism, Buddhism (563 BCE), Tantra, pre-Columbian evidence.
ClassicalPatanjali Yoga Sutra (about 500 BCE): eight limbs, mind-body approach, mysticism removed.
Post-classicalFrom about 10 CE. Nath yogis (Matsyendranath, Gorakshanath) develop Hatha yoga. Goraksha Samhita, Hatha Pradipika (Svatmarama), Gheranda Samhita, Hatha Ratnavali, Tirumandiram (7 CE).
Modern1800 CE onwards: Vivekananda, Aurobindo, Sivananda, Krishnamacharya and others. Fitness and therapy focus.

Yajnavalkya Smriti and the Mahabharata (ch 12) call Hiranyagarbha the creator of yoga. Ahirbudhnya Samhita says he gave two works: Nirodha Yoga and Karma Yoga. Guru means: gu is darkness, ru is the one who removes it (Advayataraka Upanishad). class

Honest note. Conflict in your notes. Day 1 class note: "Father of yoga: Maharshi Patanjali." Your history note: Patanjali "is not the founder", he only codified yoga scattered in the Vedas, Upanishads and Jainism. Write it this way in the exam: Patanjali is the compiler and systematiser of classical yoga, not its originator. Yoga is "as old as civilization".

7Why Hatha yoga arose

Patanjali stressed mind-discipline and strict yama-niyama, which put many people off. The Nath yogis added body and breath practice so that people could prepare for raja yoga. Ha is sun (pingala, prana), tha is moon (ida, apana). The ten Hatha techniques: shatkarma, mitahara, asana, mudra, bandha, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana, samadhi. class

International Day of Yoga: proposed at the UN General Assembly on 27 Sept 2014, declared as 21 June, first celebrated in 2015. class

Three roots of yuj

  • Yuj samyoge: to unite. Yuj samadhau: to concentrate or absorb. Yuj samyamane: to control.

02 · Unit

Vedas, Upanishads and Purushartha Chatushtaya

The scripture base of yoga.

1The Vedas

VedaContent
RigvedaChanting and mantras; 21 branches
YajurvedaYajnas and rituals; Shukla and Krishna
SamavedaSongs
AtharvavedaDaily life, healing, spells

Each Veda has four parts: Samhita (mantras), Brahmana (how to use the mantras), Aranyaka (practices and upasana, studied in the forest) and Upanishad (knowledge). Samhita + Brahmana + Aranyaka is the karma kanda; the Upanishads are the jnana kanda. Together: the Veda. class

2Upanishads

Upa (near) + ni + shad (sit): sitting near the teacher, near the truth. There are 108 Upanishads, 10 principal (mukhya). They speak about jiva, jagat and Brahman. Smriti (Manu, Yajnavalkya) tells how to live in society; the Puranas (18 and 18 upa-puranas) and the Itihasas (Ramayana, Mahabharata) carry the teaching as story. class

The ten principal Upanishads are Isha, Kena, Katha, Prashna, Mundaka, Mandukya, Taittiriya, Aitareya, Chandogya and Brihadaranyaka. general

3Purushartha chatushtaya

The four aims of human life: dharma (right living), artha (wealth), kama (desire, enjoyment) and moksha (liberation). The class note joins them to the four ashramas and the parts of the Veda: general

03 · Unit

Yoga in the Ramayana and Mahabharata

Aranya Kanda, Shanti Parva and Bhishma Parva.

1Ramayana: Aranya Kanda

The Valmiki Ramayana has 7 kandas. Aranya Kanda (75 sargas) is set in the forest, the place of the Aranyakas, so the tone shifts from court politics to spiritual teaching. class

EpisodeYogic meaning
Viradha (sargas 2-4)A cursed gandharva is freed by Rama: even enemies reach liberation through contact with the divine (bhakti idea)
Sharabhanga (5)Chooses the time to leave the body, enters fire: willed death (utkranti)
Sages (6-8)Rama protects the ascetics: pravritti and nivritti support each other
Agastya (11-13)Ashrama of total dharma, tapas, mitahara, sense control. Rama receives Vishnu's bow: wisdom arms right action (karma yoga needs jnana)
Panchavati, Jatayu (13-14)Selfless duty, sacrifice without reward

The explicit Rama Gita (Atman and Maya, three gunas, karma, jnana and bhakti yoga) is from the Adhyatma Ramayana, not Valmiki. Say this in the exam. A kundalini reading of the epic exists but is one interpretive view. class

2Mahabharata: Shanti Parva and Bhishma Parva

Bhishma Parva holds the Bhagavad Gita (chapters 25-42): karma, jnana, bhakti and dhyana yoga, and the definitions samatvam yoga and yogah karmasu kaushalam. general

Shanti Parva is the longest parva: Bhishma teaches Yudhishthira on dharma, rajadharma and moksha dharma. Moksha dharma includes Samkhya and yoga teaching, the Yajnavalkya-Janaka dialogue, and the Mahabharata calls Hiranyagarbha the first teacher of yoga. general

04 · Unit

Yajnavalkya Smriti and Yoga Yajnavalkya

Kundalini, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana.

Yajnavalkya Smriti is a Dharmashastra text of about 1,010 verses, in three kandas: Achara, Vyavahara and Prayashchitta. It is dated roughly 3rd-5th century CE, from Mithila, and comes after Manu Smriti. Its famous commentary is the Mitakshara. class

The yoga teaching belongs to the Yoga Yajnavalkya, given to his wife Gargi (Maitreyi in some versions). It is a bridge between Patanjali and Hatha yoga. class

TopicPoints from your notes
KundaliniOne of the earliest systematic accounts. Coiled energy at the base, linked to nadis, agni and prana. Wakes through pranayama and nadi-shodhana.
PranayamaAbout 100 verses. Nadis ida, pingala, sushumna. Purification, then puraka, kumbhaka, rechaka. Names ujjayi, bhastrika, alternate-nostril.
PratyaharaWithdrawal of senses, the bridge from outer to inner limbs. Five forms. Marma points: 18.
DharanaFix the gaze (tratak), japa, visualisation, five chakras with seeds lam, vam, ram, yam, ham.
DhyanaSaguna meditation (heart lotus, Vasudeva) and nirguna (jivatma is paramatma). Leads to samadhi.

The text lists ten yamas and ten niyamas, and a six-limbed scheme: pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana, japa, samadhi. class

Shad Darshanas: four areas

Epistemology, metaphysics, ethics and liberation.

Darshana comes from drish, to see. Six schools: Nyaya (logic, Gautama), Vaisheshika (matter, Kanada), Samkhya (consciousness and matter, Kapila), Yoga (Patanjali), Purva Mimamsa (ritual and dharma, Jaimini) and Vedanta (ultimate truth, Vyasa/Badarayana). Four areas of study: epistemology, metaphysics, ethics, liberation. class

Mahavakyas: Prajnanam Brahma (Aitareya), Aham Brahmasmi (Brihadaranyaka), Tat tvam asi (Chandogya), Ayam Atma Brahma (Mandukya). Core of Advaita: Brahma satyam, jagan mithya, jivo brahmaiva naparah. class

06 · Unit

Thoughts of Buddha, Jaina and Charvaka

Three nastika schools.

Astika schools accept the Veda (the six darshanas). Nastika schools do not: Buddhism, Jainism and Charvaka. Nastika here means rejecting Vedic authority, not simply atheist. class

BuddhismJainismCharvaka
KnowledgeExperience, inferenceFive kinds; anekantavada, syadvadaOnly perception
RealityImpermanence, no-self, dependent originationJiva and ajiva, karma as subtle matterFour elements; mind from matter
EthicsFive precepts, eightfold pathFive great vows, ahimsa firstPleasure
LiberationNirvanaMoksha (kaivalya)None beyond death

1Buddhism

Four noble truths: dukkha, samudaya (craving), nirodha (cessation), magga (path). Eightfold path: right view and intention (wisdom); right speech, action, livelihood (conduct); right effort, mindfulness, concentration (meditation). Three marks: anicca, dukkha, anatta. Twelve nidanas from avidya to old age and death. class

2Jainism

24 Tirthankaras, Mahavira the last. Three jewels: right perception, right knowledge, right conduct. Five great vows: ahimsa, satya, asteya, brahmacharya, aparigraha. Seven tattvas: jiva, ajiva, asrava, bandha, samvara, nirjara, moksha. Soul is pure but karma binds it. class

3Charvaka

Also Lokayata, founder traditionally Brihaspati. Only perception is valid, only four elements exist (not akasha), consciousness comes from them like intoxication from fermenting, no soul, karma or rebirth. Opponents say rejecting inference refutes itself. class

Charvaka and the four elements

  • Charvaka (materialist) accepts only four elements: earth, water, fire and air. It does not accept ether (akasha), since ether cannot be perceived.
