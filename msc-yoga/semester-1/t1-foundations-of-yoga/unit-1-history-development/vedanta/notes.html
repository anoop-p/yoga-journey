01 · Overview

What Vedanta Is

The end of the Vedas, its texts and its three main schools.

In one paragraph

Vedanta is the philosophy of the Upanishads. Its core claim (Advaita) is that Brahman alone is ultimately real, the world is mithya (experienced but not ultimately real), and the individual self is none other than Brahman. Bondage is a mistake of knowing (avidya), so liberation comes from knowledge, not from action. Vedanta notes

03 · Core ideas: epistemology

How We Know Brahman

Six pramanas, the Mahavakyas, adhyasa and the theory of error.

In Advaita, knowledge arises when the mind (antahkarana) takes the form of the object (a vritti) and the object is revealed by the consciousness that illumines it. Terms: pramata (knower), prameya (object), pramana (means), pramiti (resulting valid knowledge). Vedanta notes

3BShruti as the means to know Brahman

Know this
  • Brahman is not an object of the senses and cannot be proved by inference alone, so the Shruti (Upanishads) is the means. Vedanta notes
  • Perception and inference work in the empirical (vyavaharika) world; reasoning and experience support Shruti but do not replace it. Vedanta notes
  • Shabda is not mere belief: it is careful teaching by a competent guru, understood through shravana, manana, nididhyasana. Vedanta notes

3CError and how knowledge removes it

Know this
  • Adhyasa (Shankara): superimposing the qualities of one thing on another. The basic error is superimposing body, senses and mind (the not-self) on Atman, giving “I am the body” and “I am the doer”. Vedanta notes
  • Anirvachaniya-khyati: the “snake” seen on a rope is neither absolutely real nor absolutely non-existent (indeterminable). It is sublated once the rope is known; the criterion of truth is non-contradiction (abadhitatva). Vedanta notes
  • Other schools differ (Ramanuja: satkhyati; Madhva: abhinava-anyathakhyati) but all say error is wrong apprehension. Vedanta notes
  • Knowledge of Brahman creates no new object; it removes avidya, as knowing the rope removes the snake (jnana-nivartya). So valid knowledge is the direct means to liberation. Vedanta notes

04 · Core ideas: metaphysics

Brahman, Maya, Jiva and the World

What is real, and what only appears.

4ABrahman, Atman, Maya

Know this
  • Brahman: satyam jnanam anantam Brahma (Taittiriya 2.1.1): truth, consciousness, infinity. Sat-chit-ananda is its nature, not a set of qualities it has. Vedanta notes
  • Nirguna Brahman is the highest truth (paramarthika). Saguna Brahman (Ishvara) is Brahman with Maya as creator, sustainer, dissolver; object of worship. Vedanta notes
  • Atman: pure consciousness, witness of body, senses, mind and the three states. Not the body, senses, mind or intellect. Jiva and Brahman differ only through limiting adjuncts (upadhi). Vedanta notes
  • Maya has two functions: avarana (veiling the true nature) and vikshepa (projecting multiplicity). The world is mithya. Vedanta notes
  • Vivartavada: apparent transformation, like rope as snake; contrast Samkhya’s parinamavada. Brahman is both material (upadana) and efficient (nimitta) cause, as clay is for pots (Chandogya 6.1). Vedanta notes

05 · Core ideas: ethics and liberation

Preparation and Moksha

Sadhana chatushtaya, nishkama karma, the three-fold path and the two kinds of liberation.

5AEthics in Vedanta

Know this
  • Foundation: unity of existence. If one Self dwells in all, injury to another is injury to oneself. The Isha Upanishad: the sage who sees all beings in the Self and the Self in all has no hatred. Vedanta notes
  • Dharma: right action by role and capacity (varna-ashrama-dharma in the classical frame). Vedanta notes
  • Ethics prepares the mind (chitta-shuddhi) to understand and retain the teaching. For the knower, virtue is spontaneous. Vedanta notes
  • Nishkama karma (Gita 2.47): act without clinging to the fruits, as an offering to Ishvara. Gita 16 (daivi sampad) lists the aspirant’s qualities; Gita 12.13-19 describes the devotee. Vedanta notes
  • Ahimsa, satya, asteya, brahmacharya and aparigraha are recognised; also svadhyaya, seva, dana and daya. Vedanta notes
Sadhana chatushtayaMeaning
VivekaDiscrimination between the eternal and the non-eternal
VairagyaDispassion towards the fruits of action here and hereafter
Shat-sampattiSix virtues: shama (calmness), dama (sense control), uparati (inner restraint), titiksha (forbearance), shraddha (faith in teacher and scripture), samadhana (steady attention)
MumukshutvaIntense longing for liberation

Vedanta notes Teaching point: ethics is both a path (preparation) and a fruit (the natural conduct of the wise).

5BLiberation (moksha)

Know this
  • Bondage is not a real chain but the notion “I am a limited doer and enjoyer”, from avidya. Vedanta notes
  • Moksha is not produced by action; it is one’s nature (nitya-prapta), recognised when ignorance goes. Mundaka 3.2.9: the knower of Brahman becomes Brahman. Vedanta notes
  • Action cannot give liberation because it works within duality; knowledge removes ignorance. Karma, bhakti and yoga help by purifying and steadying the mind. Vedanta notes

6CAdvaita and Patanjala Yoga

Yoga holds a dualism of Purusha and Prakriti and reaches kaivalya through ashtanga yoga; Advaita holds non-dualism and reaches moksha through knowledge of Brahman. Yoga borrows Vedantic ideas: Atman, the koshas, the three states and turiya, and meditation on Om. Vedanta notes

10 marksVedanta

Explain Vedanta darshana (Advaita) in detail.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Vedanta is the culmination of the Vedas, the philosophy of the Upanishads, Brahma Sutras and Gita (prasthanatraya). Schools: Advaita (Shankara), Vishishtadvaita (Ramanuja), Dvaita (Madhva). Core: Brahma satyam, jagan mithya, jivo brahmaiva naparah.

Epistemology. Six pramanas; Shruti is the means to know Brahman, supported by reasoning and experience; Mahavakyas; error is adhyasa rooted in avidya; knowledge removes ignorance.

Metaphysics. Brahman as sat-chit-ananda (nirguna and saguna); Atman as witness; Maya with avarana and vikshepa; world mithya (vivartavada); three orders of reality; jiva and Ishvara as Brahman under different upadhis; koshas, three bodies, three states, turiya.

Ethics. Based on unity of beings; sadhana chatushtaya, dharma, nishkama karma; ethics prepares the mind and becomes natural conduct for the knower.

Liberation. Moksha is removal of avidya, not a produced result; shravana, manana, nididhyasana give aparoksha-jnana; jivanmukti and videhamukti.

Conclusion. Avidya binds, knowledge frees.

5 marksVedanta

Explain the six pramanas of Advaita Vedanta.

Tap to reveal answer

Pratyaksha (perception), anumana (inference), upamana (comparison), shabda (testimony; for Brahman, Shruti), arthapatti (postulation) and anupalabdhi (non-apprehension), with examples: pot; smoke and fire; gavaya; Mahavakyas; the fat man who does not eat by day; absence of a pot. Samkhya and Yoga accept three, Nyaya four.

5 marksVedanta

Explain Maya and the three orders of reality.

Tap to reveal answer

Maya is Brahman’s power by which the world appears, neither real nor unreal, with avarana (veiling) and vikshepa (projecting). Reality has three orders: paramarthika (Brahman), vyavaharika (the empirical world, real until Brahman is known) and pratibhasika (illusions such as rope-snake, sublated by later knowledge).

5 marksVedanta

Explain the sadhana chatushtaya.

Tap to reveal answer

Fourfold qualification: viveka (discrimination of eternal and non-eternal), vairagya (dispassion), shat-sampatti (shama, dama, uparati, titiksha, shraddha, samadhana) and mumukshutva (longing for liberation). They prepare the mind for knowledge.

5 marksVedanta

Compare Samkhya and Advaita Vedanta.

Tap to reveal answer

Samkhya: two realities, many Purushas, real evolution (parinama), bondage from non-discrimination, liberation as isolation. Advaita: one Brahman, one Atman, apparent world through Maya (vivarta), bondage from avidya, liberation as identity with Brahman.

5 marksVedanta

Explain jivanmukti and videhamukti.

Tap to reveal answer

Jivanmukti: liberation while living; the knower lives on, free from the sense of bondage. Videhamukti: liberation at the fall of the body after prarabdha karma is exhausted. Sanchita karma is destroyed by knowledge, agami is not accrued without doership, prarabdha runs its course.
