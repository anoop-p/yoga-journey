The Smriti and the Yoga Treatise

In one paragraph

Two things sit under the name Yajnavalkya. The Yajnavalkya Smriti is a Dharmashastra text on conduct, law and penance, second in influence only to the Manusmriti. The Yoga Yajnavalkya is a separate yoga treatise, a bridge between Patanjali and Hatha Yoga, with early systematic teaching on kundalini, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana and dhyana. The syllabus topic draws on the yogic strand. Yajnavalkya notes

1AThe text and its author

PointYour notes say
TypeOne of the most important Dharmashastra texts; Sanskrit, in sloka metre; part of the Smriti tradition; follows the Manusmriti and is regarded by many scholars as clearer, more systematic and more juristically sophisticated Yajnavalkya notes

1BStructure: three kandas

KandaSubject
AcharaRules of conduct, personal and social duties, sacraments, daily observances (the largest)
VyavaharaLegal procedure: courts, evidence, contracts, disputes, the eighteen titles of law

02 · The Smriti proper

Conduct, Law and Penance

What the Achara-kanda teaches and why the text mattered.

2ATeachings of the Achara-kanda

TeachingContent
Sarva-samarpana bhava (total surrender)Conduct after knowledge should be done as total dedication to the Divine. Unlike texts that offer only good acts, Yajnavalkya says all actions, even prohibited ones and their consequences, are to be offered, with contrition, self-reproach and a resolve not to repeat them
Dharma-sadhana (means of dharma)Helping others is good, harming is evil; non-violence and goodwill. Qualities: ahimsa, satya, asteya, shaucha, indriya-nigraha, dana, dhriti (steadfastness), kshama (forbearance), dama (self-restraint). Also permitted and forbidden foods, with expiation in the Prayashchitta-kanda
Dana (charity)Giving suited to the recipient’s needs; even someone unable to do tapas receives a share of its fruit through giving
Pitri-yajnaRites for ancestors, including pinda; sincerity and devotion; neglect is said to bring vamsha-nasha (decline of the family line); pleased ancestors give long life, wealth, learning, heaven, liberation, contentment
Panchayatana pujaWorship of five deities (Surya, Ganapati, Subrahmanya, Shiva, with Lakshmi added): Ganapati-kalpa (removing obstacles), Lakshmi-kalpa (prosperity), Graha-shanti (warding off the three kinds of affliction, adhyatmika, adhibhautika, adhidaivika)
Rajadharma and varnashramaDuties of the king and of ascetics; duties of the four varnas; the role of women (grace resides where women are honoured); everything unfolds by the Divine Will

2BSignificance and legacy

Know this
  • The three-fold structure made it easier to cite than the Manusmriti; a principal source of medieval jurisprudence. Yajnavalkya notes
  • The Vyavahara-kanda affected actual legal administration more than the corresponding part of Manu; first major European translation in German, 1849. Yajnavalkya notes
  • Major commentary: Mitakshara of Vijnaneshvara (11th-12th century), which became one of the two great schools of Hindu law (with the Dayabhaga). Yajnavalkya notes
  • The yogic component is one of the fullest pre-hatha treatments of kundalini and pranayama, influencing yoga before the 12th century. Yajnavalkya notes

03 · The yoga treatise

Kundalini, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana

The yogic teaching that the syllabus lists.

3AThe Yoga Yajnavalkya

Know this
  • A specialised yoga treatise, presented as the sage’s instruction to his wife Gargi (in some recensions Maitreyi). Spiritual instruction was not restricted to men. Yajnavalkya notes
  • Its limbs resemble Patanjali’s ashtanga yoga, and in places lists ten yamas and ten niyamas. Yajnavalkya notes
  • It contains material absent from the Yoga Sutra: a developed doctrine of kundalini, and one of the most detailed classical discussions of pranayama, pratyahara, dharana and dhyana before hatha yoga. It is a bridge text between the Patanjala system and Hatha Yoga. Yajnavalkya notes
  • A separate text, conventionally studied with the Smriti under this sage’s name; related works: the Smriti proper and the Brihad-Yajnavalkya. Yajnavalkya notes
  • Definition of yoga (YY 1.44): union of jivatman and paramatman (see the meaning page). Meaning notes

3BKundalini

One of the earliest systematic expositions: a latent, coiled energy at the base of the trunk, linked to the nadis and to older Vedic ideas of Agni (inner fire) and Prana. Its awakening is a natural extension of pranayama and nadi-shodhana, and is central to advanced attainment and samadhi. Yajnavalkya notes

3CPranayama

Know this
  • About a hundred verses are given to pranayama. Yajnavalkya notes
  • The theory of the nadis (ida, pingala, sushumna) and the need for nadi-shodhana first. Yajnavalkya notes
  • Three phases: puraka (inhalation), kumbhaka (retention), rechaka (exhalation). Yajnavalkya notes
  • Named techniques: alternate-nostril breathing, ujjayi (the victorious breath), bhastrika (bellows breath), with methods. Yajnavalkya notes
  • Physiological and psychological effects and a therapeutic role in Ayurveda. Pranayama purifies body and mind and makes the inner limbs possible. Yajnavalkya notes

3DPratyahara, dharana, dhyana

LimbTeaching in the Yoga Yajnavalkya
PratyaharaWithdrawal of the senses; the bridge between external limbs (asana, pranayama) and internal ones (dharana, dhyana, samadhi). Five forms; the theory of marma with eighteen focal points in the body
DharanaKey to mastering the mind. Methods: fixing the gaze on one point (trataka); mantra repetition (japa); visualising a form; meditation on the five chakras with seed syllables lam, vam, ram, yam, ham, to unite the mind with Parameshvara
DhyanaUninterrupted flow of awareness to one object; leads to bondage or freedom depending on what and how one meditates. Saguna: a symbol, e.g. the heart as an eight-petalled lotus with the Self as Vasudeva, Narayana or Purushottama, towards the state called Vaishvanara. Nirguna: direct realisation of the oneness of jivatman and Paramatman/Brahman

Dhyana prepares samadhi, where the individual self and the highest Self are equal and abide as one in Brahman. In the fuller scheme this gives a six-limbed (shadanga) formulation: pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana, japa, samadhi, related to but distinct from Patanjali’s eight. Yajnavalkya notes

10 marksYajnavalkya

Explain the yogic teachings in the Yajnavalkya tradition.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. The Yajnavalkya Smriti (Gupta period, three kandas) is a Dharmashastra text; the Yoga Yajnavalkya, given to Gargi, is the yoga treatise, a bridge between Patanjali and Hatha Yoga.

Kundalini. Coiled energy at the base of the trunk, linked to nadis, Agni and Prana; awakened through pranayama and nadi-shodhana; central to samadhi.

Pranayama. About a hundred verses; nadis, nadi-shodhana, puraka-kumbhaka-rechaka, alternate-nostril, ujjayi, bhastrika, effects and Ayurvedic role.

Pratyahara. Bridge between outer and inner limbs; five forms; eighteen marma points.

Dharana. Trataka, japa, visualisation, the five chakras with lam, vam, ram, yam, ham.

Dhyana. Saguna and nirguna; heart lotus with Vasudeva; leads to samadhi; six-limbed scheme.

Conclusion. A fuller pre-hatha account than Patanjali, uniting dharma in society with inner discipline.

Explain dhyana in the Yoga Yajnavalkya.

Tap to reveal answer

Uninterrupted flow of awareness to one object; leads to bondage or freedom depending on its object. Saguna dhyana uses a symbol (heart lotus, Vasudeva, Narayana) towards Vaishvanara; nirguna dhyana is realising jivatman-Paramatman oneness. It prepares samadhi.

5 marksYajnavalkya

How does the Yoga Yajnavalkya compare with Patanjali?

Tap to reveal answer

Similar limbs, but ten yamas and niyamas in places, a developed kundalini doctrine, far fuller pranayama, and a six-limbed scheme. It is a bridge between Patanjali and Hatha Yoga.
