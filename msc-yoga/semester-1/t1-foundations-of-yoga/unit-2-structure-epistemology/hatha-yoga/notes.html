1AMeaning

SyllableMeaning
HaSun: pingala, prana
ThaMoon: ida, apana

Hatha Yoga symbolically represents the balancing and integration of opposing energies: solar and lunar, activating and calming, prana and apana, pingala and ida. Purpose: to prepare the practitioner for higher states of yoga. class notes (Hatha) Gorakshanath notes

Important clarification

Hatha is not merely physical exercise. The body and breath are instruments for mental and spiritual transformation. Gorakshanath notes

Key verse

Kevalam raja-yogaya hatha-vidya upadishyate: hatha vidya is taught for the sake of raja yoga alone (Hatha Yoga Pradipika). class notes (Hatha)

02 · Historical and philosophical background

Nath Yogis and Their Texts

Matsyendranath, Gorakshanath, Svatmarama.

2AOrigin and texts

Developed by the Nath yogis, Matsyendranath and Gorakshanath. Gorakshanath is one of the most important figures associated with its development: a practical tradition of systematic methods for transforming body, breath and mind. Svatmarama wrote the Hatha Yoga Pradipika. class notes (Hatha) Gorakshanath notes

03 · Structure

Ten Techniques, Prana and Nadis

What the path is made of.

3ATen techniques (class list)

Shatkarma→Mitahara→Asana→Mudra→Bandha→Pranayama→Pratyahara→Dharana→Dhyana→Samadhi

3BPrinciple

Stable body, regulated breath, steady mind, meditation. class notes (Hatha)

3CPrana and nadis

Prana is the vital force. When the prana is controlled, the mind becomes steady; so pranayama is a method for regulating prana, calming the mind, developing concentration and preparing for meditation. Gorakshanath notes

NadiAssociation
IdaLeft; lunar; cooling; mental processes
PingalaRight; solar; heating; activating
SushumnaCentral; higher meditation; Kundalini awakening

Aim: balance ida and pingala and direct prana through sushumna. Gorakshanath notes
