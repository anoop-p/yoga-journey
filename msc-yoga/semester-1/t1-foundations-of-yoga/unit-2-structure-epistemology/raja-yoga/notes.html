1BDefinition and aim

Know this · YS 1.2

Yogash citta-vritti-nirodhah: Yoga is the stilling (nirodha) of the fluctuations (vrittis) of the mind-stuff (citta). Raja notes

YS 1.3: when the mind is still, the Seer (drashta) abides in its own true nature. Darshana notes

Understand it

Ordinary experience is a stream: a perception leads to a feeling, the feeling to a memory, the memory to a desire, the desire to an imagined plan, and back to perception. Each item in that stream is a vritti. Yoga does not try to destroy the mind. It trains the mind until the stream settles, and then the Seer (Purusha, pure consciousness) is no longer mistaken for the stream. Raja notes added

Think of a pond. While the surface is disturbed you see only ripples; when it is still you see the bottom. The ripples are the vrittis, the stillness is nirodha, and the bottom is Purusha. added: an analogy of mine, not from your notes

02 · Historical and philosophical background

Patanjali, the Sutra and Samkhya

Where Raja Yoga comes from, the text it rests on and how it differs from Samkhya, the philosophy it shares.

03 · Structure

Citta, Vrittis, Kleshas and Karma

The parts the system is made of: the mind and what disturbs it.

In the teacher’s pattern, Structure means the parts the system is made of. For Raja Yoga that is the mind and its disturbances: citta, its five levels, its five vrittis, the kleshas that disturb it, and the karma and samskara that keep the cycle going.

3ACitta: the mind-stuff

Know this
  • Buddhi, ahamkara and manas function together as chitta, the mind in the broad sense. It belongs to Prakriti, so it is not conscious by itself. Darshana notes
  • Chitta is fundamentally sattvic but is disturbed by rajas and tamas. Darshana notes
  • It is affected by perception, memory, desire, emotion, imagination, sleep and conditioning. Raja notes
  • Yoga aims to master chitta, not destroy it. Raja notes
ElementRole in knowing
Drashta (Purusha)The Seer: pure witnessing consciousness
Drishya (Prakriti and its products)The seen: body, senses, mind, intellect and ego
ChittaThe mental instrument, illumined by the Seer, that takes the form of the object
VrittiThe modification by which chitta takes the form of an object

Knowing happens when chitta, lit by consciousness, takes the shape of an object. Suffering arises when the Seer is identified with these modifications (YS 2.17). Darshana notes

Understand it · avidya (YS 2.5)

Avidya takes the non-eternal as eternal, the impure as pure, pain as pleasure, and the non-Self as the Self. Viparyaya is a momentary error (rope for snake); avidya is the deep, standing error behind suffering. Because everything else grows from it, removing avidya is what ends the kleshas. Raja notes Darshana notes

3EKarma and samskara

Know this
  • Kleshas are the roots of the store of karma (karmashaya, YS 2.12). While the roots exist, karma ripens as birth, lifespan and experience (YS 2.13). Darshana notes
  • Samskara is a latent impression produced by experience and action. The cycle is action → impression → tendency → further action; yoga practice weakens the patterns that maintain bondage. Raja notes
  • Types of karma, YS 4.7: Krishna (black), Shukla (white), Shukla-Krishna (mixed), Ashukla-Akrishna (neither; of the advanced yogin). Raja notes

04 · Method

Abhyasa, Kriya Yoga and the Eight Limbs

How the path is practised, from steadying the mind to seedless samadhi.

Method is the practice. Patanjali gives it in layers: first the two means of steadying the mind, then the remedies for the kleshas, then the eight limbs, and finally the advanced state, samyama.

Abhyasa + Vairagya→Kriya yoga→Ashtanga yoga→Samyama→Samadhi→Viveka-khyati→Kaivalya

4AAbhyasa and vairagya (YS 1.12)

MeansMeaning
AbhyasaPractice: continuous, sustained effort to keep the mind steady
VairagyaDispassion: freedom from attachment to objects of experience
Understand it

Abhyasa is the push towards steadiness; vairagya removes what keeps pulling the mind away. Either alone fails: practice without dispassion is undone by desire, and dispassion without practice leaves the mind unsteady. Together the citta becomes steady. Raja notes added

05 · Epistemology

How We Know, How We Err, What Frees

The pramanas, the five vrittis as a theory of the mind, and the knowledge that ends bondage.

Epistemology asks how we know and how we err. In Indian terms: the knower (pramata), the object known (prameya) and the valid means of knowing (pramana). In Yoga it is tied to psychology and practice: how the mind knows, how it goes wrong, and how right knowledge ends suffering. Darshana notes

5AThree pramanas (YS 1.7)

PramanaMeaningNote
PratyakshaDirect perception through the senses (and, in advanced states, direct insight)Basis of empirical knowledge; can mislead when conditions are poor (rope and snake)
AnumanaInference from a known relationshipSmoke and fire; used for what cannot be perceived, such as the nature of Prakriti and Purusha
Agama (shabda)Reliable verbal testimony of authoritative teachers and textsNot blind belief: validity depends on a reliable source and agreement with reason and experience

Darshana notes Yoga accepts three pramanas, like Samkhya. Nyaya accepts four (adds upamana, comparison). Advaita Vedanta accepts more, including arthapatti and anupalabdhi.

5BValid knowledge, error and root error

TermWhat it is
Pramana (vritti)Correct cognition
ViparyayaMomentary error: a wrong cognition not based on the real form of the object
VikalpaWord-knowledge with no real object
AvidyaThe root error: non-eternal as eternal, impure as pure, pain as pleasure, non-Self as Self (YS 2.5)
Understand it · why epistemology matters here

The yogic problem is epistemic at root. The Seer is mistaken for the activities of the mind (YS 1.4: the Seer appears to take the form of the vrittis). Bondage is therefore a case of wrong knowing, and liberation is right knowing that is experienced and kept steady, not just believed. Darshana notes added

  • Nirvichara vaisharadya and ritambhara prajna (YS 1.47-48): in deep meditation clarity arises and insight becomes “truth-bearing”. Darshana notes

06 · Metaphysics and liberation

Purusha, Prakriti, Ishvara and Kaivalya

What is real in Patanjala Yoga, why bondage happens, and what liberation is.

Metaphysics asks what is real. Yoga follows Samkhya’s dualism and adds Ishvara. Its central problem is the misidentification of Purusha with the processes of Prakriti; its central solution is discriminative knowledge. Raja notes Darshana notes

6ATwo realities

PrincipleNatureKey sutra
PurushaPure consciousness; the witness; unchanging; not the body, senses, emotions or mind; many in numberYS 2.20: the Seer is pure consciousness, though appearing to see through the mind’s modifications
PrakritiUnconscious primordial nature; source of body and mind; made of three gunas; constantly evolvingYS 2.18: the seen has the nature of illumination, activity and inertia, consists of elements and senses, and exists for experience and liberation

6BThe three gunas

GunaQualityIn the mind
SattvaIllumination, lightness, harmonyClarity, calm, insight
RajasActivity, energy, restlessnessDesire, agitation, attachment
TamasInertia, heaviness, obscurationDullness, confusion, resistance

Raja notesDarshana notes The four levels of the gunas (YS 2.19: vishesha, avishesha, linga-matra, alinga) are the manifest and unmanifest stages of Prakriti. Liberation is not merely increasing sattva: it needs discrimination of Purusha from all gunas, sattva included.

6CEvolution of Prakriti (the tattvas)

Yoga accepts Samkhya’s account: Prakriti evolves into mahat or buddhi, ahamkara, manas, five jnanendriyas, five karmendriyas, five tanmatras and five mahabhutas. Counting mahat, ahamkara, manas and the 20 later items gives 23 evolutes; with Prakriti that is 24, and Purusha makes the 25 tattvas. Darshana notes

7A10 marks: Explain Raja Yoga in detail

10 marksRaja Yoga

Explain Raja Yoga in detail.

Tap to reveal answer

1. Introduction. Raja Yoga is the “royal path” of disciplining the mind, the Patanjala Yoga Darshana. YS 1.2: yogash citta-vritti-nirodhah, yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of citta. Aim: immediate citta-vritti-nirodha, deeper viveka-khyati, ultimate kaivalya.

2. Historical and philosophical background. Systematised by Maharshi Patanjali in the Yoga Sutra (about 195 sutras, four padas: Samadhi, Sadhana, Vibhuti, Kaivalya). Commentaries: Vyasa Bhashya, Tattva Vaisharadi, Rajamartanda. One of the six darshanas; shares Samkhya’s Purusha-Prakriti dualism and gunas, adds Ishvara and a practical method (sesvara Samkhya).

3. Structure. Chitta (buddhi, ahamkara, manas) is a product of Prakriti; five levels (kshipta, mudha, vikshipta, ekagra, niruddha); five vrittis (pramana, viparyaya, vikalpa, nidra, smriti), klishta or aklishta; five kleshas (avidya, asmita, raga, dvesha, abhinivesha) with avidya as root; karma and samskara keep the cycle going.

4. Method. Abhyasa and vairagya (YS 1.12); kriya yoga (tapas, svadhyaya, Ishvara-pranidhana, YS 2.1); ashtanga yoga (YS 2.29): yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara (external) and dharana, dhyana, samadhi (internal), together samyama; kinds of samadhi: samprajnata, asamprajnata, nirbija. Vibhutis are by-products, not the goal.

5. Epistemology. Three pramanas: pratyaksha, anumana, agama (YS 1.7). Viparyaya is momentary error; avidya the root error. Liberating knowledge is viveka-khyati (YS 2.26), realised by practice, not belief.

6. Metaphysics and liberation. Purusha (conscious, many) and Prakriti (three gunas) with Ishvara as purusha-vishesha. Bondage is the conjunction of Seer and seen through avidya (YS 2.17). Liberation is kaivalya (YS 2.25, 4.34): Purusha abiding in its own nature.

Conclusion. Still the mind, develop discrimination, distinguish Purusha from Prakriti, attain kaivalya.

Citta-vrittis are the modifications of citta (YS 1.5-11), each klishta (afflicting) or aklishta:

  1. Pramana: valid knowledge (perception, inference, testimony), YS 1.7
  2. Viparyaya: misconception, e.g. rope seen as snake, YS 1.8
  3. Vikalpa: verbal construct with no real object, e.g. horns of a hare, YS 1.9
  4. Nidra: sleep, a modification based on absence of content, YS 1.10
  5. Smriti: memory, not letting go of an experienced object, YS 1.11

Yoga stills all five, not only the wrong ones.

5 marksKleshas

Explain the five kleshas.

Tap to reveal answer

Kleshas are afflictions that cause suffering (YS 2.3).

  1. Avidya: ignorance, taking the non-eternal as eternal, impure as pure, pain as pleasure, non-Self as Self; the root and field of the rest
  2. Asmita: egoism, identifying the Seer with the instruments of seeing
  3. Raga: attachment to pleasure
  4. Dvesha: aversion to pain
  5. Abhinivesha: clinging to life, fear of death

Flow: avidya → asmita → raga and dvesha → abhinivesha → suffering. Kriya yoga weakens them.

5 marksAbhyasa-vairagya

Explain abhyasa and vairagya.

Tap to reveal answer

YS 1.12: the means of steadying citta. Abhyasa is continuous, sustained effort to keep the mind steady. Vairagya is freedom from attachment to objects of experience. Together they make the citta steady: practice without dispassion is undone by desire, dispassion without practice leaves the mind unsteady.

5 marksKriya yoga

Explain Kriya Yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

YS 2.1: tapas (discipline, austerity), svadhyaya (self-study, study of sacred teachings), Ishvara-pranidhana (surrender or dedication to Ishvara). It reduces the kleshas and prepares the practitioner for deeper practice. It overlaps with the niyamas, which also list these three.

5 marksIshvara

Explain the concept of Ishvara in Patanjala Yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Ishvara is purusha-vishesha, a special Purusha (YS 1.24), untouched by kleshas, karma, the fruits of karma and latent impressions. He is not creator in the theistic sense but the ever-liberated model and teacher of even the ancient teachers (YS 1.26). Designated by Om (YS 1.27), to be repeated and contemplated (YS 1.28). Ishvara-pranidhana is an alternative or supporting means to samadhi (YS 1.23). Ishvara is what Yoga adds to Samkhya.

5 marksPramanas

Explain the three pramanas.

Tap to reveal answer

YS 1.7. Pratyaksha: direct perception; basis of empirical knowledge but can mislead. Anumana: inference, e.g. smoke to fire; used for what cannot be perceived, like Prakriti and Purusha. Agama: reliable testimony of authoritative teachers and texts; valid only when the source is reliable and agrees with reason and experience. Nyaya adds upamana; Advaita adds more.

5 marksLimbs

Explain the first five limbs of Ashtanga Yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

The five bahiranga (external) limbs, YS 2.29:

  1. Yama: ahimsa, satya, asteya, brahmacharya, aparigraha (universal as mahavrata)
  2. Niyama: shaucha, santosha, tapas, svadhyaya, Ishvara-pranidhana
  3. Asana: sthira-sukham asanam (YS 2.46), steady and comfortable, a foundation for meditation
  4. Pranayama: regulation of breath: inhalation, exhalation, retention (YS 2.49)
  5. Pratyahara: withdrawal of the senses; attention turns inward (YS 2.54)
5 marksSamyama

Explain Dharana, Dhyana and Samadhi.

Tap to reveal answer

The three internal (antaranga) limbs, YS 3.1-3. Dharana: fixing the mind on one place or object (breath, mantra, a point). Dhyana: continuous, uninterrupted flow of awareness towards that object. Samadhi: absorption in which meditator, meditation and object are integrated. Together they are samyama (YS 3.4), whose practice gives the vibhutis; the goal remains kaivalya.

5 marksPurusha-Prakriti

Explain Purusha and Prakriti.

Tap to reveal answer

Purusha: pure consciousness, witness, unchanging, non-material, distinct from body, senses, emotions and mind; many in number. Prakriti: primordial nature, unconscious source of the manifest world, operating through sattva (clarity), rajas (activity), tamas (inertia); body, senses and mind belong to it. Bondage is mistaking one for the other; viveka-khyati removes the mistake.

5 marksKaivalya

Explain Kaivalya.

Tap to reveal answer

Kaivalya is the final goal: complete freedom (isolation) of Purusha from identification with Prakriti. It comes when avidya is removed and the conjunction of Seer and seen ceases (YS 2.25); the gunas have no further purpose for Purusha and consciousness stands in its own nature (YS 4.34). It is not destruction of Purusha nor union with a higher entity. Path: yoga practice, purified citta, samadhi, viveka-khyati, kaivalya.
