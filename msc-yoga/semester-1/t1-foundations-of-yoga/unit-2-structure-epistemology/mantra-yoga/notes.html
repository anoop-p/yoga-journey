1AMeaning

TermMeaning
Man + traMan = mind; tra = protection or liberation: a sound that protects or frees the mind
Mantra YogaThe path in which sacred sounds are repeatedly recited and contemplated to concentrate, purify and elevate consciousness
Sound→Repetition→Concentration→Meditation→Realisation
Classroom slogan

Mantra Yoga = Japa + Meaning + Concentration + Meditation. Mantra notes

Mantra and the mind

The ordinary mind goes thought → thought → thought. In mantra yoga it goes mantra → mantra → mantra: one repeated object. Scattered mind → repeated mantra → one-pointed attention → calmness → meditation. Mantra notes

02 · Historical and philosophical background

Vedas to Tantra

The lineage and texts.

2AWhere it comes from

Vedas→Upanishads→Tantric traditions→Mantra Yoga traditions
Know this
  • Vedas: the earliest source of sacred-sound practice. Mantra notes
  • Upanishads (especially the Mandukya): develop the contemplative significance of Om. Mantra notes
  • Later flourished in the Tantric, Shaiva, Shakta and Vaishnava traditions. Mantra notes

Texts: Vedas (foundation); Mandukya Upanishad (Om and states of consciousness); Prashna Upanishad (Om meditation); Yoga Sutra 1.27-29; Hatha Yoga Pradipika (nada); Tantric texts (bija mantras, mantra-shastra, Shakti practices). Mantra notes

03 · Structure

Mantra, Japa and Mala

Types of mantra and forms of repetition.

3AStructure

Mantra→Japa→Concentration→Mental purification→One-pointed mind→Meditation→Higher awareness

3BTypes of mantra

TypeDescriptionExamples
Bija“Seed”: short sacred syllablesOm, Hrim, Shrim, Krim
SagunaDirected to a form or aspect of the DivineOm Namah Shivaya, Om Namo Narayanaya
Nirguna-orientedDirected to the formless AbsoluteSo’ham (“I am That”)

3CJapa and mala

Form of japaMeaning
VachikaSpoken aloud
UpamshuWhispered softly
ManasaRepeated mentally

A progression from outer to inner. A mala (commonly 108 beads) aids regularity, attention and continuity; awareness and concentration matter more than finishing the count. Mantra notes

04 · Method

Om, Japa and Contemplation

Pranava, nada, practice and goal.

4AOm, the pranava

Know this
  • YS 1.27: tasya vachakah pranavah: Om is the designation of Ishvara. Mantra notes Raja notes
  • YS 1.28: taj-japas tad-artha-bhavanam: repeat it and contemplate its meaning. Principle: japa + artha-bhavana. Mantra notes Raja notes
  • Mechanical repetition is not enough; practice needs awareness, meaning, faith, concentration and intention (bhavana). Mantra notes

4BNada: subtle sound

External sound→Internalised sound→Subtle nada→Deep concentration→Absorption

This links Mantra Yoga closely to Laya or Nada Yoga. Mantra notes

4CPractice and goal

Know this
  • Classroom practice: sit comfortably, close eyes, natural breath, chant Om slowly, listen to the sound and vibration, sit silently, observe the mind. Sound → attention → concentration → stillness. Mantra notes
  • Goal: not beautiful chanting, completed counts or worldly results, but mental purification, concentration, meditation, inner awareness and realisation (the final description varies by tradition). Mantra notes

4DLink with Bhakti Yoga

Mantra YogaBhakti Yoga
Sacred sound, repetition, concentrationLove, devotion, surrender

The same mantra (Om Namah Shivaya) can serve both paths, depending on attitude and purpose. Mantra notes

05 · Epistemology

Knowledge Through Sound

Shabda and the guru-shishya line.

5AEpistemology

Not an independent theory of knowledge. It works through shabda: authoritative verbal teaching, the mantra received in the guru-shishya tradition. Mantra notes

Hearing or receiving→Repetition→Concentration→Contemplation of meaning→Inner experience

10 marksMantra yoga

Explain Mantra Yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Man (mind) + tra (protect, free): sacred sounds repeated and contemplated to concentrate, purify and elevate consciousness.

Background. Vedas, Upanishads (Mandukya: Om), Tantric, Shaiva, Shakta, Vaishnava traditions.

Structure. Mantra, japa, concentration, purification, one-pointed mind, meditation. Types: bija, saguna, nirguna-oriented. Japa: vachika, upamshu, manasa. Mala of 108.

Method. Om as pranava (YS 1.27); japa with artha-bhavana (YS 1.28); nada; classroom practice.

Epistemology. Through shabda, the mantra received from a guru.

Metaphysics. Shabda, nada, Om, Shakti, Ishvara or Brahman.

Conclusion. Japa + meaning + concentration + meditation.
