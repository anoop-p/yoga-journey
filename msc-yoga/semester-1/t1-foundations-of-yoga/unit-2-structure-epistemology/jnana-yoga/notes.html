1AMeaning

TermMeaning
JnanaKnowledge, understanding, wisdom: not mere intellectual knowledge
Jnana YogaThe path of knowledge: knowledge of the Self and of ultimate reality

Central question: “Who am I?” Is the real self the body, senses, mind and ego, or something deeper? Jnana notes

Avidya (ignorance)→Wrong identification (“I am the body or mind”)→Attachment and suffering→Self-inquiry→Knowledge of Self→Moksha
Understand it

Jnana Yoga does not add something new; it removes a mistake. You take yourself to be the body, the thoughts, a social identity. The path examines that mistake until it falls away, as knowing the rope removes the snake in the dark. Jnana notes added

02 · Historical and philosophical background

Upanishads to Vedanta

The textual lineage.

2AWhere it comes from

Associated mainly with the Upanishadic tradition and later Vedanta. Source lineage: Vedas → Upanishads → Bhagavad Gita → Brahma Sutras → Vedanta traditions. The Upanishads investigate the nature of the Self, ultimate reality, consciousness, the relation of Atman and Brahman, and liberation. Jnana notes

See the Vedanta page for the prasthanatraya, Advaita and the Mahavakyas.

Link to Shankara

In Advaita Vedanta (Shankara), the Atman is non-dual with Brahman. Jnana Yoga is the practical path of that view. Jnana notes Vedanta notes

04 · Method

Hear, Reflect, Contemplate

Three stages, neti neti and self-inquiry.

4AThree-stage method

Shravana (listening)→Manana (reflecting)→Nididhyasana (contemplating)
StageWhat is done
ShravanaListening to the teaching from a competent teacher
MananaReflecting, reasoning, questioning what was heard
NididhyasanaDeep contemplation: assimilating the teaching

Mnemonic: Hear → Reflect → Contemplate. Jnana notes

4BNeti neti: “not this, not this”

Know this
  • An Upanishadic method of inquiry: Am I the body? Not this. The senses? Not this. Thoughts? Not this. Emotions? Not this. Jnana notes
  • Purpose: not to conclude “I am nothing”, but to inquire into the Self that stays beyond all changing objects of experience. Jnana notes

4CPractical self-inquiry

StepQuestion
1Am I aware of my body? Yes
2Am I aware of my thoughts? Yes
3Am I aware of my emotions? Yes
4What is this awareness itself? Investigate; do not just answer

Jnana Yoga emphasises inquiry, not memorised answers. Jnana notes

05 · Epistemology

How Knowledge of Brahman Comes

Valid means and the special role of Shruti.

5APramanas

PramanaMeaningExample

AnumanaInferenceFire, because smoke is seen
ShabdaReliable verbal testimonyShruti (Upanishadic revelation)
Key point

Brahman is not an ordinary sensory object, so perception alone is not enough. Shabda or Shruti has special epistemological importance for knowledge of Brahman. Jnana notes

Atman and Brahman

In Advaita (Shankara) they are non-dual. The Mahavakyas are not ordinary ego statements like “my personality is God”; they mean the identity of the deepest Self with Brahman. Jnana notes

10 marksJnana yoga

Explain Jnana Yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Jnana Yoga is the path of knowledge of the Self and ultimate reality; the question is “Who am I?” Ignorance leads to wrong identification, attachment and suffering; knowledge removes it.

Background. Upanishadic tradition and Vedanta: Vedas, Upanishads, Gita, Brahma Sutras.

Structure. Sadhana chatushtaya: viveka, vairagya, shat-sampatti (shama, dama, uparati, titiksha, shraddha, samadhana), mumukshutva.

Method. Shravana, manana, nididhyasana; neti neti; self-inquiry.

Epistemology. Pratyaksha, anumana, shabda; Shruti is key for Brahman, which is not a sensory object.

Metaphysics. Atman, Brahman, jagat, avidya, moksha; non-duality in Advaita; mahavakyas.

Conclusion. Knowledge removes avidya and gives moksha.

Jnana notes Wording mine added

5 marksJnana

Tap to reveal answer

5 marksJnana

Explain shravana, manana and nididhyasana.

Tap to reveal answer

Shravana: listening to a competent teacher. Manana: reflecting and reasoning to remove doubts. Nididhyasana: deep contemplation to assimilate the knowledge. Hear, reflect, contemplate.

5 marksJnana

Explain neti neti.

Tap to reveal answer

An Upanishadic method of negating what is not the Self: body, senses, thoughts, emotions. It does not conclude that one is nothing; it leads to inquiry into the Self beyond changing objects.

5 marksJnana

Explain the role of shabda in Jnana Yoga.

Tap to reveal answer

Brahman is not an ordinary sense object, so perception and inference are insufficient. Shabda or Shruti, the Upanishadic teaching given by a competent teacher, is the key source of Brahma-jnana, supported by reasoning and experience.

5 marksJnana

Explain the four mahavakyas.

Tap to reveal answer

Prajnanam Brahma (Aitareya): consciousness is Brahman. Aham Brahmasmi (Brihadaranyaka): I am Brahman. Tat tvam asi (Chandogya): that thou art. Ayam Atma Brahma (Mandukya): this Self is Brahman. They express the identity of the deepest Self with Brahman.
