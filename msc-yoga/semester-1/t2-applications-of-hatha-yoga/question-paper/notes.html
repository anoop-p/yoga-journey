How many types of Samadhi are described in the Gheranda Samhita?

Six types of samadhi (for the seventh step, Nirliptam).
  1. Dhyana samadhi (by Sambhavi mudra)
  2. Nada samadhi (by Khechari mudra)
  3. Rasananda samadhi (by Bhramari)
  4. Laya samadhi (by Yoni mudra)
  5. Bhakti yoga samadhi (meditating on the deity in the heart)
  6. Raja yoga samadhi (by Manomurchha kumbhaka)

Q6 · 2 marks

What precautions or preparations are needed before practising asana?

  • Clean, quiet, undisturbed place, in a matha-like room (HYP 1.12–13).
  • Best time: before sunrise (Brahmamuhurta) or two hours before sunset.
  • Empty stomach, a bath beforehand if possible, loose light clothes, remove watch, spectacles and jewellery.
  • Use a wool blanket, not a spongy mattress; practise under a guru.
  • People with ulcer, tuberculosis, hernia or hypertension: consult a doctor or an expert teacher first.

Q13 · 2 marks

What should be the breathing ratio in the higher stage of pranayama?

Inhalation : retention : exhalation = 1 : 4 : 2.

The GS gives it as 16 : 64 : 32 matras in the middle grade, and the same 1 : 4 : 2 in the highest (20 : 80 : 40) and lowest (12 : 48 : 24) grades. GS notes

The commentary lists the progression 1:1, 1:2, 1:2:2, 1:4:2 and later 1:4:2:3 (retention after exhalation too), and says the ratio should be adjusted to individual capacity. HYP book

Q15 · 5 marks

Explain the process of practising Uttana Mandukasana with its benefits.

Introduction. Uttana mandukasana is the fully raised form of mandukasana (the frog pose): the final position looks like an upright frog. It is the 23rd of the 32 asanas of the Gheranda Samhita. GS notes

Technique with breathing.

  1. Sit in mandukasana: from vajrasana, keep the knees wide apart, the big toes touching, the heels at the sides of the buttocks and the palms on the knees. Keep the spine, neck and head straight.
  2. Cross both arms behind the head and place the palms on the upper part of the opposite shoulder blades (right palm on the left side, left palm on the right), fingers pointing down.
  3. Raise the elbows and bring them close to the back of the head.
  4. Breathe normally and stay as long as it is comfortable. Release the hands and legs and return to vajrasana.

Benefits.

  • Gives all the benefits of mandukasana (improves hip flexibility and helps keep the reproductive organs healthy).
  • Improves circulation in the chest wall and tones the shoulder muscles.
  • Helps keep the spine straight and helps in spinal disorders; relieves pain from cervical spondylosis.

Caution. Avoid with severe knee, hip or shoulder pain.

Marks: introduction (1), technique with breathing (2), benefits (1½), caution (½). The GS gives no breath instruction; “normal breathing” is from the asana chapter slides. GS notes

Q16 · 5 marks

Technique, benefits and precautions of Tribandha.

Introduction. Tribandha is the three locks, moola, uddiyana and jalandhara, used together with breath retention (kumbhaka). Bandha means to bind or hold; muscles are contracted so that prana is gathered and sent up sushumna. class HYP book

  1. Moola bandha (HYP 3.61-63): sit in siddhasana or siddha yoni asana, spine erect, eyes closed. Contract and release the perineum (men) or cervix (women) gently; do not contract the anus (that is ashvini mudra). Breathe normally. Build up from partial to full contraction and hold.
  2. Uddiyana bandha (HYP 3.55-60): exhale fully, then pull the abdomen in and up towards the spine; hold briefly and release before inhaling. Begin standing with hands on the knees; later sit. Empty stomach only.
  3. Jalandhara bandha (HYP 3.70-71): sit comfortably, inhale and hold the breath, lower the chin to the chest (collarbone) and straighten the arms, raising the shoulders. Release by raising the head, then exhale slowly.
  4. Together with kumbhaka: inhale, hold, apply in the order moola, uddiyana, jalandhara; release in the reverse order (jalandhara, uddiyana, moola), then exhale.

Benefits.

  • Moola: heat in the subtle body, awakens kundalini; unites prana with apana (HYP 3.64).
  • Uddiyana: tones the abdominal organs, aids digestion, strengthens the diaphragm; the text claims it keeps the practitioner youthful.
  • Jalandhara: eases throat disorders, improves the voice, and (the commentary says) influences thyroid activity; the nectar is held from falling into the digestive fire (3.72).

Precautions.

  • Learn from a teacher; practise after asana and pranayama.
  • Uddiyana: not with stomach or intestinal ulcers, hernia, high blood pressure, heart disease, glaucoma or raised intracranial pressure.
  • Jalandhara: people with high blood pressure or heart disease only with a teacher.
  • Stop and breathe normally if there is dizziness.

Marks: introduction (1), technique of three bandhas (2), benefits (1), precautions (1). The benefits are the text and commentary claims, not tested findings.

Q17 · 5 marks

Mahamudra according to the Hatha Pradipika.

Introduction. Maha mudra (HYP 3.10-18) is a seated forward bend that joins posture, breath retention, bandha and gaze. It is called the great attitude because the verses say it removes the five kleshas and the causes of death. class HYP book

Technique (HYP 3.10-13 with Muktibodhananda).

  1. Press the left heel into the perineum and stretch the right leg, holding the outstretched foot (big toe) with both hands.
  2. Inhale and apply the throat lock (jalandhara) and hold the breath; the prana rises straight like a struck snake straightening (3.11).
  3. Kundalini straightens; ida and pingala go still as the energy enters sushumna (3.12).
  4. Exhale slowly, never quickly (3.13). Repeat on the other side and with both legs stretched.

Benefits (3.14-18).

  • Removes the five kleshas and the causes of death.
  • All food and even poison is digested like nectar.
  • Eases abdominal disorders, constipation and indigestion; gives great siddhis.

Caution. Avoid with heart problems, high blood pressure, back or knee pain; learn it under a guru and never hold the breath beyond comfort. The verse itself names only the throat lock; moola bandha and shambhavi come from the commentary and the GS. class HYP book

Marks: introduction (1), technique (2), benefits (1), caution (1).

Q19 · 5 marks

Discuss Mitahara according to the Gheranda Samhita.

Introduction. The GS makes mitahara one of three conditions before pranayama (suitable place and time, mitahara, purification of the nadis). Pranayama without mitahara brings many diseases and no success. GS notes

  • Definition: food that is pure, sweet, soft and nourishing, eaten with devotion to God and leaving part of the stomach empty.
  • The rule (GS 5.22): half the stomach with food, a quarter with water, a quarter empty for the movement of air.
  • Pathya: red rice, barley flour, wheat bread, green gram and other husk-free pulses; snake gourd, plantain, brinjal, leafy vegetables; butter, ghee, milk, sugar, jaggery, coconut, pomegranate, dates.
  • Apathya: bitter, sour, salty, pungent and dry food; curd, buttermilk, liquor, garlic, asafoetida; hard, stale, very hot or very cold food.
  • Habits to avoid: too much travel, bad company, sitting near fire, early-morning bath, fasting, eating only once a day or not at all.
  • Beginners: milk and ghee, and food twice a day, at noon and evening.

Comparison with HYP. Both texts give the half-quarter-quarter stomach rule and similar pathya and apathya lists (HYP 1.60–66 in the PDF). HYP book

Marks: introduction and definition (1), the rule (1), pathya (1), apathya (1), habits (1). GS notes

Discuss the concept of yogic diet, Pathya and Apathya, and its importance for the hatha yogi.

Introduction. Food is the fuel of practice. Swatmarama makes mitahara one of the first conditions of hatha yoga: the yogi eats for health and practice, not for taste or hunger alone.

  • Brahmacharya, mitahara, renunciation and devotion to yoga bring siddhi in about a year (1.59 in the PDF).

Q21 · 10 marks

Name all the Dhyanas described in the Gheranda Samhita and discuss the concept behind practising them in hatha yoga.

Introduction. The Gheranda Samhita teaches ghatastha yoga in seven steps (saptasadhana). The sixth step, pratyaksha (direct perception), is gained through dhyana. GS notes

1The three dhyanas

  1. Sthula dhyana (gross): meditation on a form or symbol, such as the chosen deity seated in a jewelled island in an ocean of nectar in the heart.
  2. Tejo (jyotir) dhyana (luminous): meditation on the kundalini at the mooladhara as a flame, then on that flame at the brow centre as a ray of light that is the pranava.
  3. Sukshma dhyana (subtle): perceiving the vibrations when the kundalini joins the Atman in brahmarandhra, with shambhavi mudra.

Tejo dhyana is said to be a hundred times better than sthula dhyana, and sukshma dhyana a hundred thousand times better than tejo. GS notes

2Concept behind them in hatha yoga

M48 · 5 marksBandha

Technique. Sit in siddhasana or padmasana, inhale slowly and hold the breath, lower the chin to the chest (collarbone), straighten the arms and raise the shoulders; hold comfortably, raise the head, exhale slowly. Five rounds, then five with external retention.

M49 · 5 marksBandha

Describe uddiyana bandha: technique, benefits and cautions.

Tap to reveal answer

Technique. Stand with feet half a metre apart, hands above the knees. Inhale, exhale fully, apply jalandhara, pull the abdomen in and up towards the spine, hold a few seconds, release the abdomen, then the throat, then inhale. Three rounds, up to ten.

Benefits. The text calls it the lion that conquers the elephant of death (3.57); the commentary says it tones the abdominal organs, aids digestion, massages the heart and strengthens the diaphragm.

Cautions. Empty stomach; not with ulcers, hernia, high blood pressure, heart disease, glaucoma or raised intracranial pressure. HYP book

Technique. Sit in siddhasana, hands on knees. Contract and release the perineum (men) or cervix (women), up to twenty times; then hold the contraction while breathing normally; then build up gradually from partial to full. There should be no anal contraction (that is ashvini mudra).

Benefits. HYP 3.61-64: raises the vital energy, unites prana with apana and nada with bindu; the commentary says it produces heat that awakens kundalini. HYP book
