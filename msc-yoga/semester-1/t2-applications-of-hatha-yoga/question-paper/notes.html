Q1 · 2 marks

How many types of Samadhi are described in the Gheranda Samhita?

Six types of samadhi (for the seventh step, Nirliptam).
  1. Dhyana samadhi (by Sambhavi mudra)
  2. Nada samadhi (by Khechari mudra)
  3. Rasananda samadhi (by Bhramari)
  4. Laya samadhi (by Yoni mudra)
  5. Bhakti yoga samadhi (meditating on the deity in the heart)
  6. Raja yoga samadhi (by Manomurchha kumbhaka)

Write the number and the six names for full marks. GS notes

What is the meaning of Pathya in yoga?

Pathya is suitable, wholesome food (and conduct) that helps yoga practice; the opposite is apathya.

Q6 · 2 marks

What precautions or preparations are needed before practising asana?

  • Clean, quiet, undisturbed place, in a matha-like room (HYP 1.12–13).
  • Best time: before sunrise (Brahmamuhurta) or two hours before sunset.
  • Empty stomach, a bath beforehand if possible, loose light clothes, remove watch, spectacles and jewellery.
  • Use a wool blanket, not a spongy mattress; practise under a guru.
  • People with ulcer, tuberculosis, hernia or hypertension: consult a doctor or an expert teacher first.

Any four points are enough for 2 marks. GS notes

Q20 · 10 marks

Discuss the concept of yogic diet, Pathya and Apathya, and its importance for the hatha yogi.

Introduction. Food is the fuel of practice. Swatmarama makes mitahara one of the first conditions of hatha yoga: the yogi eats for health and practice, not for taste or hunger alone.

1Mitahara (the concept)

Sloka · verse 1.60 (book 1.58)सुस्निग्ध-मधुराहारश्चतुर्थांश-विवर्जितः ।
भुज्यते शिव-सम्प्रीत्यै मिताहारः स उच्यते ॥

Food that is sweet, soft and nourishing (snigdha, madhura), eaten with one fourth of the stomach left empty, as an offering to Shiva. The stomach is half food, a quarter water, a quarter air. No overeating and no under-eating; no irregular snacking. class HYP book

2Pathya (suitable)

Sloka · verse 1.65 (book 1.63)गोधूम-शालि-यव-षाष्टिक-शोभनान्नं
क्षीराज्य-खण्ड-नवनीत-सिद्धा-मधूनि ।
शुण्ठी-पटोल-फलादिक-पञ्च-शाकं
मुद्गादि-दिव्यमुदकं च यमीन्द्र-पथ्यम् ॥
Sloka · verse 1.66 (book 1.64)पुष्टं सुमधुरं स्निग्धं गव्यं धातु-प्रपोषणम् ।
मनोभिलषितं योग्यं योगी भोजनमाचरेत् ॥
  • Wheat, rice, barley, shali and shashtika rice; milk, ghee, sugar, butter, honey.
  • Dried ginger, the five vegetables, fruits, mung beans; pure water.
  • Food that is sweet, nourishing, pleasant and in harmony with the body and the time of day.

3Apathya (unsuitable)

Sloka · verse 1.61 (book 1.59)कट्वाम्ल-तीक्ष्ण-लवणोष्ण-हरीत-शाक-
सौवीर-तैल-तिल-सर्षप-मद्य-मत्स्यान् ।
आजादि-मांस-दधि-तक्र-कुलत्थकोल-
पिण्याक-हिङ्गु-लशुनाद्यमपथ्यमाहुः ॥
Sloka · verse 1.62 (book 1.60)भोजनमहितं विद्यात्पुनरस्योष्णी-कृतं रूक्षम् ।
अतिलवणमम्ल-युक्तं कदशन-शाकोत्कं वर्ज्यम् ॥
  • Bitter, sour, pungent, salty, heating foods; sour gruel, oil, sesame, mustard.
  • Alcohol, fish, meat; curds and buttermilk, horse gram; asafoetida and garlic.
  • Reheated, dry, stale food; too much salt or acid; too many vegetables eaten as a mixed meal.
Sloka · verse 1.64 (book 1.62)वर्जयेद्दुर्जन-प्रान्तं वह्नि-स्त्री-पथि-सेवनम् ।
प्रातः-स्नानोपवासादि काय-क्लेश-विधिं तथा ॥

Habits to avoid (Gorakhnath): standing near fire, bad company and women, cold baths and fasting in the early morning, any task that hurts the body. HYP book

4Importance for the hatha yogi

  • Mitahara is the first yama in the HYP list (1.17) and, with ahimsa, the chief of the yamas and niyamas (1.40). Overeating is the first cause of failure (1.15).
  • Brahmacharya, mitahara, renunciation and devotion to yoga bring siddhi in about a year (1.59 in the PDF).
  • Light, sattwic food keeps the body lean, the digestive fire active and the mind clear, which are signs of hatha siddhi (2.78). added
  • Pranayama can begin only after steady asana and moderate diet (2.1).
  • Anyone, young, old, sick or weak, succeeds if practice is regular (1.67).

Conclusion. Pathya and apathya are guidelines, not a menu: the test is whether food leaves the body light and the mind calm for practice.

Q21 · 10 marks

Name all the Dhyanas described in the Gheranda Samhita and discuss the concept behind practising them in hatha yoga.

Introduction. The Gheranda Samhita teaches ghatastha yoga in seven steps (saptasadhana). The sixth step, pratyaksha (direct perception), is gained through dhyana. GS notes

1The three dhyanas

  1. Sthula dhyana (gross): meditation on a form or symbol, such as the chosen deity seated in a jewelled island in an ocean of nectar in the heart.
  2. Tejo (jyotir) dhyana (luminous): meditation on the kundalini at the mooladhara as a flame, then on that flame at the brow centre as a ray of light that is the pranava.
  3. Sukshma dhyana (subtle): perceiving the vibrations when the kundalini joins the Atman in brahmarandhra, with shambhavi mudra.

Tejo dhyana is said to be a hundred times better than sthula dhyana, and sukshma dhyana a hundred thousand times better than tejo. GS notes

2Concept behind them in hatha yoga

  • Order of the seven steps: purification (shatkarma), strength (asana), steadiness (mudra), composure (pratyahara), lightness (pranayama), direct perception (dhyana) and freedom (samadhi). Dhyana comes only after the body and breath have been prepared. GS notes
  • Gross to subtle: the mind first holds a form, then light, then subtle sound or vibration, becoming less dependent on the senses. added
  • Purpose: hatha yoga disciplines the body and breath so that the mind can hold one object and move on to samadhi (hatha for the sake of raja yoga). GS notes

Conclusion. The dhyanas link the body-based practices of hatha yoga to the inner work of raja yoga.

Marks: introduction (1), three dhyanas (5), concept (3), conclusion (1). The first two bullets in the concept list: the gross-to-subtle reading is reasoned (tagged added); confirm with your teacher. GS notes added

Q6 · 2 marks

What is Kevala Kumbhaka?

Sloka · verse 2.71प्राणायामस्त्रिधा प्रोक्तो रेच-पूरक-कुम्भकैः ।

Q12 · 10 marks

Discuss the Hatha Siddhi Lakshana and the role of Mitahara in attaining Hatha Siddhi.

Introduction. Hatha siddhi means the perfection of hatha yoga: the balance of ida and pingala and readiness for raja yoga. Swatmarama gives signs by which it is known, and makes moderate diet a basic condition.

AHatha Siddhi Lakshana (HYP 2.78)

Sloka · verse 2.78वपुः कृशत्वं वदने प्रसन्नता
नाद-स्फुटत्वं नयने सुनिर्मले ।
अरोगता बिन्दु-जयोऽग्नि-दीपनं
नाडी-विशुद्धिर्हठ-सिद्धि-लक्षणम् ॥
HYP 2.78Perfection of hatha yoga is achieved when there is leanness of the body, tranquil countenance, manifestation of the inner sound, clear eyes, diseaselessness, control of bindu, active digestive fire and purification of the nadis.
  1. Leanness of the body.
  2. Tranquil, cheerful face.
  3. Manifestation of the inner sound (nada).
  4. Clear, bright eyes.
  5. Freedom from disease.
  6. Control of bindu.
  7. Active digestive fire.
  8. Purified nadis.

BRole of mitahara

  • It defines the diet: agreeable, sweet food with a quarter of the stomach empty (1.58); no overloading, so digestion is light and prana is saved.
  • It supports the signs: moderate food keeps the body lean and the digestive fire active and the nadis clear, three of the eight signs.
  • It is a condition of success: a brahmachari taking moderate, pure food, regular and detached, becomes siddha after a year (1.57); siddhasana with moderate diet gives siddhi (1.40).
  • It is the base of self-control: moderate diet is called the most important of the yamas, as siddhasana is of the asanas (1.38 commentary); the HYP lists mitahara as its ninth yama.
  • It comes before pranayama: only when the body is regulated by asana and moderate diet should pranayama begin (2.1).
  • Overeating is the first cause of failure (1.15).

Conclusion. The eight signs show the result; mitahara is among the conditions that make it possible, together with asana, shatkarma, pranayama and the guru’s guidance.

HYP 2.54, 2.57–58Seetkari: draw the breath in through the mouth with a hissing sound, exhale through the nose. Sheetali: inhale through the tongue, retain, exhale through the nostrils; it cures enlarged stomach or spleen, fever, excess bile, hunger and thirst, and counteracts poisons.

M27 · 10 marksParampara

Explain the Hatha Parampara, the aim of hatha yoga and the instructions for a hatha yogi.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Hatha yoga is a stairway to raja yoga (HYP 1.1). Ha is the sun and pingala; tha is the moon and ida; balancing them is hatha yoga.

AParampara

Adinath (Shiva) → Matsyendranath → Gorakhnath → Swatmarama, the author of the HYP. class

BAim

  • To prepare the body, breath and mind for raja yoga and samadhi (1.67, 2.76).
  • The eight siddhis (anima, laghima, mahima, garima, prapti, prakamya, vashitva, ishitva) are listed in your notes. class

CInstructions for the hatha yogi

  • Live in a matha (1.12–13); moderate diet (mitahara); practise under a guru; best time before sunrise; empty stomach; wool blanket and loose clothes.
  • Sequence: shatkarma if needed, asana, pranayama with mudra and bandha, samadhi.
  • Avoid the six causes of failure; cultivate the six of success.

Conclusion. Hatha yoga works only through regular practice, not through reading or talking (1.65–66).
