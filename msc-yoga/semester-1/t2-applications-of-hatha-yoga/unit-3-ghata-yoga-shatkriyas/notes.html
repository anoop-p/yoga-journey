Understand it · why the Gheranda Samhita is “Ghata yoga” classGS slides
  • Ghata = a clay pot, and also the body. Ghatastha yoga = yoga through the body. The text says the body is like an unbaked clay pot dropped in water: it will dissolve. So bake it in the fire of yoga first (the seven sadhanas).
  • The text is a dialogue between the teacher Gheranda and his student Chandakapali. It follows the law of karma: birth and death turn like a water wheel (gatiyantra) driven by karma.
  • It omits yama and niyama and starts with shatkarma, because the first need is a clean body. The class notes add that it follows a Vaishnava view.
  • Compare the HYP: hatha vidya is a ladder to raja yoga (four chapters). The GS is a seven-step path through the body to samadhi (seven chapters).
Know this · the seven chapters, in order GS slidesclass
  • 1 Shatkarma (Pradhamopadesa) · 2 Asana · 3 Mudra (with bandhas and dharanas) · 4 Pratyahara · 5 Pranayama · 6 Dhyana · 7 Samadhi.
  • Count of practices: shatkarma 21 + asana 32 + mudra 25 (16 mudras + 4 bandhas + 5 dharanas) + pratyahara 5 + pranayama 10 + dhyana 3 + samadhi 6 = 102.
  • Your class note on 7 Oct writes the same list as “Saptanga yoga — Gheranda: shatkarma, asana, mudra, pratyahara, pranayama, dhyana, samadhi”.
Memory aid · seven in one sentence added

S-A-M-P-P-D-S: Shatkarma, Asana, Mudra, Pratyahara, Pranayama, Dhyana, Samadhi. Each step builds on the one before: clean → firm → steady → calm → light → seeing → free.

Source note. The handout writes that the text is “believed to be written around 15 CE”, and the slides write “around 17 CE”. Both appear to mean the 15th or 17th century CE. Most modern sources say the 17th century; if your teacher gives a date, use that one. The Hata and Gheranda handout also says the 3rd sadhana “sthirata” while the slides say “sthiryam”; both mean steadiness.

Understand it · each limb solves one problem added
  • Dirt in the body → shatkarma cleans it. Weakness → asana strengthens. Restlessness → mudras, bandhas and dharanas steady. Distraction by the senses → pratyahara calms. Heaviness → mitahara and pranayama lighten. Seeing only the outside → dhyana gives direct inner seeing. Bondage → samadhi frees.
  • This is why each limb comes in this order: you cannot hold a steady posture in a dirty body, and you cannot meditate while the senses run outward.

Understand it · rules for all shatkarma GS slides
  • Learn only under a guru; never from a book. Done wrongly they harm the body.
  • Empty stomach, early morning, clean place. Some practices not in extreme winter or summer.
  • The aim of dhauti: make the body (ghata) clean (ghatam kurvanti nirmalam).
  • They clean the mouth, teeth, tongue, upper palate, ear canals, nasal passages, food pipe, stomach, intestines, rectum and anus, using water, air, cloth and friction.
  • The text says they remove the faults of wind (vata), bile (pitta) and phlegm (kapha).

Source note. The GS list has 21 kinds; the HYP (2.22) lists six practices (dhauti, basti, neti, nauli, trataka, kapalabhati) with fewer variations. Gheranda describes only sutra neti, because jala neti is considered a modified form of vyutkrama kapalabhati.

Dhauti: the Thirteen Cleansing Practices

Understand it · 4 + 5 + 3 + 1 = 13 GS slides
  • Antar dhauti (inner washing): vatasara, varisara, vahnisara, bahiskrita.
  • Danta dhauti (head and neck): dantamoola, jihvamoola, karna randhra (right and left ear = 2) and kapala randhra. It is not only teeth: it cleans the organs above the neck.
  • Hrid dhauti (heart or chest): danda, vamana, vastra (vaso).
  • Moola shodhana: cleaning the rectum and anus.
PracticeWhat it isBenefit (GS) and caution
Vatasara (air)Sit, purse the lips like a crow’s beak (kaki mudra), drink air, fill the stomach, hold, move the abdomen and expel the air through the anus.Purifies the body, destroys disease, raises digestive fire. Not for heart disease or high blood pressure while holding the breath.
Varisara (water; shankhaprakshalana)Drink about 10 glasses of lukewarm saline water in rounds with set asanas (tadasana, tiryaka tadasana, kati chakrasana, tiryaka bhujangasana, udarakarshanasana) and nauli until the water leaves with the stool.Cleans the whole alimentary canal. Not in pregnancy or hypertension. Avoid salt for a month; eat khichadi. Therapeutic only, under a teacher.
Vahnisara (fire; agnisara)After full exhalation, hold the breath outside, jalandhara, and pump the abdomen to the spine, as many times as comfortable (up to 50 or more).Raises digestive fire; success in yoga. Not in pregnancy, hypertension, hernia or abdominal disease.
BahiskritaDraw in air by kaki mudra, hold it in the stomach for a long time and force it down. The other method uses the rectum in navel-deep water.Very difficult and dangerous; the text says even gods find it hard. For understanding only, never attempt.
DantamoolaRub the root of the teeth with khadira (catechu) powder or clean earth.Preserves the teeth; a great dhauti for yogis. Kapha and pitta reducer.
JihvamoolaJoin the three middle fingers, pass them to the root of the tongue and remove mucus. Second method: draw out the tongue with a clean cloth and rub with fresh butter at sunrise and sunset.Elongates the tongue; the text links this to the destruction of old age, death and disease.
Karna randhraRub the ear canals gently with the tip of the index finger (right then left).Mystical sounds are heard; better hearing of subtle sounds; safer than ear buds.
Kapala randhraRub the roof of the mouth (upper back palate) with the right thumb three times daily: waking, after meals, end of day.Reduces kapha; purifies the nadis; clear or “divine” vision.
DandaPass a stalk of plantain, turmeric or soft cane into the food pipe and withdraw it. (A rubber tube is the modern form, popularised by Kuvalayananda.)Expels mucus and bile; massages the food pipe. Also called kunjal kriya in some books. Only under a teacher.
VamanaDrink lukewarm saline water to the throat and vomit it out, early morning, empty stomach.Prevents kapha and pitta imbalance.
Vastra (vasodhauti)Swallow a wet strip of thin cotton cloth (about 4 fingers wide, 6.5 m long) in squatting, keeping a foot outside; do nauli or agnisara; take out slowly. Never keep the cloth in for more than five minutes.The text says it cures intestinal tumours, fever, spleen disease, leprosy and kapha and pitta faults; health and strength grow daily. Not for hypertension, heart disease or weakness.
Moola shodhanaClean the anal sphincter and rectum with a turmeric stem, or the middle finger with ghee and water, again and again.Smooth working of apana; cures constipation, indigestion and dyspepsia; raises digestive fire.

The details of preparation and aftercare for varisara and vastra are from the slides and are modern teaching practice. For the GS verse numbers (I-17 to I-39), see the slides; not printed here for every practice.

ABasti (colon cleansing)

Kinds GS slides

  • Jala basti (water): stand navel-deep in water in utkatasana, insert a lubricated tube of about 1 cm × 15 cm into the rectum; contract the anal sphincter to draw water in, release to expel. Repeat with ashvini mudra.
  • Sthala (shushka) basti (dry or on land): lie on the back, raise the hips as in viparita karani, and contract and release the anal sphincter (ashvini mudra) to draw in air.

Benefits GS slides

  • Cures prameha, udavarta (digestive disorders) and krura vayu (wind disorders); the body becomes pure and as handsome as Kamadeva (jala basti).
  • Sthala: constipation never occurs; raises digestive fire; cures wind disorders and joint pain.
  • A modern note: both are advanced and need a teacher.

BSutra neti

Understand it GS slides
  • Sutra = thread. Pass a waxed cotton thread (about 4 mm × 45–50 cm) or a soft rubber catheter through the nose to the throat, draw it out through the mouth, hold both ends and move it gently back and forth 5–10 times, then the other nostril.
  • Benefits in the GS: khechari siddhi, destroys disorders of phlegm and gives clear (“divine”) sight.
  • Not for chronic nose bleeding or nasal disease. No force; the inside of the nose is delicate.

Know this · nauli in short GS slides
  • Standing, knees bent, palms on thighs, exhale fully, hold the breath outside, jalandhara and uddiyana, then isolate the muscles. Warm up with agnisara. Stage 4 only after 1–3 are mastered.
  • GS benefit: destroys all diseases and increases bodily fire. Modern notes: tones the abdomen; stimulates manipura.
  • Not for heart disease, high blood pressure, hernia, gallstones, acute ulcer, pregnancy or after abdominal surgery.

DTrataka

Understand it GS slides
  • Gaze steadily at a small point without blinking until tears come; then close the eyes and see the after-image in the space in front of the closed eyes (chidakasha). Repeat about 5 times, then palming.
  • Objects: a ghee or oil lamp flame at eye level an arm’s length away (jyoti trataka), also crystal, sun, moon or yantra.
  • GS benefits: attains shambhavi mudra, cures eye diseases, gives divine vision. Slides add: strengthens concentration; a bridge between hatha and raja yoga.
  • Not for epilepsy, migraine, glaucoma or serious mental disorders.

Siddhasana (verse 1) GS notes

  • Verse: press one heel at the perineum and the other above the genitals, chin on the chest, motionless, senses controlled, gaze steady at the eyebrow centre. It breaks open the door to liberation.
  • Technique: sit in dandasana; left heel at the perineum; right heel above the genitals so that the two heels lie one over the other; knees on the floor; back, neck and head straight; chin lock; chin mudra; eyes closed, relax.
  • Breath: retain after inhaling with jalandhara and moola; release moola, then jalandhara, then exhale long.
  • Benefits: steadies the spine for meditation; activates mooladhara and ajna; sits long for pranayama. Avoid with severe knee or ankle pain.

Padmasana (verse 2) GS notes

  • Verse: right foot on the left thigh and left on the right, cross the hands behind and hold the toes, chin on chest, gaze at the nose tip. It destroys all diseases. (The GS describes the bound form, baddha padmasana.)
  • Technique: both soles upturned, heels near the lower abdomen; knees on the floor; arms behind to hold the big toes; jalandhara; gaze at the nose tip (agochari mudra).
  • Benefits: calms the mind; corrects posture; stretches respiratory muscles; strengthens the thigh and calf; improves hip, knee and ankle flexibility. Avoid with severe knee or shoulder issues.

Bhadrasana (verse 3) GS notes

  • Verse: ankles in reverse beneath the genitals, hands crossed behind to hold the toes, jalandhara, gaze at the nose tip. Destroys all diseases.
  • Technique: from vajrasana, spread the knees; ankles under the genitals, toes back; arms behind to hold the big toes; jalandhara; nasagra drishti.
  • Benefits: flexibility of knees and ankles; healthy urogenital muscles; stimulates mooladhara.

Simhasana (verse 7) GS notes

  • Verse: ankles crossed beneath the genitals, knees on the ground, palms on the knees with fingers spread, mouth wide open, tongue stretched, jalandhara, gaze at the nose tip. Destroys all diseases.
  • Technique: from vajrasana cross the ankles and sit on them; palms like lion’s paws; open the mouth wide and stretch the tongue out.
  • Benefits: good for ear, nose and throat problems; energises; stimulates the salivary glands; relaxes face and neck muscles. Not for severe knee and ankle trouble.

Mayurasana (verses 30–31) GS notes

  • Verse: press the ground with both palms, place the sides of the navel on the elbows, raise the body straight like a staff.
  • Benefits (verse): burns even large amounts of food eaten wrongly; kindles the digestive fire; digests deadly poison (kalakuta); quickly cures disease including abdominal disorders and fever.
  • Caution: high blood pressure, abdominal surgery, ulcer.

Vajrasana and Muktasana GS notes

  • Vajrasana: legs firm like a thunderbolt, feet on both sides of the anus; gives success to yogis. Sit between the heels; knees together; hands on the knees; can be done after meals for digestion.
  • Muktasana: left ankle under the anus, right ankle on top; head, neck and body straight; gives success. A variant of siddhasana.
  • Both avoid severe knee pain.
Know this · others worth a line GS notes
  • Mritasana (shavasana): lie flat like a corpse; removes tiredness and calms the mind. Muscles at minimum tension; body awareness; relaxation.
  • Guptasana: feet tucked between thigh and calf, anus resting on the soles; attention on mooladhara; helps apana control; ideal for mudras and bandhas.
  • Matsyasana: padmasana lying down with elbows around the head; destroys disease. Matsyendrasana: spinal twist; the notes say it kindles the fire, wakes kundalini and massages the liver, spleen and pancreas.
  • Gorakshasana: soles up between knees and thighs, hands over ankles, jalandhara, gaze at nose tip; success to yogis.
  • Bhujangasana: prone cobra; heat in the body; destroys diseases; wakes kundalini.

Source note. The benefits above are as printed in your GS asana notes. The verse itself gives a benefit for only a few asanas; the rest are modern additions in the notes (marked as such in the file). For exam answers, give the verse benefit first, then add one modern benefit.
