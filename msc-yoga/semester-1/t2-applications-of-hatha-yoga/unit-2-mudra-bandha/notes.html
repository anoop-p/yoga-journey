Understand it · what a mudra and a bandha are classHYP book
  • Mudra = seal, gesture or attitude. A mudra is a body position that channels the energy made by asana and pranayama into particular centres and arouses a particular state of mind (Muktibodhananda on 3.5).
  • Bandha = lock. A bandha is a muscular contraction that holds prana in one region: throat (jalandhara), abdomen (uddiyana), perineum (moola).
  • In the old tantric texts all of these were called mudras. Hatha yoga separated the words: jalandhara, uddiyana and moola became bandhas, but the three together become a mudra (maha mudra, the “great attitude”).
  • So the HYP’s list of ten holds six mudras and four bandhas. Some texts call the same practice a mudra and others a bandha, so a difference in name between books is normal.
  • Why they matter: sloka 3.5 says the sleeping kundalini at the door of Brahma must be roused with all effort by practising mudra. Asana and pranayama give “minor” benefits; mudra and bandha give the “major” siddhis (3.8).
verse 3.6–7महामुद्रा महाबन्धो महावेधश्च खेचरी ।
उड्डीयानं मूलबन्धश्च बन्धो जालन्धराभिधः ॥
करणी विपरीताख्या वज्रोली शक्तिचालनम् ।
इदं हि मुद्रादशकं जरामरणनाशनम् ॥

Maha mudra, maha bandha, maha vedha, khechari, uddiyana, moola bandha and jalandhara bandha; viparita karani, vajroli and shakti chalana. These ten mudras destroy old age and death.

Memory aid · ten as 3 + 1 + 3 + 3 added

Three Mahas (mudra, bandha, vedha) → Khechari → three locks (Uddiyana, Moola, Jalandhara) → the last three (Viparita karani, Vajroli, Shakti chalana). That is 3+1+3+3 = 10, in the order of the verse.

Know this · three rules the text repeats HYP 3.2, 3.8, 3.9
  • Guru: kundalini is awakened by the guru’s grace (3.2). Muktibodhananda warns that raising it without a guru can be disturbing and hazardous.
  • Siddhis are a by-product: Adinath says the ten give the eight divine powers (3.8) but Patanjali says siddhis are obstacles to samadhi, so they are not the aim.
  • Secrecy: guard them like a box of jewels (3.9). This is why the text gives only outlines and the details come from the teacher.

02 · Maha Trio

Maha Mudra, Maha Bandha and Maha Vedha

HYP 3.10–31. Three practices that are taught as one sequence: gather the energy, lock it in, then drive it into sushumna.

1
sequence: gather, lock, channel
Understand it · the three work as one sequence HYP book
  • HYP 3.25 says maha mudra and maha bandha are fruitless without maha vedha, as a beautiful woman is nothing without a husband.
  • Muktibodhananda’s picture: maha mudra and maha bandha fill the tank and start the engine; maha vedha is releasing the brake and steering. Without it, the energy may fall back into ida, pingala or mooladhara.
  • So the order is maha mudra → maha bandha → maha vedha, and 3.30–31 calls the three together a great secret that destroys old age and death and raises the digestive fire.

AMaha mudra: the great attitude

verse 3.10पादमूलेन वामेन योनिं सम्पीड्य दक्षिणाम् ।
प्रसारितं पदं कृत्वा कराभ्यां धारयेद्दृढम् ॥

Press the perineum with the left heel, stretch the right leg and hold the foot firmly with both hands.

verse 3.11कण्ठे बन्धं समारोप्य धारयेद्वायुमूर्ध्वतः ।
यथा दण्डहतः सर्पो दण्डाकारः प्रजायते ॥

Apply the throat lock and hold the breath: prana rises straight like a snake struck with a stick.

Technique (Muktibodhananda) HYP book

  • Sit with the right leg stretched, left heel at the perineum. Breathing out, lean forward and hold the big toe, head erect, back straight.
  • Khechari mudra, inhale slowly, tilt head back slightly, shambhavi (gaze at the eyebrow centre).
  • Hold the breath, apply moola bandha, rotate awareness through ajna, vishuddhi, mooladhara.
  • Release moola, lower the head, exhale slowly. Three rounds on each side, then three with both legs stretched.

Benefits and cautions HYP 3.14–18

  • Slow exhale only, never fast (3.13). The kundalini “straightens” and ida and pingala go still as energy enters sushumna.
  • Removes the five kleshas and the causes of death (3.14). Foods and even poisons are digested like nectar (3.16).
  • Eases consumption, leprosy, constipation, enlarged spleen and indigestion (3.17).
  • Avoid with heart problems, high blood pressure, back or knee pain; learn from a guru (class notes).

BMaha bandha: the great lock

verse 3.19–20पार्ष्णिं वामस्य पादस्य योनिस्थाने नियोजयेत् ।
वामोरुपरि संस्थाप्य दक्षिणं चरणं तथा ॥
पूरयित्वा ततो वायुं हृदये चिबुकं दृढम् ।
निष्पीड्य वायुमाकुञ्च्य मनो मध्ये नियोजयेत् ॥

Left heel at the perineum, right foot on the left thigh; breathe in, chin firmly to the chest, contract the lower lock, and fix the mind at the centre.

Technique HYP book

  • Hatha form (verses): half padmasana, inhale through the left nostril, hold, jalandhara, moola, shambhavi. Release in the reverse order (shambhavi, moola, jalandhara) and exhale through the right. Then the other side.
  • Common form today: siddhasana, exhale fully, hold the breath outside, apply jalandhara, uddiyana and moola; rotate awareness mooladhara → manipura → vishuddhi. Release moola, uddiyana, then jalandhara.
  • Three rounds at first, up to nine in the second form after months.

Benefits (3.23–24) HYP book

  • Stops the upward leak of energy in all the nadis; gives great siddhis.
  • Frees from the bonds of death; ida, pingala and sushumna meet at ajna; the mind reaches “Kedara”, the seat of Shiva.
  • Muktibodhananda: shakti is locked in the torso and recirculates upward; the pineal and the whole endocrine system are regulated.
  • Needs the three bandhas perfected first; not for high or low blood pressure, hernia or ulcer.

CMaha vedha mudra: the great piercer

verse 3.27समहस्तयुगो भूमौ स्फिचौ सन्ताडयेच्छनैः ।
पुटद्वयमतिक्रम्य वायुः स्फुरति मध्यगः ॥

With palms on the floor, gently beat the buttocks on the ground; the prana leaves the two side channels and moves in the middle one.

Technique HYP book

  • Sit in padmasana (if it is not perfected you cannot do this properly). Palms on the floor beside the thighs, fingers forward.
  • Inhale slowly, hold, jalandhara. Lift on straight arms and gently beat the buttocks on the ground three to seven times, awareness at mooladhara.
  • Rest, release the throat lock, exhale slowly. Begin with three rounds; up to five after some months, no more.
  • The beating action is called tadan kriya. Land buttocks and thighs together.

Benefits and cautions HYP 3.26–29

  • Prana leaves ida and pingala and enters sushumna; the three unite and a death-like stillness comes (3.28).
  • Great perfections; keeps away wrinkles, grey hair and the trembling of old age (3.29).
  • Class notes add: removes fear, strengthens the core, and “improves immune power” (no evidence given).
  • Avoid with heart problems, high blood pressure, back or knee pain.

03 · Khechari

Khechari Mudra and Why It Matters

The longest practice in HYP chapter 3, and the one the text calls unequalled. Learn it as a word, two forms, benefits and a link to samadhi.

3.32–54
verses in chapter 3
2
forms: hatha and raja
4.38–53
more in chapter 4
1.43
“no mudra equals khechari”
Understand it · why it is called khechari HYP 3.41
  • Kha = space (also Brahman); chari = moving. Verse 3.41: the mind moves in kha because the tongue moves in kha. Muktibodhananda: the attitude of dwelling in supreme consciousness; it is also called nabho mudra.
  • It is the longest section of chapter 3 because it joins three ideas: the tongue turned back into the cavity above the palate, the gaze at the eyebrow centre, and keeping the bindu (the nectar of the moon) from falling into the fire at the navel.
  • Its place in the system: the same nectar that jalandhara and viparita karani protect is held here at its source. That is why verse 3.54 calls it “the one mudra” of the single state (manonmani).
verse 3.32कपालकुहरे जिह्वा प्रविष्टा विपरीतगा ।
भ्रुवोरन्तर्गता दृष्टिर्मुद्रा भवति खेचरी ॥

When the tongue turns back into the cavity of the skull and the gaze is fixed between the eyebrows, it is khechari mudra.

FormWhat it isWho
Hatha form (verses 3.33–37)“Milking” the tongue and gradually cutting the frenum over six months so that the tongue reaches the eyebrow centre.Only from a guru, to a prepared student, over many years. Describe as history; do not attempt.
Raja formTurn the tongue back so its under surface touches the soft palate; the tip goes towards the nasal opening behind the throat if it can. Eyes closed, gaze at the eyebrow centre.Simple; anyone can try gently. Used with japa, meditation and ujjayi.
Know this · benefits listed in the verses HYP 3.38–54
  • A moment with the tongue raised frees from poison, disease, death and old age (3.38). No fainting, hunger, thirst, sleep or laziness (3.39).
  • One who knows khechari is not troubled by disease, not stained by karma, not bound by time (3.40).
  • The bindu does not fall even when the yogi is with a woman (3.42–43); steady sipping of the moon’s nectar conquers death in a fortnight (3.44); a snake bite does not spread the poison (3.45).
  • The embodied soul does not leave a body full of the moon’s nectar, as a lamp does not leave its oil and wick (3.46).
  • Chapter 4: khechari and shambhavi both give ecstasy (4.38); it is perfected when sushumna flows and prana rests in the void (4.43–44); it awakens vyoma chakra (4.45); it leads to unmani (4.47) and yoga nidra (4.49).
  • Muktibodhananda: these claims describe the experience of supraconsciousness (samadhi), not everyday health. “Knowing” khechari means knowing by experience.
Understand it · the Gheranda Samhita version classGS notes
  • Khechari: the tongue tip reaches between the eyebrows; the upturned tongue enters the cavity above the palate; gaze fixed at the eyebrow centre. The notes describe no cutting.
  • Nabho mudra is listed separately: turn the tongue gently upward toward the palate, hold the breath. A modified, preliminary form of khechari; it “destroys the diseases of yogis”.
  • Benefits in the notes: no fainting, hunger, thirst or laziness; no disease, old age or death; a radiant body that fire does not burn, wind does not dry, water does not wet and a snake does not bite; samadhi; and the tastes of butter, ghee, milk, curds, buttermilk, honey, grape juice and nectar.

Source note. The tongue-cutting verses (3.33–36) are classical history, not a method for students. The teacher’s seminar notes say: show only the simple form, and do not suggest anyone stretches or cuts the tongue. Quote the verses as text, with the safe form as the practical answer. The verse “no mudra equals khechari” is numbered 1.45 in your verses PDF and 1.43 in the Muktibodhananda book; use whichever your teacher quotes.

Exam angle · “Importance of Khechari” added

Answer in five points: (1) meaning of the word (3.41), (2) the two forms (hatha and raja), (3) the benefits in 3.38–41, (4) its link with bindu, sushumna and vyoma chakra (3.37, 3.45, 4.43–45), (5) no mudra equals it (1.43) and it leads to unmani. Close with the Muktibodhananda point that the benefits describe samadhi.

AViparita karani mudra: the reversing attitude

Understand it · the idea behind the inversion HYP 3.77–82
  • The moon (bindu, the nectar) sits at the palate and drips down; the sun at the navel (manipura) burns it up, and that is said to cause ageing (3.77).
  • Turn the body so the navel is above and the palate below: the sun is above and the moon below (3.79), and the nectar is not lost. The text calls it a technique that “cheats the sun” (3.78).
  • Learn it from the guru, not from crores of scripture arguments (3.78).
verse 3.79ऊर्ध्वं नाभेरधस्तालोरूर्ध्वं भानुरधः शशी ।
करणी विपरीताख्या गुरुवाक्येन लभ्यते ॥

With the navel above and the palate below, the sun above and the moon below: this is called viparita karani, obtained through the guru’s words.

Technique (Muktibodhananda) HYP book

  • Lie in shavasana. Raise the legs and bring them a little behind the head so the back rises to about 45°, feet to the ceiling.
  • Support the lower back with both hands, elbows on the floor. Breathe normally, awareness at the throat (vishuddhi).
  • Come down: lower the back first with legs raised, then the legs slowly. Rest in shavasana.
  • Stage 2 adds ujjayi; stage 3 adds khechari with ujjayi. Empty stomach, at least three hours after food.

Benefits and cautions HYPclass

  • Raises the digestive fire, so eat well (3.80); with scanty food the fire burns the body (3.81).
  • Day one: stay inverted for a moment only; add a little each day (3.81). After six months grey hair and wrinkles are said to fade (3.82).
  • The three-hour practice in 3.82 is not advisable; Muktibodhananda warns against it.
  • Class notes: more blood to the head, better concentration and memory. Avoid with high blood pressure, vertigo, weak heart, glaucoma, cerebral thrombosis and during menstruation.

BVajroli mudra

Understand it · what the word means HYP 3.83–103class
  • Vajra = thunderbolt. Here it means the vajra nadi, the second layer inside sushumna that governs the urogenital system. Vajroli is one of three oli mudras: vajroli, sahajoli (the form for women) and amaroli.
  • Verse 3.83: even one who lives freely, without the disciplines of yoga, becomes a vessel of siddhis if he truly knows vajroli. Verse 3.88: preserving the bindu is life, releasing it is death.
  • Muktibodhananda: tantra does not suppress this energy but redirects it into ojas (vitality). The classical stages need years under a guru and are not taught in a class.
  • Class-notes version: in siddhasana (men) or siddha yoni asana (women) draw the genital muscles up as if holding back urine, hold, release, repeat five times. It strengthens the pelvic floor and is like Kegel exercises. This is a later modern adaptation.

Source note. Your Gheranda chapter 3 notes describe the GS vajroli as an inverted balance on the palms with the legs and head raised, like sirshasana without the head touching. Muktibodhananda describes it differently (balancing on the buttocks with feet behind the head) and says the GS states no genital contraction. Say that the two sources word it differently and quote the one you are following.

CShakti chalana mudra: moving the energy

Understand it · the aim HYP 3.104–130
  • Kundalini is pictured as a sleeping serpent coiled at the base of the spine, closing the door to Brahma with her mouth (3.106). Many names mean the same thing: kutilangi, kundalini, bhujangi, shakti, ishvari, arundhati (3.104).
  • It sleeps above the kanda, the root from which the nadis spring; it gives liberation to yogis and bondage to the ignorant (3.107). One who knows this is a knower of yoga.
  • 3.109–110: she sleeps between ida (Ganga) and pingala (Yamuna). Seizing her by force and moving her gives the supreme state of Vishnu.
  • It comes last because it moves the energy the other mudras prepare.

Technique (Muktibodhananda) HYP book

  • Sit in siddhasana or siddha yoni asana, eyes closed. Inhale slowly through the right nostril, hold the breath and apply moola bandha as tightly as possible.
  • Exhale, then apply jalandhara and uddiyana and practise nauli clockwise up to ten rotations. Release in order; inhale only after the head is raised.
  • The verses name vajrasana for pressing the kanda near the ankles (3.114) and add bhastrika (3.115) and uddiyana at manipura (3.116).
  • The 90 minutes in the verse (3.112) are not practical. Begin with five rounds; advanced, needs a guru.

Benefits and Gheranda comparison HYPclass

  • Regular practice frees from disease (3.119) and makes the yogi a vessel of siddhi who conquers time (3.120).
  • GS (class notes): practise in a closed room; cover the navel with a soft white cloth 9 in × 3 in; smear the body with ash; sit in siddhasana; inhale through both nostrils, join prana and apana, perform ashvini mudra until the air enters sushumna.
  • GS says practise shakti chalana before yoni mudra, because yoni mudra is not effective without it.
  • Both texts: advanced practice, learn from a guru. Avoid forceful retention with heart disease or high blood pressure (general precaution, not from the notes).
Exam angle · six mudras in both texts seminar notes

The Dasha Mudra seminar notes give this comparison: maha mudra (GS adds shambhavi), maha vedha (almost identical), khechari (HYP cuts the frenum; GS does not), viparita karani (same principle), vajroli (different practice), shakti chalana (HYP: moola bandha, nauli, kanda squeeze, bhastrika; GS: ashvini mudra, ash on the body).

Understand it · the three locks as a pipe HYP bookadded
  • Picture the trunk as a pipe with a tap at each end. Moola bandha closes the bottom so apana cannot escape downward. Jalandhara closes the top so prana cannot go up and out. Uddiyana pulls the middle in and up, so the two opposite winds are pushed together at the navel.
  • When apana and prana meet at the navel (manipura, the seat of fire) the heat rises and wakes kundalini (3.66–69). That is why the text says the three are practised together as maha bandha (3.74–76).
  • Class notes: order of doing: moola, uddiyana, jalandhara. Order of release: jalandhara, uddiyana, moola. Memory hook: the one you lock last is the first one you open (add the bottom first, take the top off first).

Know this · bandhas with other practices HYP book
  • Bandhas are done after asana and pranayama or with them, but each should be perfected on its own first.
  • Moola with jalandhara in internal retention (antar kumbhaka); with uddiyana and jalandhara in external retention (bahir kumbhaka).
  • Uddiyana is easier after an inverted asana and with the bowels empty; it must be perfected before nauli. Jalandhara is always done with uddiyana.
  • The bandhas are what make the ten work as a system: with them the three Mahas (maha mudra, bandha, vedha) become possible.

The “hormone” and “thyroid” explanations are Muktibodhananda’s modern commentary, not the verse text.

Understand it · what nadanusandhana is HYP 4.65–66class
  • Nada = sound; anusandhana = exploration or tuning in. Nadanusandhana is listening to the inner sound until the mind rests in it. It is meditation on sound.
  • Verse 4.66: there are one and a quarter crore ways to laya (dissolution) told by Adinath; the author judges nada the best. 4.65: Gorakhnath’s method, open even to the unlearned.
  • Method (4.82): close the ears with the hands, listen to the sound within, fix the mind there. Within fifteen days restlessness is overcome (4.83). Practise at night in silence (GS: midnight, when animals are quiet).
  • The sound starts coarse and becomes subtle. Keep attention on the subtler sound (4.87); do not let the mind go to anything else (4.88).

  • The sound is shakti; what remains when the sound ends is the supreme self (4.101–102). Mind and the heard sound dissolve in one (4.100).

Correlation of Manas and Prana

HYP 4.21–30 and 2.2. The reason hatha yoga works through the breath: mind and prana rise and fall together.

Understand it · mind and prana move together HYP 4.21–25class
  • Manas (thought and counter-thought) is a part of the whole mind (antah karana). Prana is the life force and the breath that carries it. The text says they are tied: restrain one and the other is restrained (4.21).
  • Chitta has two causes: vasana (deep impressions and desires) and prana. If one is destroyed the other becomes still (4.22). Where the mind is still, prana is still (4.23).
  • Verse 4.24: mind and prana are like milk and water mixed together; they act alike. Where there is pranic movement there is mind, and where there is mind there is prana. If one goes the other goes (4.25); if both are controlled, moksha.
  • Class notes (7 Oct, verse 2.2): mind affects breath, breath affects mind. On 28 Oct the line “chale vate chalam chittam” is marked as a sloka to write in the exam: when the breath moves, the mind moves; when the breath is still, the mind is still.
verse 4.21पवनो बध्यते येन मनस्तेनैव बध्यते ।
मनश्च बध्यते येन पवनस्तेन बध्यते ॥

He who has restrained the breath has restrained the mind; he who has restrained the mind has restrained the breath.

verse 4.24दुग्धाम्बुवत्संमिलितावुभौ तौ
तुल्यक्रियौ मानसमारुतौ हि ।
यतो मरुत्तत्र मनःप्रवृत्तिः
यतो मनस्तत्र मरुत्प्रवृत्तिः ॥

Mind and breath are blended like milk and water and act alike; where the breath moves, the mind is active, and where the mind is active, the breath moves.

verse 2.2चले वाते चलं चित्तं निश्चले निश्चलं भवेत् ।
योगी स्थाणुत्वमाप्नोति ततो वायुं निरोधयेत् ॥

When the breath moves, the mind moves; when it is still, the mind is still. The yogi attains steadiness, so he should restrain the breath.

Know this · the ladder from the senses to nada HYP 4.26–30
  • 4.29: mind rules the senses, prana rules the mind, laya rules prana, and laya rests on nada. So the steps run senses → mind → prana → laya → nada.
  • 4.26–27: mind and mercury are both unstable by nature; fix mercury and mind and what cannot be perfected? Mercury is the alchemy image for the shifting mind and prana.
  • 4.30: when mind and prana go into laya, an indescribable ecstasy arises; some call it moksha, some do not.
  • Practice link: kevala kumbhaka (4.31) comes before absorption of the mind; nada (4.64–102) is the method.
  • This is why pranayama comes before dharana and dhyana: breath is the easier handle, and the mind follows it.
Understand it · everyday proof added
  • Notice anger, fear or hurry: the breath becomes short and quick. Notice a calm moment or sleep: the breath is long and slow. The two change together without effort.
  • A kite and its string: wind (prana) moves the kite (mind), but the kite also pulls the string. Hold the string steady and the kite settles. That is why a slow, steady breath calms thought.
  • Do not read this as a medical claim; the HYP says it as yoga theory.

Source note. Your class notes mention the correlation as the verse 2.2 idea (mind affects breath, breath affects mind). The detailed verses are in HYP chapter 4 (4.21–30). Muktibodhananda explains manas and chitta as parts of the larger antah karana and says not to be confused by the terms.
