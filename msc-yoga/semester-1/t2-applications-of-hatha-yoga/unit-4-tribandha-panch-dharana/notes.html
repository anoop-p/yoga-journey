Memory aid · 16 as 8 + 8 added

The first eight run from the greatest (maha, nabho, mahavedha) to the strongest (khechari, vajroli, shakti chalani). The last eight are lighter and mostly simple: tadagi, manduki, shambhavi, ashvini, pashini, kaki, matangi, bhujangini.

Understand it · what mudras are for GSH handout
  • A mudra is a seal: it closes the leaks of prana so the mind steadies. The GS says mudras give sthirata (steadiness).
  • Many are also done in the HYP. In the GS list, Maha, Mahavedha, Khechari, Viparitakarani, Vajroli and Shakti chalani are the ones you already met in Unit 2; here only the GS differences and the new ones are given.

Source note. The GS handout says to practise shaktichalana before yoni mudra, because yoni mudra without it is not effective. The HYP counts ten mudras and bandhas; the GS counts 25 practices. Do not mix the two lists in an answer.

Maha mudra (III 4–5) GSH

  • Technique: press the left heel at the perineum; right leg straight; hold the right big toe with both hands; inhale; jalandhara, then moola; shambhavi; hold; exhale slowly; other side.
  • Benefits (verse): removes wrinkles and grey hair, delays ageing, cough, constipation, spleen disorders and all disease; raises digestive fire.
  • Caution: heart problems, hypertension, back or knee pain.

Mahavedha (III 17–20) GSH

  • Technique: sit in padmasana, palms beside thighs, inhale, jalandhara, lift the buttocks on the hands and gently beat the ground, with awareness at mooladhara; exhale.
  • Benefits: removes fear; delays ageing and death; wakes kundalini; core strength.
  • Same cautions as maha mudra.

Khechari GSH

  • The tongue tip reaches the eyebrow centre, entering the cavity above the palate, with gaze fixed between the eyebrows.
  • Benefits: no fainting, hunger, thirst or laziness; free of disease, old age and death; radiant body; not burnt by fire, dried by wind or wetted by water; no snake bite; samadhi soon; the yogi tastes butter, ghee, milk, curd, buttermilk, honey, grape juice and nectar.

Viparitakarani (III 30–32) GSH

  • Lie down, raise both legs vertical, support the hips with the hands, back about 45 degrees; do not jerk; concentrate on manipura and vishuddhi.
  • Benefits: delays old age and death; becomes a siddha and does not perish even at dissolution (GS); grey hair becomes black in six months (HYP); blood to the brain.
  • Caution: high blood pressure, vertigo, weak heart, glaucoma, menstruation.

Understand it · tribandha GSHadded
  • Tribandha = three locks: jalandhara (throat), uddiyana (abdomen), moola (perineum). Done together with kumbhaka they push prana into sushumna. This is why the Unit 4 heading is “Tribandha and Panch-dharana”: the three locks plus five dharanas.
  • Order for practice: jalandhara first at the end of inhalation, then uddiyana (after exhalation) or moola, release in the reverse order: moola, then jalandhara, then exhale.

Source note. Your handout says “six months” of jalandhara makes one an adept. Unit 2 used the same figure for uddiyana (HYP 3.59). Keep the GS figure for jalandhara only.

Source note. Your files (the GS handouts, the notes and the slides) do not contain a section on Shat Shatru or Atma Layatva. What follows is general background, tagged as added by me; check with your teacher or book (Swami Niranjanananda’s Gheranda Samhita) before using it in an answer.

Shat Shatru · the six enemies added
  • The six inner enemies of the sadhaka are kama (craving), krodha (anger), lobha (greed), moha (delusion), mada (pride) and matsarya (jealousy).
  • Everyday image: a driver trying to reach a place with six backseat passengers, each pulling the wheel in a different direction. Yoga practice (shatkarma, asana, mudra, pratyahara) slowly makes them quiet.
  • Link to your notes: the six badhaka tattvas of the HYP (overeating, exertion, talkativeness, rule-clinging, company, unsteadiness) are the practical obstacles; shat shatru are the inner ones.
Atma Layatva · merging into the self added
  • Layatva = dissolving. Atma layatva is the dissolving of the mind (and prana) into the Self, which is the meaning of samadhi in the GS: “detaching the mind from the body and making it one with Paramatman” (your handout).
  • The six samadhis in section 8 (dhyana, nada, rasananda, laya, bhakti, raja) are the six doors to the same state.
  • Memory: Atma = self, laya = melting, like salt in water: it is still there, but you cannot find it separately.

Memory aid · bijas and deities added

La-Va-Ra-Ya-Ha go from earth up to space; Brahma, Vishnu, Rudra, Ishvara, Sadashiva go from creator up to the all-pervading. Everyday image: five floors of a building, heavy at the bottom, light at the top.

Understand it GS notes
  • In each, imagine the element in its place with its colour, shape, letter and god, and hold prana together with chitta for two hours (the text gives two hours; practise only as much as is comfortable).
  • Each dharana gives a small gain, but the real result is steadiness (sthirata): the mind learns to stay on one thing.

Source note. Colours differ between your handouts: the notes say the earth dharana is “golden arsenic” and the handout says “golden”; many editions give earth as yellow and water as white. Sthana (place) · kuteera GS ch 4 handout

  • Not far away with no security, not in a forest with no protection, not in a capital city or crowd.
  • A well-governed place where alms come easily and there is no nuisance.
  • A kuteera (cottage): with a compound, a well or pond; neither too high nor too low; free of insects; floor smeared with cow dung; secluded.

Kala (time) GS ch 4 handout

  • Do not begin in hemanta (early winter), shishira (winter), grishma (summer) or varsha (monsoon): it causes sickness.
  • Best: vasanta (spring) and sharad (autumn): the yogi becomes free of disease and successful.
  • Daily timing is in your HYP notes: 4 a.m. brahmamuhurta and evening.
Mitahara (moderate diet) GS ch 4 handout
  • Fresh, pure, pleasant, a little oily food. Fill half the stomach with food, a quarter with water, and leave a quarter empty for air. Eat with devotion (verses 21–22).
  • Without mitahara, pranayama brings many diseases and no progress.
  • In the beginning take milk and ghee, and two meals a day: noon and evening (verse 32).
Pathya (good)Apathya (avoid)
Red rice, barley and wheat flour, green gram, horse gram, Bengal gram (clean, husk free); snake gourd, yam, plantain in all forms, brinjal, leafy vegetables (five kinds), fresh butter, ghee, milk, sugar cane, jaggery, coconut, pomegranate, grapes, cardamom, dates, nuts, cloves, rose apple, haradBitter, sour, salty and pungent foods; dry food, curd and buttermilk; heavy vegetables; liquor; palm nuts; jackfruit; kulatha, masoora, ash gourd, vegetable stems, gourds, berries, bilva, garlic, hing and similar; hard, polluted, putrid, stale, very hot or very cold food
Know this · nishiddha (forbidden) habits GS ch 4 handout
  • Avoid long travel, the company of bad people, sitting by a fire, early-morning bath and fasting, and anything that causes fatigue.
  • Avoid eating only once a day, not eating at all, or eating again within three hours (verse 31).
  • Food should be easy to digest, agreeable, soft, and nourish the seven dhatus.

Source note. The handout lists items such as “dragon fruit” and “a fragrant spicy chilli” as translations of Sanskrit names; these are uncertain identifications (the verse names are dadima, karkotaki, rambha, dumbari etc.). Learn the categories, not each fruit. The list of pathya foods in the HYP (Unit 1) is different from this one.

Nadishuddhi · the preparation GS ch 4 handout
  • Sit facing east or north on kusha grass, antelope skin, tiger skin or a blanket. Nadishuddhi is required before the eight kumbhakas: when the nadis are clean prana enters sushumna easily.
  • Samanu: done with bija mantras. Vayu bija yam (inhale left 16, retain 64, exhale right 32), then agni bija ram (right in, left out), then moon tham/vam/lam (left in, right out; right in, left out), in a 16:64:32 pattern.
  • Nirmanu: done through the dhauti group (vatasara, varisara, vahnisara, bahiskrita) without mantra.

Sahita in detail GS ch 4 handout

  • Sagarbha: inhale left with a (red, Brahma, rajas) 16 times; uddiyana; retain with u (dark blue, Vishnu, sattva) 64 times; exhale right with m (white, Shiva, tamas) 32 times; then the other side.
  • Nigarbha: no bija; the ratio 20:80:40 (best), 16:64:32 (middle) or 12:48:24 (low) matras. One matra = one turn of the left palm round the left knee.

Results by grade GS ch 4 handout

  • Best (uttama): levitation. Middle: trembling of the spine. Lowest: warmth.
  • Also: moving in the air, diseases destroyed, kundalini wakes, manonmani, bliss.
