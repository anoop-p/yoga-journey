02 · Chapter 1

Orthography

The system of writing a language correctly.

Orthography is the set of rules, symbols and conventions used to write a language correctly. It includes correct spelling, how each letter is written, and the order in which letters are arranged. class

Sanskrit nameMeaning
Akshara-vinyasa (shastra)Arrangement of letters
Varna-vinyasa (shastra)Arrangement of varnas
Lekhana-paddhatiMethod of writing

AAlphabet or alphabets?

  • "Alphabet" is a collective noun: the whole set of letters of one language, like a garland made of many flowers. One language has one alphabet, one varnamala.
  • A single letter (A, B, ka, kha) is a letter, not an alphabet. Saying "A is an alphabet" is wrong.
  • "Alphabets" in the plural is used only for the sets of two or more languages: the Tamil and Telugu alphabets, the Malayalam and Hindi alphabets.
  • Other collective nouns work the same way: team, furniture, equipment. A single player is not a team.

03 · Chapter 1

Akshara and Varna

Two words for "letter", with a subtle difference.

Normally you may use either word for a letter. The subtle difference is between the sound and its written form. class

AksharaVarna
MeaningThe sound itself (phoneme)The written form or shape of the sound (grapheme)
Root ideana kshara: that which does not perishvarnayati: that which describes or gives a form
QualityPermanent, unchangingThe shape given to the sound
GenderNeuter (napumsaka-linga)Masculine (pullinga)

Why the sound is "imperishable": water turned into ice or vapour has not lost its fluid nature, because water exists elsewhere. In the same way the sound "a" stays the same in whatever word it is used. The teacher stressed that examples are only a way to understand the idea. class

  • Mahabhashya of Patanjali (2nd ahnika): the akshara is imperishable; it is also called varna. So either word can be used.
  • Medini Kosha (a Sanskrit dictionary) defines the letters from "a" up to "ksha" as 51, called varnas.
  • The three genders are masculine, feminine and neuter. Patanjali says gender is learned by usage, not taught by rule (lingam ashishyam lokashrayatvat).
  • If "akara" is used in the masculine it is a name of Vishnu (Vishnu Sahasranama), meaning all-pervading.
  • The grammarians also call sound Shabda Brahma: air passing through eight places in the mouth takes the form of sound and then returns to its source.

04 · Chapter 1

Devanagari and its features

The script used to write the Sanskrit varnamala.

A script (lipi) is the visual system used to write a language: the characters and the rules for writing them. Sanskrit and Hindi share one script, Devanagari. The word "Rama" can be written in Roman, Malayalam or Devanagari script and sounds the same. class

Meaning of Devanagari. Deva (divine beings) + nagara (city). A nagara is a city with large buildings and facilities like mountains. Devanagari is "that which came from the city of the gods". It is feminine because bhasha and lipi are feminine words. The title shows the script's beauty and structure. class

ACharacteristics of the script

FeatureDetail
Number of letters48 primary letters: 15 vowels + 33 consonants
DirectionWritten left to right, in curved shapes without sharp edges
HeadlineLetters hang from a horizontal top line, the shirorekha
Inherent vowelEach consonant carries a short "a" (ka, ca). Other vowels are added as marks (ku, kru)
PhoneticWords are written exactly as spoken. No silent letters (English: learn, know, sign, guitar)
Akshara systemVowels are written separately; each consonant letter carries a vowel
SystematicLetters are arranged in a grid by the place in the mouth where the sound is made
SandhiSounds join when words come together

Eight places of articulation (varnotpatti-sthana): chest, throat, head, root of the tongue, teeth, nose, lips, palate. The teacher will discuss these again in a later class. class

BFirst look at sandhi

When two words come close, the spelling changes to match the blended sound. The teacher's examples: maha + isha = mahesha (the two vowels become "e") and ganga + udakam = gangodakam (they become "o"). class I identified these as guna sandhi examples, so confirm when sandhi is taught. added

06 · Chapter 2

Vowels (svara)

15 of them, divided by length.

Svara is the vowel (singular, masculine). Svarah is the plural. There are 15: a, ā, i, ī, u, ū, ṛ, ṝ, ḷ, e, ai, o, au, plus aṃ (anusvara) and aḥ (visarga). Another version counts only 13 vowels and treats the last two as special characters. class

Naming a letter: add "kara" after it: akara, ikara, kakara, makara. The one exception is ra, called repha, not "rakara". class

ALength of vowels (matra)

A matra is the time taken to say a sound.

TypeNameTimeNote
ShortHrasva1 matra (ekamatra)a, i, u, ṛ
LongDirgha2 matras (dvimatra)Two short vowels join to make one long: a + a = ā
ProlongedPluta3 matras (trimatra)Said when calling from a distance ("Rama-a-a!"); written by adding a "3" after the vowel

Which vowels have which lengths: a, i, u, ṛ have short, long and pluta. ḷ has only short and pluta in classical Sanskrit (its long form is Vedic only). e, ai, o, au have only long and pluta (their short forms are Vedic only). class

Why length matters: it changes the time of the word, the poetic metre (chandas) and even the meaning. The teacher gave a Vishnu Sahasranama word, pranadah (giver of life), which would mean something very different if the vowel length were wrong. class

Honest note. The contrasting word in the teacher's example is garbled in the transcript, so only the correct word is written here.

BAnusvara and visarga

NameSoundExample
Anusvara aṃNasal soundrāmam, harim, gurum
Visarga aḥA light "h", like half a harāmaḥ, hariḥ, guruḥ

Both are called ayogavaha: they do not merge into the vowels or consonants but attach to the sound before them. Each takes half a matra (ardhamatra). class

07 · Chapter 2

Consonants (vyanjana)

33 of them, in groups.

Vyanjana (singular) and vyanjanani (plural). Neuter gender. There are 33, and each takes half a matra. class

Group (varga)Letters
ka-vargaka kha ga gha ṅa
ca-vargaca cha ja jha ña
ṭa-vargaṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa
ta-vargata tha da dha na
pa-vargapa pha ba bha ma
The last 8ya ra la va, śa ṣa sa ha
  • Varga means series. To refer to a series, take its first letter and add "varga": ka-varga, ca-varga. class
  • The last letter of every varga is a nasal sound (ṅa, ña, ṇa, na, ma). class
  • Writing tip: tha and dha in the ta-varga, and bha in the pa-varga, do not have the full headline. It runs only along the stem. Examples: ratham, dhanam, abhayam. class

08 · Chapter 2

Purnakshara, ardhakshara, samyuktakshara

The three forms of a letter.

TermMeaningExamples
Purnakshara (full letter)A consonant combined with a vowel. Also called gunitakshara because it includes a vowel. The inherent "a" countska, ki, ku, ca, ti, pa
Ardhakshara (half letter)A pure consonant with the vowel removed. Shown with a small slanting stroke below, the halanta. Takes half a matrak (क्), c, t
Samyuktakshara (conjunct)Two or more consonants joined with no vowel between themkka, mma (amma), kṣa, tra

How a samyuktakshara is formed: remove the vowel from the first consonant (making it an ardhakshara) and add another consonant. Example: k + ka = kka. In the word amma: a + m + ma, and the double ma is the samyuktakshara. class

Word endings: a word ending in a vowel is ajanta; a word ending in a consonant is halanta. These come back in Unit 1 under subantas. general

ASummary from the end of Chapter 2

  • Vowel = svara. Short 1 matra, long 2, pluta 3.
  • Anusvara and visarga = ayogavaha, half matra.
  • Consonant = vyanjana, half matra.
  • The last eight consonants = mrduvyanjana (soft).
  • Consonant with vowel = purnakshara (gunitakshara).
  • Consonant without vowel = ardhakshara.
  • Joined consonants = samyuktakshara.
09 · Chapter 3

Roman transliteration

Changing the script, not the language.

TranslationTransliteration
What changesThe languageThe script
MeaningStays the sameStays the same
Examplemother = amma (Malayalam)amma written in Roman letters instead of Malayalam letters

It is what we do when we type our own language in English letters on a phone. class

The problem. Sanskrit has 48 letters, the Roman script has 26. The solution: small marks added above or below Roman letters, called diacritical marks. So Roman transliteration means using diacritical marks on Roman letters to give the exact sound of the Devanagari letter. Example: Malayalam, Keralam: a line over the long a and a dot under the retroflex l. class

AThe 5 marks and where they are used

MarkWhereLetters
Acute accent (slanting line above)Sibilantś
Over dotNasal of the ka-vargaṅ (rarely also the anusvara)
Under dotVowels and retroflex lettersṛ ḷ ṭ ṭh ḍ ḍh ṇ ṣ and ṃ ḥ
Macron (line above)Long vowelsā ī ū ṝ
Tilde (curved line above n)Nasal of the ca-vargañ

Only 17 places need a mark: the long vowels, ṛ ṝ ḷ, aṃ aḥ, ṅ ñ, the five retroflex letters, and ś ṣ. Do not memorise the technical names of the marks; learn how they are used. class

BRules

  • The ṭa-varga and the ta-varga differ only by the dot below. Without the dot you have the ta-varga.
  • The anusvara is written ṃ. Sometimes it is seen as ṁ (dot above); same sound.
  • At the end of a word, no mark is needed: the anusvara is written as a plain "m": rāmam.
  • In a conjunct there is no vowel between the consonants, so no "a" is written: kaṅkaṇam (ṅ + ka).
