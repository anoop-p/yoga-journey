01 · Section A

Section A: Two-mark questions (answer any 10)

Questions 1 to 13.

Q1 · 2 marksUpasarga

Is ‘vi’ an upasarga or an avyaya?

Tap to reveal answer
‘vi’ (वि) is an upasarga (prefix).

There are 22 upasargas: pra, parā, apa, sam, anu, ava, nis, nir, dus, dur, vi, ā, ni, adhi, api, ati, su, ut, abhi, prati, pari, upa. Because an upasarga never changes its form, it also belongs to the wider class of avyayas, but the specific name is upasarga. Example: vi + jñāna = vijñāna (special knowledge).

general

Q2 · 2 marksSubanta

What is the meaning of ‘Ajanta word’?

Tap to reveal answer
A word that ends in a vowel (ac).

Ac means vowel and anta means ending. Examples: rāma (राम), hari (हरि), guru (गुरु), latā (लता). A word ending in a consonant is halanta: marut, vāc.

general

Q3 · 2 marksNipata

What is the meaning of ‘api’? Write an example.

Tap to reveal answer
api (अपि) means also, even, too.

Example: saḥ api gacchati (सः अपि गच्छति), “he also goes”. In the Yoga Sutra: tasya api becomes tasyāpi (“of that also”).

general

Q4 · 2 marksUpasarga

Write two upasargas and their use.

Tap to reveal answer
pra (forward) and sam (together, well).

An upasarga is put before a verb or noun and changes its meaning. pra + āṇa = prāṇa (life breath); sam + ādhi = samādhi (absorption). Others: vi (apart, special), ud (up), ni (down).

general

Q5 · 2 marksGuna sandhi

Write an example of Guna sandhi from the Yoga Sutra.

Tap to reveal answer
svādhyāya + īśvara = svādhyāyeśvara (2.1).

The Yoga Sutra 2.1 reads tapaḥ-svādhyāyeśvara-praṇidhānāni kriyā-yogaḥ. Here a (of svādhyāya) + ī (of īśvara) = e. Rule: a or ā followed by i, u, ṛ becomes e, o, ar (ād guṇaḥ, 6.1.87).

general

Q6 · 2 marksScript

Write two samyuktaksharas in Roman transliteration.

Tap to reveal answer
kṣ (क्ष) as in kṣetra, and jñ (ज्ञ) as in jñāna.

Other conjuncts: tr (त्र) in mitra, śv (श्व) in śvāsa, sth (स्थ) in sthira. A samyuktakshara is two or more consonants joined with no vowel between them.

Class example: kka and mma (in amma). class general

Q7 · 2 marksVibhakti

How many vibhaktis are in Sanskrit? Note them.

Tap to reveal answer
Seven (eight if the vocative is counted).

Prathamā (nominative), dvitīyā (accusative), tṛtīyā (instrumental), caturthī (dative), pañcamī (ablative), ṣaṣṭhī (genitive), saptamī (locative), and sambodhana (vocative, counted as the eighth). Forms of rāma: rāmaḥ, rāmam, rāmeṇa, rāmāya, rāmāt, rāmasya, rāme, he rāma.

general

Q8 · 2 marksSandhi

What is the meaning of ‘vicchedaḥ’?

Tap to reveal answer
Separation: splitting a joined (sandhi) word into its parts.

Example: yogānuśāsanam = yoga + anuśāsanam. Sandhi joins; viccheda splits.

general

Q9 · 2 marksNipata

Write two examples for nipatas.

Tap to reveal answer
ca (and) and na (not).

A nipata is an indeclinable particle. More: vā (or), eva (only), hi (indeed), tu (but), iti (thus), api (also). Examples: rāmaḥ ca kṛṣṇaḥ ca (Rama and Krishna); saḥ na gacchati (he does not go).

general

Q10 · 2 marksKaraka

Which vibhakti is used for the karana karaka?

Tap to reveal answer
Tritiya (the third, instrumental).

Rule: kartṛ-karaṇayos tṛtīyā (Pāṇini 2.3.18). Example: bālaḥ hastena likhati (the boy writes with the hand).

general

Q11 · 2 marksSloka

What is anvaya in a sloka?

Tap to reveal answer
Arranging the words of a verse in their normal prose order.

Verse order is set by the metre. Anvaya puts them as karta, karma, kriya, so the meaning can be read. See Q20 for an example.

general

Q12 · 2 marksAvyaya

Give an example for ‘anyatra’.

Tap to reveal answer
anyatra (अन्यत्र) means elsewhere, in another place.

Example: saḥ anyatra gacchati (सः अन्यत्र गच्छति), “he goes elsewhere”. It is an avyaya: it never changes.

general

Q13 · 2 marksAvyaya

Note the meaning of ‘tada’.

Tap to reveal answer
tadā (तदा) means then, at that time.

Example from the Yoga Sutra 1.3: tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe ‘vasthānam, “then the seer abides in his own nature”. Its pair is yadā (when).

general

Q14 · 5 marksAvyaya

What is meant by avyaya? Give 5 examples.

Tap to reveal answer

An avyaya is an indeclinable word: it has the same form in every gender, number and case. The old verse says it does not change (vyeti) in any of the three genders, any case or any number.

AvyayaMeaningExample
ca (च)andrāmaḥ ca lakṣmaṇaḥ ca

Avyayas include upasargas, nipatas, adverbs (atra, tatra, sadā) and conjunctions.

Q16 · 5 marksVarna

Note the classification of Varnas.

Tap to reveal answer

Varna means a letter. The Devanagari varnamala has 48 primary letters: 15 vowels and 33 consonants. class

GroupLetters
Vowels (svara), 15a ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ e ai o au, plus aṃ (anusvara) and aḥ (visarga). By length: hrasva (short, 1 matra), dīrgha (long, 2 matras), pluta (prolonged, 3 matras). Aṃ and aḥ are ayogavāha, half a matra.
Consonants (vyañjana), 33Five vargas (25, sparśa): ka-varga (k kh g gh ṅ), ca-varga (c ch j jh ñ), ṭa-varga (ṭ ṭh ḍ ḍh ṇ), ta-varga (t th d dh n), pa-varga (p ph b bh m). Antastha: y r l v. Ūṣman: ś ṣ s h. Each consonant takes half a matra.
Mṛduvyañjana (soft consonants)The last 8: y r l v ś ṣ s h. Made with light, open air flow. A beginner-level term.

By form: pūrṇākṣara is a consonant with a vowel (ka, ki, ku; also called guṇitākṣara). Ardhākṣara is a pure consonant without a vowel, shown with a halanta (k). Saṃyuktākṣara is two or more consonants joined with no vowel between them (kka, kṣa, tra). class

By place of production: kaṇṭhya (throat), tālavya (palate), mūrdhanya (cerebral), dantya (teeth), oṣṭhya (lips). general

Marks: vowels (1), consonants (1), mṛduvyañjana (1), pūrṇākṣara, ardhākṣara, saṃyuktākṣara (2). Use your teacher's terms.

Q17 · 5 marksKaraka

Discuss the karaka and its usage in Sanskrit.

Tap to reveal answer

A karaka is the relation of a noun to the verb (kriyānvayi kārakam). It tells who does what, to whom, with what, from where and where. There are six karakas:

KarakaVibhaktiMeaningExample
kartāprathamādoerbālaḥ paṭhati (the boy reads)
karmadvitīyāobjectbālaḥ pustakaṃ paṭhati
karaṇatṛtīyāinstrumenthastena likhati
sampradānacaturthīreceiverbālāya phalaṃ dadāti
apādānapañcamīpoint of separationvṛkṣāt patati
adhikaraṇasaptamīplace or timegṛhe tiṣṭhati

Sambandha (genitive: “of”) and sambodhana (address) are not karakas: they do not connect to the verb directly. Usage: if you know the karaka, you know the case ending, so the word order can change without changing the meaning.

Marks: meaning (1), six karakas (4). general

Q18 · 5 marksSubanta

Write a note on subantas.

Tap to reveal answer

A subanta is a word that ends in a sup case ending: a noun, pronoun or adjective that has taken its vibhakti and vacana (suptiṅantaṃ padam). There are 21 sup endings (seven cases × three numbers). A word that ends in tiṅ is a verb (tinanta).

Subantas are grouped by gender (masculine, feminine, neuter) and by final letter (ajanta or halanta). Example, rāma (masculine, ends in a):

Q19 · 5 marksSandhi

Describe the Savarnadirgha sandhi.

Tap to reveal answer

Rule: akaḥ savarṇe dīrghaḥ (Pāṇini 6.1.101). When a simple vowel (a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ, short or long) is followed by a similar vowel, both become one long vowel.

CombinationResultExample
a/ā + a/āāyoga + anuśāsanam = yogānuśāsanam
i/ī + i/īīkavi + indra = kavīndra
u/ū + u/ūūguru + upadeśa = gurūpadeśa
ṛ + ṛṝpitṛ + ṛṇam = pitṝṇam

From the Yoga Sutra: tasya + api = tasyāpi (1.51) and yoga + anuśāsanam = yogānuśāsanam (1.1).

Marks: rule (1), table (3), example (1). general

Q20 · 10 marksHatha Pradipika sloka

Take a sloka from the Hatha Pradipika and explain the anvaya, pratipadartha, bhava, etc.

Tap to reveal answer

Verse (HYP 1.1):

श्रीआदिनाथाय नमोऽस्तु तस्मै येनोपदिष्टा हठयोगविद्या ।
विराजते प्रोन्नतराजयोगमारोढुमिच्छोरधिरोहिणीव ॥

śrī-ādināthāya namo ‘stu tasmai yenopadiṣṭā haṭha-yoga-vidyā | virājate pronnata-rājayogam āroḍhum icchor adhirohiṇīva ||

AAnvaya (prose order)

yena haṭha-yoga-vidyā upadiṣṭā, tasmai śrī-ādināthāya namaḥ astu. (sā) pronnata-rājayogam āroḍhum icchoḥ adhirohiṇī iva virājate.

BPratipadartha (word meanings)

WordMeaning
śrī-ādināthāyato the revered Adinatha (Lord Shiva)
namaḥ astulet there be salutation
tasmaito him
yenaby whom
upadiṣṭāwas taught
haṭha-yoga-vidyāthe knowledge of hatha yoga
virājateshines
pronnata-rājayogamthe very high raja yoga
āroḍhum icchoḥof one who wishes to climb
adhirohiṇī ivalike a staircase

CTranslation and bhava (meaning)

Translation: Salutation to Lord Adinatha, who taught hatha yoga, which shines like a staircase for one who wishes to climb to the high peak of raja yoga.

Bhava: The author begins with a salutation to the first teacher, Shiva, and states the aim of the book: hatha yoga is not an end but a ladder to raja yoga (samadhi). Beginners need a ladder to reach a high place; so hatha yoga prepares body and breath for the stilled mind.

Other points: Metre: Upajati (Indravajra and Upendravajra mix). Figure of speech: upama (simile, “iva”). Sandhi: namo ‘stu = namaḥ + astu; yenopadiṣṭā = yena + upadiṣṭā.

Marks: verse (1), anvaya (2), pratipadartha (3), translation (2), bhava (2). general

Q21 · 10 marksSandhi

Discuss the use and variations of Sandhis.

Tap to reveal answer

Introduction. Sandhi (joining) is the change of sounds when two words or parts meet. The rule is saṃhitaikapade nityā nityā dhātūpasargayoḥ, nityā samāse vākye tu sā vivakṣām apekṣate: it is compulsory inside a word and in compounds, and optional between words in a sentence. It makes speech smooth and is the basis of the verse metre. Splitting it back is called viccheda.

AThree kinds

KindMeetsTypes and example
Svara (ac) sandhivowel + vowelSavarna-dirgha (yoga + anuśāsanam = yogānuśāsanam); Guna (svādhyāya + īśvara = svādhyāyeśvara); Vriddhi (sadā + eva = sadaiva); Yan (iti + ādi = ityādi); Ayadi (ne + anam = nayanam); Purvarupa (so + api = so ‘pi); Pararupa; Prakritibhava
Vyanjana (hal) sandhiconsonant + consonant or vowelJastva (vāk + īśaḥ = vāgīśaḥ); Anusvara (sam + yoga = saṃyoga); Parasavarna; Schutva (tat + ca = tacca); Stutva
Visarga sandhivisarga + any soundUtva (saḥ + api = so ‘pi); Ru-sandhi (punaḥ + api = punarapi); Visarga becomes s, ś or ṣ; lopa (saḥ + eṣaḥ = sa eṣaḥ)

BUse in yoga study

Yoga Sutra and Hatha Pradipika verses are full of sandhi: yogānuśāsanam, tasyāpi, svarūpe ‘vasthānam. Without splitting them, one cannot give the anvaya and pratipadartha.

Conclusion. Sandhi is three families with a few rules each; learn the rule, an example and the split.

Marks: intro (2), three kinds with examples (6), use and conclusion (2). general
